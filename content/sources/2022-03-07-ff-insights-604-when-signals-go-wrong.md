## Core Argument
Institutions miss critical signals hidden in plain sight, leading to preventable crises and policy failures.

## Key Points
- Silence itself can be a signal—absence of information often carries as much meaning as presence
- Noise obscures patterns: breaking 97% of intelligence didn't prevent Pearl Harbor because key signals were misinterpreted
- Policy creates perverse outcomes when institutions ignore ground signals: UP's teacher hiring vs B.Ed admissions mismatch

## Open Question
How can institutions systematically distinguish between genuine signals and noise when even perfect information access (97% of intercepted codes) fails to prevent catastrophe?

## India Angle
Indian policymakers face similar signal-interpretation failures in governance—from education policy creating unemployed B.Ed holders to ineffective targeting of subsidies. UP's education-employment mismatch mirrors systemic blindspots in state capacity to read ground realities.
