## Core Argument
Automation displaces jobs but creates new ones; success requires intentional reskilling strategies and collaborative problem-solving.

## Key Points
- AVs will eliminate some roles while creating new mid-range jobs like site supervisors and autonomy technicians requiring minimal formal education
- Technological transition is more complex than simple AI replacement; human oversight, maintenance, and calibration remain essential
- Adversarial collaboration—working constructively with disagreeable partners—produces better outcomes than defensive debate on contentious issues

## Open Question
How do we bridge the temporal and skill mismatch between workers displaced from driving roles and new job creation in AV maintenance and oversight?

## India Angle
India's large workforce in transportation, manufacturing, and service sectors faces automation disruption; reskilling policies and job creation strategies must accompany tech adoption. Adversarial collaboration model could improve policy dialogue between tech advocates and labor representatives.
