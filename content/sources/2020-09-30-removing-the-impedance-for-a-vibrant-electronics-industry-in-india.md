## Core Argument
Import substitution policies without manufacturing incentives crippled India's electronics industry; structural barriers prevent competitive domestic scale-up.

## Key Points
- Import duties and license-raj in 1970s-80s suppressed innovation while global competitors like Samsung scaled manufacturing and consumer products.
- Indian IT giants (HCL, Wipro) abandoned electronics for services exports, showing domestic electronics was uncompetitive despite policy protection.
- Auto industry's 50% import ratio vs electronics' 180% ratio reveals policy alone insufficient—execution, manufacturing culture, and entrepreneurial capability matter.

## Open Question
If protectionist policies failed and liberalisation came in 1991, why hasn't India's electronics industry caught up like autos did—what structural or capability barriers remain unaddressed?

## India Angle
India's electronics industry remains import-dependent ($86B imports vs $47B domestic production) despite 70 years of policy intent. Unlike autos, structural impediments prevent scale-up, requiring systemic policy redesign for domestic manufacturing capability and strategic autonomy.
