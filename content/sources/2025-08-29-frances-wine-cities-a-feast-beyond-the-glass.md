## Core Argument
European wine cities build identity through gastronomy, not just tourism; Dijon exemplifies how cities can anchor economic strategy around cultural heritage.

## Key Points
- Dijon positions itself as gastronomic capital by subsidizing non-food attractions, directing visitor spending to restaurants and wineries.
- French dining culture treats meals as rituals requiring time and multi-course structure, contrasting with global speed-eating trends.
- Wine regions like Burgundy and Beaujolais offer authentic experiences beyond Paris, attracting diverse global tourists through cultural specificity.

## Open Question
How do wine cities balance preserving authentic dining rituals and local identity with the pressures of mass tourism and globalization?

## India Angle
Indian tourists typically visit Paris and southern France; this article highlights lesser-known wine regions as alternatives, offering a different travel narrative for Indian travelers seeking authentic culinary experiences beyond mainstream destinations.
