## Core Argument
Disruption is a specific three-phase process, not mere change; entrepreneurs must understand before executing.

## Key Points
- Disruption has three distinct phases: entry into new/underserved market, displacement of incumbents, market expansion and dominance.
- Most startups conflate innovation with disruption—launching a novel coffee shop isn't disruption, it's a new business model.
- Incumbents respond defensively to disruption; entrepreneurs exploit the waves by understanding the underlying process.

## Open Question
If disruption takes many years to play out, how do entrepreneurs know whether their venture is genuinely disruptive or simply ahead of its failure point?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs often misuse 'disruption' as marketing jargon; understanding Broughton's three-phase framework helps founders distinguish genuine disruption from incremental innovation, critical for India's startup ecosystem where many claim to disrupt but merely iterate.
