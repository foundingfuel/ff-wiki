## Core Argument
Training must shift from entitlement rituals to problem-solving accountability linked to clear business outcomes.

## Key Points
- Corporate training in India is coercive compliance, not voluntary learning, reflecting deeper cultural dysfunction around ownership.
- Asian Paints replaced annual calendars and generic programs with monthly problem-solving teams tied to specific business outcomes.
- Learning becomes effective when accountability stays with business managers, not training departments, and skills are self-sourced from internet resources.

## Open Question
If the internet enables self-directed skill acquisition, what is the sustainable business model for training vendors and corporate L&D departments that have built their entire revenue structure on mandatory programs?

## India Angle
Indian corporations use coercive methods (denying promotions, docking pay) to enforce training attendance, revealing a broken learning culture where training is compliance theater rather than capability-building—a distinctly Indian Inc. problem.
