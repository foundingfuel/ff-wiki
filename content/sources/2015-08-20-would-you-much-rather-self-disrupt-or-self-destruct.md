## Core Argument
Established players self-disrupt through technology adoption or self-destruct through inertia; disruption alone won't kill them.

## Key Points
- Core banking functions (intermediation, capital deployment, settlement) remain unchanged for 500 years; structure and process evolve.
- Technology commoditizes quickly; revenue/profit pool expands with innovation—banks have absorbed every disruptor from credit cards to mobile payments.
- Disruptors face margin pressure on thin fee-based models; banks' profitability anchors in net interest income and investments, not payments alone.

## Open Question
If technology always commoditizes and revenue pools expand, why do some established industries fail to adapt—is it truly about courage and foresight, or are there deeper structural constraints the article underestimates?

## India Angle
Indian banks face pressure from fintech and digital payment startups; the article's argument that established players can out-compete disruptors through technology adoption and strategic courage is directly relevant to India's financial sector transformation and the RBI's regulatory sandbox initiatives.
