## Core Argument
Humans evolved for hunter-gatherer risks; modern decisions about money, relationships, careers trigger predictable errors.

## Key Points
- Our brains evolved to handle physical threats, not complex long-term decisions like mortgages or healthcare planning.
- Basic decision-making errors (stress, too many options, defaults) are universal; culture shapes how we prioritize specific domains.
- Understanding what motivates employees beyond salary—recognition, meaning, autonomy—enables better business outcomes.

## Open Question
If cultural values shape decision-making priorities differently, how do global organizations apply universal behavioural insights without flattening local context?

## India Angle
Indian businesses and leaders operate in fast-paced, high-stress environments with limited resources—understanding cognitive biases becomes critical for better hiring decisions, employee motivation, and organizational culture. Also relevant for Indian policymakers designing behavioural interventions in financial inclusion and healthcare.
