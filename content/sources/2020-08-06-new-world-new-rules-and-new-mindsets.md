## Core Argument
Business success now requires abandoning binary thinking for probabilistic decision-making and continuous experimentation over rigid planning.

## Key Points
- Younger generation thinks in probabilities and distributed options, not binary choices; old leaders cling to success-validated worldviews.
- Digital infrastructure enables billion-dollar companies in days through collaboration, not ownership; cost of failure approaches zero.
- MBA education must teach process questioning and outcome-driven thinking, not process compliance and scale mechanics.

## Open Question
If organizational processes naturally narrow bandwidth as companies scale, how can large Indian enterprises unlearn these mechanisms without dismantling operational stability?

## India Angle
Indian business leaders and MBA graduates must unlearn industrial-era thinking to compete with younger founders leveraging digital collaboration and zero-cost experimentation. India's tech talent can either remain bound by legacy process-driven cultures or become innovation leaders by embracing abundance mindset.
