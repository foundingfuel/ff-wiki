## Core Argument
India's strategic autonomy in a US-China-Russia-EU multipolar order requires managing great power differences without ceding policy veto to any single nation.

## Key Points
- US leverages tech dominance and administrative tools to contain China, but lacks comprehensive Asian strategy or commitment to trade as foreign policy.
- EU recognizes China as systemic competitor and Russia threat, but has limited leverage in Asia and weak power projection capabilities.
- India quietly rebalances away from Russia toward strategic autonomy, reducing Russian arms imports share while maintaining diplomatic hedging across all powers.

## Open Question
How can India maintain genuine strategic autonomy and avoid great power coercion when all major powers—US, China, Russia, EU—are actively constraining each other's regional influence?

## India Angle
India navigates a multipolar world where it cannot afford exclusive alignment with any major power. Its strategic autonomy—managing relationships with Russia, US, and China simultaneously while securing energy and defense needs—defines its geopolitical positioning and economic leverage.
