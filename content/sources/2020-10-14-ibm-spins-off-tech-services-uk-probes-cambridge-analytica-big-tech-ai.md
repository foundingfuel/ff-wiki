## Core Argument
Strategic excellence requires 'not-to-do' lists; Facebook's real problem is amplification, not privacy.

## Key Points
- IBM's spinoff exemplifies strategic focus through subtraction, not addition—a model other conglomerates must consider.
- Cambridge Analytica scandal revealed ad-tech ineffectiveness but exposed Facebook's core danger: amplifying fringe ideologies at scale.
- Big Tech monopolies resist breakup because services are entangled; targeted antitrust approaches matter more than wholesale regulation.

## Open Question
If Cambridge Analytica's ads were ineffective, why does Facebook's amplification mechanism remain dangerous—and how do regulators target the real problem without crushing legitimate expression?

## India Angle
IBM's India operations (25-33% of workforce) will be affected by the spinoff; India's tech majors (TCS, Infosys) now compete with IBM directly, signaling shifts in India's IT services landscape and regulatory attention needed as social media's amplification mechanisms spread misinformation in Indian elections.
