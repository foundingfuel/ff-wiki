## Core Argument
Social media platforms engineered for engagement harm teenage mental health; regulation must distinguish between communication tech and addictive platforms.

## Key Points
- Facebook knowingly allowed Instagram to harm teenage girls; platforms optimize for engagement over user welfare.
- Distinction exists between direct communication tools (Snapchat, FaceTime) and validation-seeking feeds (Instagram, TikTok) targeting children.
- Small groups of influencers (12 individuals drove 65% of anti-vax content) amplify medical misinformation through wellness narratives.

## Open Question
If platforms acknowledge internal research on harms, why do they continue business models that create those harms—and what regulatory intervention actually changes incentives?

## India Angle
India has rapidly increasing teenage social media adoption with minimal regulatory oversight; platform toxicity, mental health impacts, and misinformation spread (as seen with anti-vax movements) pose unique risks in a high-population, low-digital-literacy context where fact-checking infrastructure is weak.
