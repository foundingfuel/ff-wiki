## Core Argument
Big ideas emerge from many small insights across diverse disciplines, requiring both technical and humanistic understanding.

## Key Points
- Innovation is cumulative: many small insights from diverse fields combine to create breakthroughs, not single eureka moments.
- Technology alone is insufficient; solving human problems requires understanding psychology, society, and humanities alongside STEM.
- As automation increases, uniquely human skills—curiosity, empathy, critical thinking—become competitive advantages over machines.

## Open Question
If human skills become our only competitive edge against machines, how do we scale education systems to teach these interdisciplinary capabilities in resource-constrained contexts like India?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs and leaders need liberal arts education alongside STEM to solve local human problems—critical as India scales tech solutions for development, governance, and social impact across diverse populations.
