## Core Argument
Activists struggle reconciling environmental goals with policy trade-offs when supporting farmer protests against modernization laws.

## Key Points
- Indian agriculture generates 140Mt CO2 annually from crop burning alone, affecting 50 million people's air quality.
- Current system wastes 67Mt of food yearly while extracting groundwater unsustainably; 65% will be over-exploited by 2030.
- Activism rejects compromise; policymaking requires trade-offs—environmental activists face this dilemma supporting farm law protests.

## Open Question
How can India's legitimate environmental imperatives (reducing emissions, water depletion, food waste) be achieved without harming small farmers whose livelihoods depend on current systems?

## India Angle
India's farm laws represent a critical tension between environmental necessity (reducing emissions, water depletion, food waste) and farmer welfare concerns. The dilemma reflects how India must modernize agriculture for sustainability while managing political and social pressures.
