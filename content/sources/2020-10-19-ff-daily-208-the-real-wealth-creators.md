## Core Argument
Industrial entrepreneurs building productive capacity create real national wealth; trade entrepreneurs primarily enrich themselves.

## Key Points
- TELCO built world-class R&D and beat Japanese competitors before 1991 liberalisation, contradicting 'dark ages' narrative
- Two classes of entrepreneurs exist: trade-focused (extractive) versus industrial-focused (capability and wealth-building)
- Community-embedded systems—women teachers, local ownership—outperform top-down institutional models in education and healthcare

## Open Question
If pre-1991 TELCO thrived on R&D and exports without liberalisation, why does current policy continue to reward financial engineering over manufacturing capability?

## India Angle
Indian policymakers conflate trade entrepreneurs with industrial wealth-creators; pre-1991 TELCO's R&D success challenges the 'reform narrative' and questions whether current policy prioritizes rent-seeking over productive capability-building
