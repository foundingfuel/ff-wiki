## Core Argument
Regulatory crackdowns on tech giants reflect governments' shift toward controlling financial systems as national security.

## Key Points
- Ant Group's dominance—controlling 40% of lending fees while bearing minimal risk—triggered regulatory alarm over systemic financial exposure.
- Xi's framing of financial stability as national security justified state intervention, signaling no sector is too big for private control.
- Ma's 'pawnshop mentality' critique was a trigger, not the cause; the real issue was unchecked market power and data-driven financial engineering.

## Open Question
How can governments balance innovation incentives with financial stability concerns when tech platforms become systemically important but operate outside traditional regulatory frameworks?

## India Angle
India's fintech and ecommerce sectors face similar regulatory uncertainty; Ma's fall illustrates how rapid private-sector dominance in financial services can trigger state intervention, relevant as India's RBI and government navigate fintech growth and platform monopolies.
