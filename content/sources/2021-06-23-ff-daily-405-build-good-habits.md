## Core Argument
Small habits reshape identity; meaningful change requires voting for new beliefs through repeated actions, not radical overhauls.

## Key Points
- Outcomes lag habits; identity shifts require accumulating evidence through consistent small actions over time.
- Each habit is a vote for a desired identity; majority, not perfection, determines lasting behavioral change.
- Trusting oneself emerges from repeated small wins that alter the narrative one tells about capabilities.

## Open Question
If habits shape identity and outcomes are lagging measures, how do individuals overcome initial momentum gaps when results aren't visible during early habit formation?

## India Angle
For Indian professionals and leaders navigating rapid change—from startups to large corporations—building habits around identity and incremental progress offers a practical framework for personal mastery and organizational transformation, critical in a high-churn talent market.
