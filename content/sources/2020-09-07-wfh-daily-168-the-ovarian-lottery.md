## Core Argument
Success depends vastly on unearned advantages; gratitude and redistribution are moral imperatives, not charity.

## Key Points
- Birth circumstances (country, family, postcode) determine 80% of life outcomes; largely uncontrollable.
- Innovation requires: resourceful innovators, incentives to risk, and diverse/tolerant societies open to new ideas.
- Credentialing, not education, is school's primary function; internet and meritocracy will disrupt traditional systems.

## Open Question
If success is mostly luck, how do we balance gratitude/humility with personal accountability and drive to improve?

## India Angle
Indian professionals face compounded 'ovarian lottery' disadvantages—birth location, family circumstances, access to quality education/networks—that amplify inequality compared to developed nations; yet also opportunities to challenge credentialing systems through meritocracy and internet-enabled self-education.
