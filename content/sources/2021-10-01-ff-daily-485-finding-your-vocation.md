## Core Argument
Vocations are grown through sustained engagement, not discovered through sudden insight or epiphany.

## Key Points
- Marie Curie's vocation crystallized gradually over decades of research, not through a moment of enlightenment.
- Most people expect vocations to arrive via external validation or self-reflection breakthrough, creating false expectations.
- Career transitions require patience and iterative exploration rather than waiting for clarity or divine calling.

## Open Question
If vocations are grown slowly through experimentation, how do people in economic distress or with limited opportunities find the time and resources to explore without external validation?

## India Angle
Indian professionals navigating post-pandemic career transitions need to understand that vocation develops through sustained practice and experimentation, not sudden epiphany—relevant for a culture valuing predetermined career paths.
