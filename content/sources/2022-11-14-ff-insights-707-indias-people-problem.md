## Core Argument
India's census data gap prevents evidence-based policy for a nation transitioning to population replacement level.

## Key Points
- India hasn't conducted formal census in over a decade despite becoming world's most populous nation.
- Regional fertility disparities require opposite policy responses: Kerala needs elder care, Bihar needs job creation.
- Migration data—hardest to estimate—is crucial but only Census captures it with reasonable accuracy for planning.

## Open Question
How can India's decentralised governance system effectively tailor policies to vastly different regional demographic needs without comprehensive, current census data?

## India Angle
India's decade-long gap in census data hampers policy-making for a demographically diverse country where regional variations (Kerala vs Bihar) demand drastically different interventions—elder care vs job creation. Inability to track migration undermines development planning.
