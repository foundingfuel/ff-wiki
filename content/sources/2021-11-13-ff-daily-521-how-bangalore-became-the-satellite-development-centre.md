## Core Argument
Strategic consolidation of talent and infrastructure, not just equipment, built India's satellite development capacity despite regional opposition.

## Key Points
- U.R. Rao moved satellite teams from Trivandrum to Bangalore after identifying superior intellectual and physical infrastructure there.
- Kerala unions resisted the transfer; Rao managed this by gradually relocating engineers individually and leaving a small team behind temporarily.
- Bangalore's ecosystem—IISc proximity, aerospace talent, informal networks—proved critical to building a world-class space programme.

## Open Question
How do you balance the need for institutional excellence and consolidation with legitimate regional claims and worker welfare when moving critical programmes?

## India Angle
How India strategically built institutional capacity for space technology by consolidating expertise in Bangalore, overcoming regional resistance and creating a hub that became foundational to ISRO's global competitiveness.
