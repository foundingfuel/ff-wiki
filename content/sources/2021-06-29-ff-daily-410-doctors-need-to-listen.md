## Core Argument
Physicians must balance efficiency with deep listening; psychological factors require time and cannot be systematized without harming care quality.

## Key Points
- Denying patient symptoms ignores physician fallibility and creates mind-body splits, missing psychological roots of physical illness.
- Treating medicine as assembly-line business erodes doctor-patient partnership and increases cognitive errors despite seeming efficiency gains.
- Complicated medical problems demand unhurried thinking; 15-20 minutes suffices for straightforward cases, but complex issues require dedicated time.

## Open Question
How can India's resource-constrained healthcare system honor the time-intensive nature of good medical thinking while serving millions with insufficient doctor-to-patient ratios?

## India Angle
India's healthcare system faces acute doctor shortage and efficiency pressure; listening-based medicine requires systemic change in how medical practice is structured and incentivized, especially for vulnerable populations.
