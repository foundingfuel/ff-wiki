## Core Argument
India lacks political will and institutional capacity to execute malaria control despite scientific knowledge and resources available.

## Key Points
- Sri Lanka achieved malaria-free status through V.A.S.T. strategy (vector control, access, surveillance, treatment) and the three D's (data, decision-making, delivery).
- India's challenges include drug resistance, poor surveillance at primary health level, limited access in tribal areas, and historical complacency after 1960s progress.
- Half of malaria deaths occur in hilly tribal regions where traditional cures persist and health education remains weak despite 40% Maharashtra tribal mortality.

## Open Question
Why does India, with superior GDP and research capability compared to Sri Lanka, remain unable to replicate a proven public health model despite having greater resources and political visibility?

## India Angle
India's failure to eradicate malaria despite 115 years of knowledge reveals systemic gaps in political commitment, surveillance infrastructure, and resource allocation—contrasting sharply with Sri Lanka's success and exposing India's state capacity crisis.
