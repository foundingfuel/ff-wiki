## Core Argument
WhatsApp has become India's primary political platform, exploiting rural-urban perception gaps and information asymmetries.

## Key Points
- WhatsApp groups enable targeted political messaging that outperforms Facebook/Twitter due to guaranteed visibility and credibility perception.
- Rural voters value WhatsApp group membership as access to insider information; urban elites view it as noise—revealing deep information behavior divides.
- WFH has increased workday length by 48.5 minutes and meeting frequency; leaders must focus on output, not hours, and acknowledge productivity variance.

## Open Question
How can organizations prevent WhatsApp from becoming a channel for misinformation and manipulation while respecting its role as a trusted communication medium for underserved populations?

## India Angle
WhatsApp's dominance in India (200M+ users) makes it a unique political and communication tool, with rural voters valuing group messages differently than urban elites—a distinctly Indian phenomenon shaping electoral outcomes and organizational communication.
