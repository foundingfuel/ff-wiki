## Core Argument
Founders fail not from invisible threats, but from wilful blindness to obvious market shifts they refuse to see.

## Key Points
- Goyal micromanaged despite hiring global experts, creating talent churn and ignoring strategic advice about competitive threats.
- Low-cost carriers disrupted full-service model post-2008; Goyal denied reality for over a decade, betting on revival.
- Brinkmanship gamble failed: Goyal miscalculated Etihad's commitment and government's election-year willingness to bail out a failing airline.

## Open Question
How can seasoned entrepreneurs with access to global expertise and political networks remain strategically blind to obvious market threats for over a decade?

## India Angle
Jet Airways' collapse reflects a critical failure in Indian entrepreneurship—how founder ego and political leverage can blind leaders to market disruption (low-cost carriers), and why reliance on government support masks structural business model failures.
