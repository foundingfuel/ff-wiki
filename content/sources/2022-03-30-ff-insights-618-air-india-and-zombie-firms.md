## Core Argument
Zombie firms drain public finances and suppress high-productivity competitors; privatisation and institutional strengthening unlock growth.

## Key Points
- Zombie firms—artificially kept alive by state support—harm public finance and market competition in weak institutional environments.
- Low-productivity firms survive by evading taxes or violating pollution laws when enforcement is weak; reallocation to high-productivity firms yields GDP growth.
- Politically-connected firms thrive through regulatory favour but collapse when sweetheart arrangements end, lacking genuine productivity.

## Open Question
How can governments distinguish between legitimate support for strategic sectors and harmful zombie-firm propping when budget constraints are soft and political incentives exist?

## India Angle
India's weak budget discipline and enforcement capabilities allow zombie firms—especially PSUs and politically-connected entities—to survive, distorting competition and suppressing GDP growth. Strengthening institutional quality and law enforcement is critical for productivity gains.
