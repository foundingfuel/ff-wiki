## Core Argument
Art's power to protest is suppressed when political pressure constrains cultural expression during collective crisis.

## Key Points
- Durga Puja persisted despite RG Kar tragedy, but artists faced implicit censorship when using art as political commentary.
- Sponsorship dependencies and committee ecosystems make pandals vulnerable to political pressure, inhibiting dissent.
- Only 5 of 150 pandals subtly addressed the tragedy; self-censorship and theme changes suggest fear, not neutrality.

## Open Question
How do communities balance cultural continuity with moral urgency when institutions that should protect them fail—and who decides what art can express?

## India Angle
The tension between artistic expression and political pressure in India, exemplified through Kolkata's Durga Puja during a moment of national trauma and collective outrage over institutional failure.
