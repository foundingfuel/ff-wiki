## Core Argument
Career purpose emerges from tension between external constraints (money, societal expectations) and internal meaning-making as life circumstances shift.

## Key Points
- Generational career choices reflect available opportunities: ancestors had none, parents sought security, author's generation faces paralyzing abundance.
- External forces (economic need, social expectation) drive initial career paths; internal purpose develops later if at all.
- Sunk cost fallacy and inertia prevent mid-career transitions despite desire for meaning and fulfillment over financial necessity.

## Open Question
How do we distinguish between genuine calling and the stories we tell ourselves about work we've already invested 20 years in?

## India Angle
Indian career trajectories shaped by generational shifts—from limited options (seafaring, farming) to abundance in post-liberalization era, creating tension between security-seeking and self-actualization.
