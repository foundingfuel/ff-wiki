## Core Argument
AI and fourth industrial revolution will transform industries exponentially, but benefits concentrate while job losses disperse widely.

## Key Points
- AI excitement cycles repeat historically (1950s-80s, now), but current investment and deployment across tech companies suggests this cycle differs.
- Industrial revolutions' impact varies: railways/electricity reshaped India structurally; third industrial revolution's productivity gains slowed post-1970.
- WEF projects 7.1 million job losses by 2020; surplus concentrates with technology controllers, raising urgent workforce transition questions.

## Open Question
If exponential technological progress creates winners and losers asymmetrically, how do societies prevent destabilization—especially in countries like India with fragile employment ecosystems?

## India Angle
Indian tech companies like Infosys must pivot from offshore models to AI/automation; Indian railways and electricity history exemplify how transformative technologies reshape society and caste structures—relevant precedent for current disruption.
