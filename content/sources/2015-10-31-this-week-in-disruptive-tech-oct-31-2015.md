## Core Argument
Genomics, IoT, wearables, and renewables are reshaping industries; incumbents face disruption but classical theory may not fully explain outcomes.

## Key Points
- Genomics and food safety applications (Clear Labs) show practical near-term demand, especially in India.
- IoT ownership will determine computing future; context has shifted so fundamentally that business models must adapt entirely.
- Disruptive innovation theory questioned by researchers—not all incumbents follow predicted trajectory of failure.

## Open Question
If Clayton Christensen's disruptive innovation theory doesn't universally hold, what actually determines whether incumbents survive technological transitions?

## India Angle
India has significant opportunities in genomics outsourcing, IoT manufacturing, wearable health tech for aging population, and renewable energy disruption—but needs robust data security frameworks and entrepreneurial ecosystems to capture these.
