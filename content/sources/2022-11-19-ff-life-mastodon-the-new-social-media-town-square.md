## Core Argument
Mastodon offers federated social media town squares as decentralized alternative to centralized platforms like Twitter.

## Key Points
- Mastodon is not a Twitter replacement but a collection of community-specific servers organized by interest.
- Decentralized federation model prioritizes civil discourse over viral trends, reducing trolling and spam.
- Technical complexity and fragmentation create friction—users must navigate multiple servers, switching processes, and plugin risks.

## Open Question
Can decentralized platforms maintain user engagement and critical mass when they prioritize moderation and community control over discoverability and network effects that centralized platforms exploit?

## India Angle
Indian journalists and media professionals are exploring alternatives to Twitter; relevant for India's growing tech-savvy population seeking less toxic digital spaces amid platform consolidation risks.
