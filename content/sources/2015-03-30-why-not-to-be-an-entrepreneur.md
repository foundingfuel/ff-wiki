## Core Argument
Entrepreneurship is valuable but not universally right; not everyone should start businesses despite cultural valorization.

## Key Points
- Entrepreneurship requires specific genetic, psychological, and circumstantial filters—not universally applicable like 'entrepreneurial mindset' traits.
- Macro-trends (freelancing, gig work) don't equal entrepreneurship; large organizations remain essential for complex production.
- Entrepreneurs aren't inherently noble or superior; they're self-interested humans like IAS officers or managers, yet media mythologizes them.

## Open Question
If entrepreneurial mindset (self-management, ambition) is universally valuable, why isn't entrepreneurship itself—when both require similar psychological traits and both are presented as solutions to India's growth challenges?

## India Angle
In India's entrepreneurship boom, there's uncritical promotion of startup culture as universal aspiration; this challenges the narrative that everyone should become an entrepreneur, especially relevant as Indian institutions and associations push entrepreneurship as a solution to employment and economic growth.
