## Core Argument
India's negligible indigenous electronics presence stems from mid-20th century policy failures, not capability gaps.

## Key Points
- Solar cooker (1950s) exemplifies state-led tech failures despite political backing and media hype.
- Private sector liberalization post-1990s (mobile, internet) outperformed government-directed technology initiatives.
- India imports three-fourths of consumer electronics, raising forex and technological sovereignty concerns.

## Open Question
If private liberalization works better than state direction, why does India still lack sovereign technology capabilities in critical areas like semiconductor manufacturing and core electronics components?

## India Angle
India imports 75% of consumer electronics demand ($300B in 2020) due to failed 1960s-era technology policies. Indigenous tech projects rarely achieve global impact; private liberalization (mobile, internet) succeeded where state-led initiatives (solar cooker) failed. Critical for India's forex security and technological sovereignty.
