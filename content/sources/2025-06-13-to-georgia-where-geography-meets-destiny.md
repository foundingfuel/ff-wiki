## Core Argument
Georgia's geography and art reveal how small nations resist erasure through cultural memory amid geopolitical pressure.

## Key Points
- Georgian artists under Soviet rule encoded dissent subtly in art, chronicling real history versus state-approved narratives.
- Geography shapes destiny: Georgia's position between Europe and Asia creates persistent geopolitical tensions and identity struggles.
- Cultural preservation through figures like poet Shota Rustaveli sustains native identity despite successive foreign influences.

## Open Question
How can a small nation maintain cultural independence and democratic aspirations when geopolitically wedged between competing superpowers with material interests in controlling it?
