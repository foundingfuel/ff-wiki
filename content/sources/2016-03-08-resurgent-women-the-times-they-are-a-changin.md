## Core Argument
Women's rising education and ambition in India will fundamentally reshape marriage, family, work and social structures within a decade.

## Key Points
- Girls outperforming boys academically; women comprise third of IT/financial services workforce, driving systemic change
- Rising female education delays marriage, lowers fertility, increases paid work participation—contradicting traditional gender roles
- Men unprepared for confident, educated women partners; expectation mismatch threatens existing socio-economic partnership models

## Open Question
If women continue outpacing men educationally and professionally, but cultural norms still expect male superiority in partnerships, how will marriage and family structures sustainably adapt across India's socio-economic strata?

## India Angle
India's 586 million women represent 17% of world's female population; their economic participation, education, and workforce integration will reshape Indian society's marriage norms, fertility rates, gender expectations, and consumer behaviour with global implications.
