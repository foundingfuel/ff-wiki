## Core Argument
LLMs are sophisticated pattern-matching systems, not intelligent agents—lacking causality, goals, and continuous learning.

## Key Points
- LLMs predict tokens through compression and mimicry, but cannot form goals or learn from real-world feedback like true intelligence.
- Turing Award winners (LeCun, Sutton, Karpathy) argue current architectures are fundamentally limited and will be obsolete within five years.
- The AI boom's scaling doctrine (The Bitter Lesson) is collapsing—diminishing returns appear, companies lose money despite premium pricing.

## Open Question
If leading AI architects believe LLMs cannot reach human-level intelligence and will soon be obsolete, why are trillion-dollar companies and governments betting transformational amounts on systems they privately doubt?

## India Angle
Indian enterprises rushing to adopt LLM-based solutions risk building on potentially ephemeral technology; policy and talent strategy should account for the possibility that current LLMs may become obsolete within 5 years, requiring different skill investments and architectural thinking.
