## Core Argument
True anti-fragility comes from aligning your life with your authentic 18-year-old self, not modern constructions of success.

## Key Points
- Social work in India increasingly corporatized through philanthropy language and five-star hotel discourse, alienating practitioners grounded in real impact.
- Anti-fragility is choosing a simpler life aligned with personal values—kitchen gardens, conversation, daily bread—over external validation markers.
- Digital disconnection enables authentic human connection and clarifies what actually constitutes a good life beyond status.

## Open Question
How do social enterprises and impact investors scale solutions to systemic problems without losing the authenticity and ground connection that makes them meaningful?

## India Angle
Critiques India's emerging philanthropic/social enterprise class for adopting corporate jargon ('impact investing', 'scaling') disconnected from ground realities; explores alternative models of meaningful work and life beyond India's urban rat race.
