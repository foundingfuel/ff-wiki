## Core Argument
China app ban could reshape India's tech ecosystem from dependency to indigenous innovation or revert to status quo.

## Key Points
- Six of ten top Indian apps are Chinese; ban signals geopolitical shift with immediate user migration to American alternatives.
- India could build copycats capturing niches, leading to national app economies instead of global monopolies across countries.
- Ideal scenario: India develops user-centric, commons-based tech model differing from US corporate or Chinese state-driven approaches.

## Open Question
Will India's ban catalyze genuine innovation in user-centric tech models, or simply entrench American platform dominance while Indian startups remain marginal players?

## India Angle
The Chinese app ban represents a pivotal moment for India to build indigenous tech alternatives and potentially evolve a unique model that prioritizes data ownership and user sovereignty—distinct from US monopoly capitalism or state-driven Chinese models.
