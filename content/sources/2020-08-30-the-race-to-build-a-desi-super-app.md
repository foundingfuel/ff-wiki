## Core Argument
Super Apps require building ecosystems, not just writing code; India's race mirrors China's, but winners will be those who organize the chaotic real-world layers.

## Key Points
- Super Apps are high-frequency consumer aggregators that become operating systems for daily life through integrated payments, logistics, and financial services.
- China's WeChat and Alipay succeeded because they had strong backbones (messaging, payments) and deep merchant integration; India's contenders (Jio, WhatsApp, Google, Paytm, Flipkart) lack this ecosystem orchestration.
- Silicon Valley players struggle because they avoid organizing messy real-world ecosystems; Amazon-like willingness to build missing infrastructure (logistics, payments) may prove decisive.

## Open Question
Can global tech companies (Google, WhatsApp) fundamentally change their DNA from code-writing to ecosystem-building to compete, or will India's Super App winners be homegrown or patient real-world operators like Amazon and Jio?

## India Angle
India's massive internet user base (second largest globally) makes it the battleground for Super App dominance. Chinese models (WeChat, Alipay) set the template, but Indian players must navigate unique ecosystem challenges—fragmented merchant networks, payment infrastructure, and regulatory complexity—while global tech giants scramble to avoid missing India like they missed China.
