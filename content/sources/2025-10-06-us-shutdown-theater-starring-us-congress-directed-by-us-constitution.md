## Core Argument
US constitutional design prioritizes checks over efficiency, creating recurring shutdowns; Westminster systems resolve budget crises through governments falling, not state paralysis.

## Key Points
- US Constitution fragments budget power across House, Senate, President with no final authority, enabling brinkmanship and shutdowns.
- India and Britain avoid shutdowns via executive-dominated budgets and party discipline; failure to pass appropriations triggers no-confidence votes.
- Since 1970s, 20+ US funding gaps and shutdowns have become normalized rituals disrupting economy and eroding public trust.

## Open Question
How can a fragmented system designed to prevent tyranny function as a modern economy requires long-term fiscal planning and certainty, especially against rivals with unified budget authority?

## India Angle
India's Westminster system with strong executive control avoids government shutdowns entirely; contrasts sharply with US constitutional gridlock, positioning India's fiscal governance as more stable and decisive for institutional confidence.
