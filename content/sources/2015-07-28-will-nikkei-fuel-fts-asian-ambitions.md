## Core Argument
Asian acquirers buying Western media at premium valuations risks repeating costly mistakes if lacking coherent global expansion strategies.

## Key Points
- Nikkei paid 35x operating income due to strategic compulsions: stagnant home market and need for global diversification.
- FT's India entry blocked by regulatory barriers, local incumbents dominance, and unresolved trademark litigation with Times of India.
- FT's successful digital subscription model must drive expansion; Nikkei's print-heavy focus creates strategic alignment uncertainty.

## Open Question
Can Nikkei leverage FT's digital-first subscription model globally when its core competency and strategy remains print-dependent, especially given Asia-Pacific's regulated media landscapes?

## India Angle
FT's repeated failed attempts to enter India's regulated media market, compounded by a two-decade trademark dispute with Times of India Group, highlight barriers foreign media brands face. Nikkei's acquisition raises questions about whether Asian ownership can succeed where Western publishers have struggled in Asia-Pacific markets.
