## Core Argument
Transparency, humour, and brand strength defeat dark PR better than retaliation or silence.

## Key Points
- Dark PR spreads rapidly in markets with prior food safety trauma; China's melamine scandal created lasting vulnerability.
- Transparency, quick response, and supplier accountability mitigate damage; secrecy amplifies rumour spread.
- Strong brands with loyal communities survive attacks; weak brands with trust deficits collapse faster.

## Open Question
How do companies distinguish between genuine food safety concerns requiring immediate action and malicious rumours requiring restraint—especially when both spread identically on social media?

## India Angle
Indian companies expanding globally—especially in Asia—face similar dark PR risks. Maggi's crisis in India mirrors international patterns; Indian brands need robust crisis communication strategies when operating in markets with high food safety concerns and active social media ecosystems.
