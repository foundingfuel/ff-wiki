## Core Argument
Indian electoral system masks true spending; enforcement gaps enable persistent bribery without deterrence.

## Key Points
- Political parties building in-house competencies to eliminate dependence on private consultancy firms competing for contracts.
- Election Commission seizures capture only ~2-3% of actual spending; Karnataka's Rs 170 crore seizure negligible against estimated Rs 5,600-10,500 crore total.
- Weak prosecution of vote-buying handlers and drivers; cases abandoned post-election create no deterrent effect.

## Open Question
If parties are moving away from external consultants to strengthen internal competencies, does this improve accountability or further entrench centralized power while obscuring true campaign costs?

## India Angle
Electoral politics in India reveals systemic gaps: massive unaccounted spending, weak enforcement of electoral violations, and consolidation of political power through in-house capabilities. Political parties' shift from external consultants to direct employment represents institutional learning but also centralizes campaign machinery, raising questions about democratic competition and state capacity.
