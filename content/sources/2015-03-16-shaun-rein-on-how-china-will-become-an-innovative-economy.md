## Core Argument
China's shift from copycat to innovator economy driven by vanishing low-cost advantages and maturing markets.

## Key Points
- Low-hanging fruit disappearing forces Chinese entrepreneurs to innovate for survival, not just profit
- Rising wages and rents eliminate China's cost advantage, requiring move up value chain
- Venture capital now sees profit in innovation, removing capital barrier that previously deterred risk-taking

## Open Question
If innovation requires market maturation and rising costs to force change, how do countries prevent the painful transition period where competitiveness drops before innovation gains traction?

## India Angle
India faces a similar middle-income trap and must shift from cost-based competition to innovation-driven growth; Chinese success in this transition offers both a playbook and competitive warning for Indian entrepreneurs and policymakers seeking global value chain positioning.
