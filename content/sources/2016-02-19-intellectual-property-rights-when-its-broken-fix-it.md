## Core Argument
Current IP regimes prioritize monopolistic capital accumulation over innovation and healthcare access, harming the poor.

## Key Points
- IP regimes have become tools for monopoly creation rather than innovation incentives, particularly in pharmaceuticals.
- Evidence shows patents don't correlate with increased medical innovation; pharma spends more on marketing and patent defence than R&D.
- India's poor face healthcare vulnerability because medicine costs are artificially inflated by IP protections and monopolies.

## Open Question
How can societies incentivize genuine innovation without allowing IP regimes to create monopolies that exclude the poor from life-saving medicines?

## India Angle
India's healthcare affordability crisis and access to generic medicines are directly threatened by global IP regimes. Indian generic drug producers face pressure from foreign pharma and IP enforcement, while the Indian government struggles to balance healthcare access against patent obligations and lobbying pressures.
