## Core Argument
Mental accounting bias causes investors to evaluate decisions in isolated silos rather than holistically, leading to suboptimal financial outcomes.

## Key Points
- Money is fungible, but our brains treat it in separate mental ledgers for each source or investment vehicle.
- Loss aversion makes losses psychologically 3x more painful than equivalent gains, distorting portfolio decisions.
- We evolved to track reciprocity with individuals, but apply the same mental architecture to abstract fungible assets like money.

## Open Question
If mental accounting bias is deeply evolutionary and protective, when is it truly harmful versus when is it an appropriate risk-management instinct that shouldn't be overridden?

## India Angle
Indian retail investors and wealth managers frequently fall prey to mental accounting biases; understanding these patterns is critical for financial literacy and better investment decision-making in India's growing investor base.
