## Core Argument
Boredom at work signals midlife transition, not entrepreneurial calling; entrepreneurship requires purpose-driven conviction, not escape.

## Key Points
- Boredom in high achievers is a predictable life transition (late 30s-40s), not a career failure signal.
- True entrepreneurs are pulled by 'future-backward' vision or pushed by circumstance ('present-forward'), never by restlessness alone.
- Midlife transition requires self-awareness and exploration within current role or alternate paths—not impulsive business launch.

## Open Question
How does one distinguish between legitimate entrepreneurial calling and the existential restlessness that characterizes midlife transition—especially when both can feel equally compelling?

## India Angle
Relevant to Indian high achievers in corporate roles who face midlife transitions; addresses the cultural pressure to 'do something on your own' without deeper self-examination, common in India's entrepreneurship-obsessed ecosystem.
