## Core Argument
Success narratives exploit cognitive bias; circumstance and practice matter more than talent or age.

## Key Points
- Human minds crave familiar narratives of underdogs and overcoming odds, even when evidence contradicts them.
- Narrative fallacy: we retroactively construct explanations for success/failure based on outcomes, not actual causes.
- Tendulkar and Kambli had similar starts; Kambli's 'talent' came from cramped practice conditions, not innate ability.

## Open Question
If narratives are cognitive illusions that obscure truth, how do we actually identify and nurture real talent in sports, business, or any field?

## India Angle
Indian sports narrative through Tendulkar-Kambli comparison reveals how India's storytelling about success masks luck, circumstance, and narrative fallacy in evaluating talent and performance.
