## Core Argument
Living Wills shift India's end-of-life care from family-driven, emotionally-driven decisions to patient-centered autonomy despite deep cultural and systemic resistance.

## Key Points
- AMDs legally document individual preferences for life-sustaining treatment when incapacitated, guiding doctors and families in terminal illness situations
- Indian culture treats death discussions as taboo and inauspicious; financial dependence makes children, not patients, decision-makers in medical care
- Doctors lack palliative care training, fear litigation, and have financial incentives to pursue futile aggressive treatment in private healthcare systems

## Open Question
How can India culturally normalize end-of-life planning when death remains deeply taboo, families fear 'giving up,' and the healthcare system economically incentivizes aggressive intervention?

## India Angle
India's taboo around death discussions, family-centered (not individual-centered) medical decision-making, financial dependence of elderly on children, and weak palliative care training create unique barriers to end-of-life autonomy that AMDs must overcome
