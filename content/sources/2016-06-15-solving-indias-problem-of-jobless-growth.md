## Core Argument
Jobless growth is a systemic 5C problem requiring integrated 5S solutions, not siloed ministry fixes.

## Key Points
- India's employment elasticity halved from 0.44 (1999-2005) to 0.01 (2005-2010), approaching jobless growth despite high GDP.
- Siloed ministry approaches backfire: skilling without jobs, investment without ease of doing business, flexibility without social security worsen the system.
- Automation will displace 35-50% of jobs by 2035; India must integrate labour, skills, investment, regulation, and social security policies.

## Open Question
How can India pursue rapid job creation through Make in India and Skill India when simultaneous technological automation threatens to displace workers faster than new jobs emerge?

## India Angle
India's paradox of high GDP growth (fastest globally) but low job creation (slowest globally) requires systemic policy redesign; employment elasticity collapsed from 0.44 to 0.01, risking social instability despite economic expansion.
