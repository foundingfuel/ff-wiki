## Core Argument
Balance technology enthusiasm with scepticism; recognize costs alongside benefits to avoid narrow solutions.

## Key Points
- Sceptical optimism means evaluating technology pragmatically like any major purchase—understanding tradeoffs, not blind faith.
- Hedonic normalisation and replacement mean yesterday's revolutionary tech becomes today's baseline; this informs sustainable innovation strategy.
- Every technology has hidden costs; overstatement of benefits while ignoring downsides (climate change from coal power) leads to systemic problems.

## Open Question
If technological solutions are inherently limited and often create unintended costs, why should entrepreneurs and societies continue pursuing them rather than addressing root causes directly?

## India Angle
India's rapid technology adoption and startup culture often mirrors Silicon Valley optimism; a sceptical optimist approach could help Indian entrepreneurs and policymakers avoid costly over-investments in technological fixes for complex social problems like poverty and healthcare without addressing root causes.
