## Core Argument
Translated Indian-language literature reveals lived experiences and ideas invisible in English-only ecosystems.

## Key Points
- Regional Indian languages capture diverse lived experiences and intellectual traditions inaccessible through English alone.
- Translation infrastructure and awards are now making Indian-language literature visible globally, exemplified by Geetanjali Shree's Booker Prize win.
- Despite progress, ideas and literature flow into India through translations far more than they flow out to global audiences.

## Open Question
How can India balance celebrating its growing translation ecosystem while ensuring regional-language literature reaches truly global audiences, not just English readers?

## India Angle
Indian literature written in regional languages remains invisible to global audiences and even English-reading Indians; translation infrastructure is critical to democratizing access to India's intellectual and cultural wealth across linguistic boundaries.
