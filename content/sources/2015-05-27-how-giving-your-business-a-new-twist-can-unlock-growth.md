## Core Argument
Successful enterprises systematically iterate their strategy, holding resources in reserve for pivots until finding product-market fit.

## Key Points
- Initial strategies almost always fail; successful companies pivot multiple times before discovering working models.
- Enterprises must reserve resources for strategic iteration rather than committing everything to first-mover assumptions.
- Wikipedia and YouTube succeeded by abandoning original concepts and embracing emergent opportunities revealed by market feedback.

## Open Question
How much resource reserve is prudent before pivot becomes drift, and how do boards/investors distinguish between disciplined iteration and strategic indecision?

## India Angle
Indian startups and enterprises often launch with rigid initial strategies tied to founder vision or investor expectations; embracing the 'twist strategy' would help Indian businesses survive market realities, pivot faster than Western competitors, and build resilience in volatile domestic markets.
