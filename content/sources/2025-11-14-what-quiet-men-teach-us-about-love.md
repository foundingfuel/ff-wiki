## Core Argument
Silence inherited from past generations can express love as fully as words, requiring time and intention to translate.

## Key Points
- Indian men taught to see silence as strength inherit emotional restraint that becomes muscle memory across professions.
- Tenderness and vulnerability are reframed as strength, not weakness—a generational shift visible in how sons mirror fathers.
- Love expresses itself through different family roles: one person's voice balances another's stillness—neither hierarchical, both necessary.

## Open Question
How can men authentically bridge the gap between modern expectations for emotional openness and deeply ingrained cultural conditioning that equates silence with integrity?
