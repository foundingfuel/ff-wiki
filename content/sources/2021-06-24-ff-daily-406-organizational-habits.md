## Core Argument
Organizational habits, not individual decisions, drive company outcomes and create stability amid internal rivalries.

## Key Points
- Organizational routines emerge from uncoordinated patterns, gossip, and informal behaviors that converge into consensus decisions.
- Habits provide organizational memory and create 'truces' between competing groups, preventing internal conflict from destroying value.
- Remote work fatigue stems not from location but from clinging to office-centric practices designed for 1926 factory workers.

## Open Question
If organizational habits create necessary stability and truces, how can companies innovate and adapt without destabilizing the internal consensus that habits provide?

## India Angle
Indian organizations often struggle with entrenched hierarchies and informal decision-making; understanding organizational habits could help leaders at Indian companies—from startups to large conglomerates—recognize how uncoordinated routines shape strategy and culture, and deliberately redesign them for agility.
