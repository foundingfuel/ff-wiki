## Core Argument
Our anxiety about time stems from industrial abstraction of time; reconnecting to task-oriented living restores meaning and fluidity.

## Key Points
- Medieval task-orientation tied work to natural rhythms; modern clock-time creates artificial urgency and incompleteness.
- Abstract time containers create perpetual busyness or boredom; concrete tasks offer organic completion and presence.
- Power dynamics in aid systems persist because elites control funding; local ownership requires structural redistribution of decision-making authority.

## Open Question
If task-oriented living feels expansive and meaningful, why can't modern knowledge workers simply choose it, or must entire systems change first?

## India Angle
Indian knowledge workers and professionals increasingly adopt Western time-oriented productivity culture; understanding task-oriented rhythms could reshape how Indian organizations approach work, especially in agriculture and informal sectors where seasonal/natural rhythms still dominate.
