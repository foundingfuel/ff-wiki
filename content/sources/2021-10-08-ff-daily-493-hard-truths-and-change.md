## Core Argument
Hard paternalism through bans fails; libertarian paternalism using choice architecture and technology works better.

## Key Points
- Karnataka gaming ban uses hard paternalism; restricts liberty under guise of protection, replicating flawed global approaches.
- Libertarian paternalism nudges behaviour without eliminating choice—South Korea's permit system and fatigue mechanics offer alternatives.
- Regulators must shift from coercive bans to tech-enabled, choice-preserving solutions aligned with individual and societal welfare.

## Open Question
How can regulators balance protecting vulnerable populations (minors) from genuine harms without infantilizing adults or stifling innovation and personal freedom?

## India Angle
Karnataka's gaming ban reflects India's pattern of blunt regulatory approaches; the article suggests smarter alternatives like libertarian paternalism and tech-enabled nudges rather than outright bans, crucial for India's digital economy growth.
