## Core Argument
Covid-era sports bubbles expose and amplify existing hierarchies in fandom, access, and player economics.

## Key Points
- Live stadiums created authentic collective experience; simulation (fake crowd noise, gamified engagement) fragments that shared reality.
- Stadium access now tiered by wealth and tourism, excluding local supporters while global fans can pay entry—reversing traditional fandom economics.
- Players face unequal choices: elites can opt out; mid-tier players (Tewatia, Caruso, Saka) must play for career survival despite pandemic risk.

## Open Question
Does the post-Covid sports bubble represent temporary emergency measures or a permanent shift toward gamified, mediated, unequal access that benefits broadcasters and elites while eroding collective fandom?

## India Angle
Indian sports ecosystem mirrors global hierarchies—IPL bubble players (like Rahul Tewatia) face career pressure similar to global counterparts; stadium access exclusivity at Wankhede reflects broader equity issues in Indian sports fandom and institutional access
