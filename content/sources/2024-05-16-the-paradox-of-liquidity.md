## Core Argument
Liquidity paradox: public market flexibility breeds short-term thinking; private market illiquidity enforces patience and outperformance.

## Key Points
- Real-time pricing in public markets triggers behavioural biases (overconfidence, disposition effect, loss aversion) that destroy wealth through excessive trading.
- Private equity's 7-8% annual outperformance stems partly from forced patience and thorough due diligence, not just reduced risk.
- Investors should adopt private-market mentality—long-term fundamentals analysis—while using public market liquidity only for genuine tactical needs.

## Open Question
How can public market investors harness liquidity's genuine benefits (emergency access, risk management) without succumbing to the psychological trap of constant trading that erodes returns?

## India Angle
India's retail investor base is growing rapidly but lacks investing discipline; public market liquidity encourages speculative trading rather than wealth creation. Private markets (PE, VC, real estate) remain concentrated among institutional investors; bridging this gap requires shifting investor mindset from trading to ownership thinking.
