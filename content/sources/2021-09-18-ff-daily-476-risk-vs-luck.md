## Core Argument
Success stories obscure luck's role; bold rule-breaking becomes wisdom only in hindsight.

## Key Points
- The line between visionary entrepreneurship and recklessness depends heavily on outcome, not intent or method.
- Regulatory frameworks often lag behind innovation, creating ethical grey zones where law-breaking appears necessary for progress.
- Survivors like Vanderbilt are celebrated while failures are forgotten, creating survivorship bias in how we teach entrepreneurship.

## Open Question
If successful rule-breaking is celebrated as entrepreneurial wisdom, how should we distinguish between justified innovation and genuine crime when outcomes alone cannot determine morality?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs and regulators face similar tensions between enabling innovation and enforcing rule of law; historical figures like Tata and Birla navigated similar ambiguities, raising questions about how modern startups should balance disruption with compliance.
