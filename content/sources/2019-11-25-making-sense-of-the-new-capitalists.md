## Core Argument
Platforms operate via a playbook that captures markets through network effects, not production; traditional businesses face extinction without transformation.

## Key Points
- Platforms don't produce goods but wield disproportionate economic and social power through digital intermediation.
- Traditional businesses underestimate platform threats until aggregators achieve critical mass and market dominance.
- Platforms use five-part playbook: flywheel effects, subsidies, data leverage, regulatory arbitrage, and behavioral lock-in.

## Open Question
If platforms are inevitable and regulators lack effective tools to manage them, how can incumbent businesses and governments prevent monopolistic concentration without stifling innovation?

## India Angle
Indian businesses and regulators face unprecedented disruption from platform models (Swiggy, Zomato, Flipkart, Amazon); traditional firms lack digital DNA to compete; regulatory frameworks designed for older markets fail to address platform power dynamics and predatory practices in India's context.
