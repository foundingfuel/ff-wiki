## Core Argument
Good overpowers evil through nonviolent mass mobilization; India's coal crisis reflects governance indifference to early warnings.

## Key Points
- Nonviolent campaigns succeed 50% vs 26% for violent ones by mobilizing 11x more diverse participants.
- India's coal shortage stems from ignored warnings since 2016, not Black Swan events—a governance and institutional failure.
- Clear, transparent goals build trust; ambiguous reopening plans erode employee confidence and organizational effectiveness.

## Open Question
How can institutions balance nimble crisis response with the transparency needed to maintain stakeholder trust during rapid change?

## India Angle
India faces immediate coal supply crisis due to systemic governance failures; broader lesson on how institutional capacity and transparency affect economic stability and worker trust during transitions like office reopening.
