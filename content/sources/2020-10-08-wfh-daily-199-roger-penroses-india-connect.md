## Core Argument
Indian physicist Raychaudhuri's equation was foundational to Penrose's Nobel Prize-winning black hole research.

## Key Points
- Amalkumar Raychaudhuri developed the Raychaudhuri equation in 1955 at Calcutta's Ashutosh College
- Penrose and Hawking used this equation to develop singularity theorems that proved black holes exist
- Indian scientific contributions often remain obscured in global recognition of international breakthroughs

## Open Question
How can India ensure its foundational scientific contributions receive proportionate global recognition and institutional support for future research?

## India Angle
Indian physicist Amalkumar Raychaudhuri's foundational equation enabled Penrose's Nobel Prize-winning work on black holes, yet remains relatively unknown globally—highlighting how Indian scientific contributions often go unrecognized in international discourse.
