## Core Argument
Salaried employees increasingly adopt gig-mindset behaviours, but organisations remain stuck in control-command leadership, creating frustration.

## Key Points
- Gig mindset—taking initiative without approval when solving problems—is emerging in salaried employees across sectors globally.
- Leadership attitudes lag digital capabilities; only 25% of organisations practice open, participatory leadership despite 55-70% having digital tools.
- This mismatch creates emotional extremes in gig-minded workers: pride in achievements, frustration at being constrained by traditional hierarchies.

## Open Question
How can organisations harness gig-mindset entrepreneurial energy without undermining accountability, coordination, and strategic alignment that hierarchies provide?

## India Angle
Indian organisations, particularly in tech and large enterprises, face similar leadership-mindset gaps as described; gig mindset behaviours could unlock entrepreneurial talent within corporates and government agencies, but require cultural shifts from hierarchical control-and-command structures prevalent in Indian management.
