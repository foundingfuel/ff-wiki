## Core Argument
WhatsApp's data-sharing terms can be challenged under existing Indian law, not just future legislation.

## Key Points
- WhatsApp collects metadata (contacts, frequency, duration) even if chats remain encrypted end-to-end.
- India's IT Act Section 43A and Rules already provide data protection principles: consent, retention, usage, transfer.
- Puttaswamy judgment establishes privacy as fundamental right, enabling legal challenges to unconscionable terms.

## Open Question
How can India balance enabling digital platforms' business models with protecting citizen privacy when regulatory frameworks lag behind technological capability?

## India Angle
Indian users and regulators can invoke existing IT Act provisions (Section 43A, Rules) to challenge WhatsApp's data-sharing terms without waiting for the Personal Data Protection Bill; Puttaswamy judgment affirms privacy as fundamental right, giving legal precedent for contestation.
