## Core Argument
Employee monitoring assumes dishonesty; structured work and clarity eliminate need for surveillance.

## Key Points
- Monitoring via cameras unreliable—cannot measure mental engagement or intention, only physical presence.
- Honour-code systems reduce costs; trust with accountability consequences outperforms surveillance infrastructure.
- WFH monitoring ignores Indian legal obligations (creche facilities, caregiving) and disproportionately impacts working parents.

## Open Question
If structured work needs no supervisor, why do companies believe WFH requires intensified monitoring rather than clearer outcomes and fewer touchpoints?

## India Angle
Indian companies rushing to adopt surveillance tech during WFH transition without weighing legal (Maternity Benefit Act), cultural, and organizational costs; highlights gap between BCCL's techno-optimism and ground reality for Indian workers with caregiving responsibilities.
