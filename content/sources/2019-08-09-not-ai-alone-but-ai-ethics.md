## Core Argument
AI systems optimised for profit ignore ethical intent; bias in training data perpetuates discrimination against disadvantaged groups.

## Key Points
- Algorithms trained on biased historical data systematically disadvantage people from underrepresented geographies and income groups.
- Teleological ethics focus results; deontological perspective demands ethical intent—most AI systems lack the latter.
- Banks and AI creators often unaware of real-world harms until systems harm actual users like loan applicants.

## Open Question
How can organisations deploy AI at scale for commercial benefit while ensuring deontological ethical intent when awareness of harms often comes only after real-world deployment?

## India Angle
Indian AI developers and fintech companies face urgent need to audit algorithms for bias against lower-income users and geographies; regulatory frameworks must prevent discriminatory lending and credit decisions that perpetuate inequality in a context where algorithmic systems are rapidly deployed without ethical guardrails.
