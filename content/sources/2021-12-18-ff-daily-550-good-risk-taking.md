## Core Argument
Survival must precede success; asymmetric losses make path-dependent risk-taking fundamentally different from gambling.

## Key Points
- Order of events matters: losing capital first can create insolvency, making recovery impossible even if gains follow later.
- The 'absorbing barrier' concept shows why entrepreneurs must prioritize staying solvent over maximizing returns.
- Warren Buffett's principle—survive first, succeed second—applies to all risk-taking ventures, not just finance.

## Open Question
How do you balance the need for aggressive risk-taking to build breakthrough ventures with the survival-first principle that prevents catastrophic failure?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs and investors often operate with limited capital and high failure consequences; understanding asymmetric risk and survival-first principles is critical for sustainable business building in resource-constrained contexts.
