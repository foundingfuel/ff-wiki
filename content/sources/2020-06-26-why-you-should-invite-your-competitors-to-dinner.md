## Core Argument
Social ties and informal networks drive MSME success more than formal systems or technology efficiency alone.

## Key Points
- Tiruppur textile entrepreneurs strengthen peer relationships through rituals like personal dinner invitations, enabling trust-based advice.
- Banerjee and Munshi's research proves social networks matter: community ties influence investment patterns and business decisions.
- Technology solutions fail in MSMEs because they ignore the informal social fabric that actually drives business problem-solving.

## Open Question
How can policymakers and technologists design effective interventions for MSMEs when success relies on relationships and rituals that cannot be systematized or digitized?

## India Angle
Indian MSMEs, particularly in clusters like Tiruppur, rely on informal social networks and peer relationships rather than formal institutions. This contrasts with Western business models and explains why technology solutions often fail without understanding cultural context.
