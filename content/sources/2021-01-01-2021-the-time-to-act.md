## Core Argument
Covid-19 revealed fault lines in economies; India must solve systemic challenges through interdisciplinary thinking, not silos.

## Key Points
- Economists lack frameworks to measure human wellbeing and relationships; quantification alone is insufficient for policy.
- India's informal sector provides grassroots economic mobility; formalization without care risks eliminating opportunity pathways.
- Industrial policy sequencing matters: domestic competitiveness must precede equitable participation in global supply chains.

## Open Question
How can India balance the need for growth to lift poor people out of poverty with environmental sustainability and inequality reduction when growth itself has historically driven both degradation and inequity?

## India Angle
India must rethink industrial policy, informal sector dynamics, and employment creation post-Covid, rejecting both blind globalization and inward thinking in favor of sequenced, inclusive growth that strengthens domestic competitiveness before deep global integration.
