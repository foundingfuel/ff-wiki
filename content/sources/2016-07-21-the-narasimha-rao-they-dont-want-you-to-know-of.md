## Core Argument
Narasimha Rao's leadership during India's 1991 economic crisis reveals how visionary leaders operate under extreme constraints.

## Key Points
- Rao faced triple crisis: political weakness, currency collapse ($1.2B reserves), and national humiliation requiring bold reform.
- Strategic use of Manmohan Singh as shield allowed Rao to orchestrate transformative reforms while deflecting political backlash.
- Leadership traits—vision, courage, patience, ability to work alone—matter more than ideology or popular support in driving systemic change.

## Open Question
If Rao was the true architect of reforms but allowed Singh public credit, what does this reveal about the relationship between authentic leadership, institutional legitimacy, and historical narrative?

## India Angle
Reassesses P.V. Narasimha Rao's role in India's 1991 economic liberalisation—critical for understanding how Indian leaders navigate crisis, institutional constraints, and systemic transformation despite political isolation.
