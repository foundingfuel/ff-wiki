## Core Argument
Normalised contract labour in Indian manufacturing risks legal, organisational, and societal collapse despite past warnings.

## Key Points
- 42% of organised manufacturing workforce now on contract, up from temporary exception to permanent structural model
- Contract workers earn 30-40% less, lack benefits and face symbolic discrimination creating cultural fault lines within organisations
- Supreme Court judgments warn against equal-pay violations and contractor misuse; companies slide into legal grey zone despite risks

## Open Question
How can companies justify structural reliance on precarious labour when past crises like Maruti Suzuki (2012) proved such models destabilise organisations, yet no systemic change has occurred in 13 years?

## India Angle
India's organised manufacturing sector now has 42% contract workforce—highest ever—creating structural precarity that undermines both corporate health and social stability amid rigid labour laws and weak enforcement of constitutional employment principles.
