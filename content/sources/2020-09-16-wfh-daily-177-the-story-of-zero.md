## Core Argument
Revolutionary ideas face resistance until practical benefits overcome ideological objections and enable progress.

## Key Points
- Zero's invention was an imaginative leap solving a symbolic need, not a practical one initially.
- Religious and cultural resistance delayed zero's adoption in Europe despite its mathematical superiority.
- Commercial utility eventually overcame all objections, making zero indispensable for trade and progress.

## Open Question
How do we distinguish between legitimate ideological concerns and resistance that merely delays inevitable adoption of transformative ideas?

## India Angle
Illustrates how India's mathematical innovation (zero) faced global resistance but eventually enabled commerce—relevant to how Indian businesses today must navigate regulatory resistance and demonstrate value to gain legitimacy; also touches on Toyota's India expansion tensions around tax policy and investor confidence.
