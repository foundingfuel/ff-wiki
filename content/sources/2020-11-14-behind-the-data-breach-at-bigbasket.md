## Core Argument
Data breach disclosure in India exposes tension between transparency norms and extortion accusations.

## Key Points
- BigBasket and Paytm took legal action against breach-reporter Cyble, framing disclosure as extortion rather than public duty.
- Indian companies lack mature protocols for breach response, creating ambiguity on disclosure timing and vendor accountability.
- Infosec community divided on whether public disclosure serves public good or enables extortion threats against firms.

## Open Question
If security researchers have a duty to disclose breaches publicly, how do companies distinguish between responsible disclosure and blackmail—and who sets these norms in India's nascent regulatory environment?

## India Angle
Indian startups and enterprises lack mature data protection frameworks and crisis management playbooks; the BigBasket-Cyble incident reveals gaps in how Indian companies handle breaches, regulatory compliance, and vendor relationships in the absence of clear GDPR-like standards.
