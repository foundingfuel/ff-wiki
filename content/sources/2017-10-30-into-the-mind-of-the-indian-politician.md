## Core Argument
Politicians understand voters as three concentric circles with distinct motivations, not as homogeneous masses.

## Key Points
- Group I: ideologically committed thinkers and doers who form the party's core organizational backbone.
- Group II: self-serving business classes and NRIs seeking transactional benefits, easily appeased by promises.
- Group III: largest vote bank of neutral PLU voters shaped by media narratives, not ideology.

## Open Question
If Group III voters are uninformed and media-driven, how can meaningful democratic participation and accountability function in a system where the largest vote bank lacks coherent ideological moorings?

## India Angle
Understanding how Indian politicians segment and strategically engage voter bases—the ideological core, self-serving elites, and neutral masses—reveals mechanisms of political power and democratic participation unique to India's electoral context.
