## Core Argument
Design thinking—balancing technical, human, and financial constraints—is becoming the next mandatory business paradigm like Quality did in the 1980s.

## Key Points
- Designers solve problems by simultaneously considering feasibility, desirability, and viability; managers traditionally ignore this integrated approach.
- Design thinking methodology (empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test) is teachable and applicable across industries and complex multi-stakeholder contexts.
- Critics argue design thinking is oversold as a process fix; real transformation requires training people to be creative, not just following methodology steps.

## Open Question
Can design thinking be systematized and taught as a universal process, or does it require deeper cultural and individual creative capability that processes alone cannot unlock?

## India Angle
Indian enterprises scaling beyond operational excellence need design thinking to solve complex multi-stakeholder problems (e.g., fintech, healthtech, gov tech); adoption remains limited outside tech startups, representing a competitive gap.
