## Core Argument
Pursuing meaningful hobbies requires intentional choice, not time; it transforms how leaders show up in work and life.

## Key Points
- Time scarcity is a myth—the real constraint is intent and priority-setting among busy leaders.
- Choosing hobbies you're bad at builds resilience and separates identity from professional competence.
- Solo, immersive hobbies create space for decompression and self-dialogue essential for high-performing individuals.

## Open Question
How do leaders sustainably protect hobby time when organizational culture implicitly rewards those who prioritize work above all else?

## India Angle
Indian high-performing executives in retail and advertising balance intense work schedules with personal passions—a model relevant for India's demanding corporate culture where work-life integration remains aspirational rather than practiced.
