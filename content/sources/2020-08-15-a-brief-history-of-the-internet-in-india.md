## Core Argument
India's internet story reveals how scarcity, high costs, and infrastructure gaps delayed connectivity but created unique opportunities.

## Key Points
- VSNL's 1995 launch made internet theoretically available but prohibitively expensive (Rs 15,000+ annually for basic access).
- Landline infrastructure scarcity meant internet access required decade-long waiting lists, limiting early adoption to offices and institutions.
- Cost barriers forced knowledge workers to share single office connections, creating bottlenecks but also communal learning spaces.

## Open Question
How did India leapfrog from being excluded by infrastructure and cost barriers in 1995 to becoming a mobile-first digital economy by 2020, and what was lost in that transition?

## India Angle
Traces India's internet adoption from 1995 (VSNL launch) through prohibitive costs and infrastructure barriers that delayed mass adoption, establishing how India's digital divide shaped the nation's tech trajectory differently from the West.
