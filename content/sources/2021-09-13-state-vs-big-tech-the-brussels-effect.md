## Core Argument
EU's GDPR sets global regulatory precedent, forcing Big Tech to internalize compliance costs and reshape business models.

## Key Points
- GDPR inspired 120+ countries; Big Tech spent $100M+ on compliance, reshaping digital service architecture globally.
- EU regulatory approach rooted in historical privacy concerns and protection of local industries against American tech dominance.
- Brussels Effect: EU's market size forces American tech giants to adopt stringent standards globally, not just regionally.

## Open Question
Can strict privacy regulation protect individuals and competition without stifling innovation and burdening startups disproportionately compared to entrenched giants?

## India Angle
India is developing its own data protection framework (DPDP Act); understanding EU's regulatory model and its global spillover effects is critical for Indian policymakers balancing innovation with privacy protection, and for Indian tech companies operating internationally.
