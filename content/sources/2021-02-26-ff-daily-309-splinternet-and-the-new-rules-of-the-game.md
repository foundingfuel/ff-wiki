## Core Argument
National regulations are fragmenting the internet, forcing global platforms to operate under conflicting local rules.

## Key Points
- Rising nationalism and trade disputes prompt governments worldwide to impose divergent internet regulations
- Platform fragmentation happens through regulatory crackdowns, not just outright bans—Australia's news deal shows collateral damage
- India's draft IT rules exemplify this global trend, reshaping how tech and media companies must operate locally

## Open Question
How can companies operating global platforms adapt to contradictory local regulations without fragmenting user experience or compromising their business model?

## India Angle
India's new IT intermediary rules are part of a global splintering of the internet driven by nationalism and local regulation, directly affecting how Indian tech platforms operate and compete internationally.
