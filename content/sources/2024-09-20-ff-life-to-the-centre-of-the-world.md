## Core Argument
Travel reveals how ancient cultural practices and architectural resilience persist across geographies and historical upheaval.

## Key Points
- Tashkent's three distinct urban layers—Islamic old city, Russian colonial quarters, modern capital—reflect post-Soviet identity reconstruction.
- Centuries-old pedagogic practices in madrasahs anticipated modern breakout room learning, suggesting innovation often rediscovers tradition.
- Cultural artifacts like the 7th-century Quran survived political upheaval through circulation across empires, embodying cultural continuity.

## Open Question
How do post-Soviet Central Asian nations balance preservation of Islamic heritage, Soviet legacy, and modernization without erasing any layer of their identity?

## India Angle
Travel writing that connects Uzbek cultural practices (ikat weaving, madrasah pedagogy) to Indian parallels (Odisha ikat), offering Indian readers a mirror to understand their own heritage through Central Asian examples.
