## Core Argument
AI debate ignores existential risks at top and digital apartheid at bottom; India must pursue both governance and grassroots access.

## Key Points
- Demis Hassabis proposed global AI governance body modeled on IAEA, rejected by world leaders despite feasibility.
- Sachin Joshi's 'AI on Wheels' mobile bus teaches disadvantaged children AI tools while building foundational literacy in Nashik slums.
- Without proactive inclusion, 2+ billion people risk permanent technological exclusion, creating a global digital caste system.

## Open Question
How can developing nations like India simultaneously pursue cutting-edge AI sovereignty while ensuring grassroots populations gain basic technological literacy before AGI emerges?

## India Angle
India faces dual AI crisis: existential risk of AGI without governance frameworks, and digital exclusion of 2+ billion people. Solutions like 'AI on Wheels' in Nashik show grassroots innovation, but require scaling across Indian cities and coordination with global governance bodies like IAEA.
