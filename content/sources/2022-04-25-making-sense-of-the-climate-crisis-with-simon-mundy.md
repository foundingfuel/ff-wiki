## Core Argument
Climate crisis demands systemic business-policy innovation, not just consumer action; supply chain justice ties developed and developing worlds.

## Key Points
- India exemplifies climate paradox: severe impacts with minimal historical emissions; urgent energy and agricultural adaptation needs.
- Green transitions carry hidden costs—EV cobalt mining, solar labor practices, engineered foods—revealing developed-world solutions creating developing-world harms.
- Businesses must embed climate strategy into core operations; government policy and citizen pressure matter more than individual consumer choices.

## Open Question
How can developing nations like India achieve energy security and poverty reduction while bearing disproportionate climate impacts, without subsidizing developed-world green transitions through exploitative supply chains?

## India Angle
India faces acute climate impacts (droughts, floods, cyclones) while lacking carbon footprint of developed nations; energy scale needs, agricultural vulnerability, and cobalt mining ethics directly challenge India's development-climate balance.
