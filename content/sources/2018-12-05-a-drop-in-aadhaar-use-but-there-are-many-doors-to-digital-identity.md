## Core Argument
Aadhaar's regulatory constraints push India toward decentralized, citizen-controlled digital identity alternatives.

## Key Points
- Aadhaar enrolments dropped 88% post-Supreme Court verdict; authentications fell 24% due to biometric access restrictions.
- Supreme Court's contradictory verdict on private-sector biometric access harmed fintech but left Reliance advantaged.
- Multiple digital identity paths (DEPA, OTP, QR codes, private platforms) create ecosystem fragmentation and monopoly risks.

## Open Question
Can India build a truly decentralized, citizen-controlled digital identity ecosystem without recreating new monopolies through tech giants or inadvertently fragmenting its fintech industry further?

## India Angle
Aadhaar's post-Supreme Court decline creates both risks and opportunities for India's digital identity ecosystem. The drop in private-sector authentication access threatens fintech growth but forces exploration of sovereign alternatives (DEPA, QR codes), positioning India to develop citizen-controlled digital identity models against global tech monopolies.
