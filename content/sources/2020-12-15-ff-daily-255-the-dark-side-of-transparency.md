## Core Argument
Transparency without integrity, honesty, and informed citizenry creates illusion of accountability without substance.

## Key Points
- Transparency is a tool exploited by dishonest actors to create false appearance of honesty without actual ethical behaviour.
- Information overload and poor information diets prevent citizens from extracting truth from disclosed data, rendering transparency ineffective.
- Systemic change requires cleaning up root causes and building public will to consume information responsibly, not just exposing problems.

## Open Question
If transparency alone cannot guarantee honesty and citizens lack capacity or willingness to interpret disclosed information critically, what combination of institutional design, media literacy, and incentives actually drives accountability?

## India Angle
India's governance and regulatory systems increasingly rely on transparency mechanisms (RTI, disclosure norms); this article challenges whether transparency alone drives accountability without institutional integrity and citizen capability to process information.
