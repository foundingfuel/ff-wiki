## Core Argument
India's choice of heroes reveals systemic blindness to real achievers, hampering societal progress and inspiration.

## Key Points
- Well-educated Indians can't identify outstanding achievers like Manindra Agrawal despite their public visibility and accolades.
- Media, political parties, and cultural norms elevate film/sports/political figures while marginalizing scientists, social workers, and educators.
- Women role models remain scarce; most celebrated are family inheritors or NRI-returned multinational executives, not self-made achievers.

## Open Question
If real achievers are publicly visible and accomplished, why do ordinary Indians not recognize them—is it media failure, cultural preference, or systemic invisibility by design?

## India Angle
India fails to recognize and celebrate its own achievers—scientists, social workers, educators—while disproportionately elevating film stars, cricketers, and politicians. This reflects gaps in media coverage, political processes, and cultural values that undermine nation-building.
