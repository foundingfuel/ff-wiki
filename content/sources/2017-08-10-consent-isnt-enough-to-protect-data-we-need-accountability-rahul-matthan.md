## Core Argument
Consent alone cannot protect individuals from data harm; accountability mechanisms must replace or supplement consent frameworks.

## Key Points
- Consent fatigue and forced adoption make true consent impossible when services become social necessities.
- Interconnected databases create emergent harms impossible to predict even with full disclosure.
- Financial harm from algorithmic credit decisions disproportionately affects the newly banked in India.

## Open Question
How can accountability mechanisms function without creating regulatory burden that prevents financial inclusion through data-driven lending?

## India Angle
India is drafting its first comprehensive data protection bill amid rapid fintech growth and algorithmic decision-making in microfinance. Consent-based frameworks are inadequate for protecting vulnerable populations entering digital finance; accountability mechanisms must be built into Indian data law from inception.
