## Core Argument
Large-startup partnerships require genuine business model commitment, not token investments; India's ecosystem faces distribution and capability gaps.

## Key Points
- Corporations lack bandwidth for innovation; startups offer external ideas but require market access and ecosystem knowledge.
- Successful partnerships happen at the intersection of corporate priority and non-core territory; genuine investment in capacity and distribution is essential.
- Indian context: startups seek market access over capital; large firms fail to provide distribution, limiting partnership value beyond funding.

## Open Question
If startups want VC funding and market access over corporate capital, and corporations struggle to provide distribution, what is the actual incentive structure that makes large-startup partnerships work in the Indian ecosystem?

## India Angle
Indian corporations struggle with distribution access for startups and lack commitment to business model innovation; startups must navigate ecosystem where VCs, not corporates, provide primary funding, requiring different partnership strategies.
