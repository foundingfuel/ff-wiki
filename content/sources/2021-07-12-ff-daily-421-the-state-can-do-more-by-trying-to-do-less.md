## Core Argument
State effectiveness increases by reducing bureaucratic burden and focusing on high-impact priorities rather than maintaining status quo processes.

## Key Points
- Bureaucratic bloat (thick files) signals low-priority issues circulating endlessly; thin files indicate actionable opportunities deserving focused attention.
- Rural health infrastructure gaps—expired drugs, staff shortages, missing protocols—undermine pandemic preparedness despite centralized SOP guidelines.
- Vaccine distribution reveals tension between humanitarian intent and geopolitical considerations; India's export halt prioritizes domestic over global need.

## Open Question
How can the state do more by doing less when crisis response (pandemic, healthcare) demands expanded institutional action and resource deployment?

## India Angle
Indian state capacity and bureaucratic efficiency directly impact pandemic preparedness and rural healthcare delivery; systemic inefficiencies in governance prevent effective crisis response despite institutional intent.
