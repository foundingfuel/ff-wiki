## Core Argument
Statistical models risk prioritizing fit over predictive accuracy, misleading policymakers during crises.

## Key Points
- Overfitting makes models look better on paper but fail in real-world predictions, crowding out honest alternatives.
- Brazil's experience shows assumptions about herd immunity and low-risk groups can collapse unexpectedly.
- Decision-makers often work backward to rationalize model idiosyncrasies rather than questioning underlying assumptions.

## Open Question
How can policymakers balance the need for predictive models in crises while guarding against the human tendency to rationalize flawed assumptions and overfit data?

## India Angle
India's pandemic response relied heavily on predictive models for healthcare capacity planning; the piece warns Indian policymakers and health authorities about overfitting risks when projecting COVID caseloads and policy decisions, especially as Brazil's second wave caught many off guard.
