## Core Argument
Pandemics force impossible trade-offs between lives and livelihoods; politicians must mediate expert claims, not defer to either discipline.

## Key Points
- Epidemiologists and economists have structural incentives to overstate risks, creating asymmetric forecasting errors.
- Pandemic policy choices require political accountability, not technocratic authority, because they involve irreducible value trade-offs.
- Transparency and candor—in shareholder letters and workplace communication—build stakeholder trust during crisis uncertainty.

## Open Question
If both epidemiologists and economists systematically overstate their domain's risks, how can policymakers calibrate the true magnitude of pandemic threats to make informed trade-offs?

## India Angle
India's pandemic response exposed institutional gaps in balancing epidemiological vs economic priorities; policymakers lacked frameworks for managing competing expert advice and long-term institutional learning from historical pandemics.
