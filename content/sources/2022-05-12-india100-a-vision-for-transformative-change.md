## Core Argument
India needs a new paradigm of progress, not incremental improvements to a flawed growth model.

## Key Points
- 1991 reforms lifted millions from poverty but failed to deliver political, social, economic freedoms or environmental sustainability.
- Crises (2008, 2020, Ukraine) reveal structural weaknesses; returning to old models is neither feasible nor desirable.
- Transformative change requires holding 'creative tension' between clear vision and honest reality; leaders must unlearn old thinking.

## Open Question
How can India pursue necessary paradigm shifts when powerful stakeholders benefit from existing systems and transformation risks disrupting what currently works, however imperfectly?

## India Angle
India must fundamentally rethink its post-1991 economic model by 2047 to achieve genuine development beyond GDP growth—addressing inequality, environmental degradation, and social oppression that reforms failed to solve.
