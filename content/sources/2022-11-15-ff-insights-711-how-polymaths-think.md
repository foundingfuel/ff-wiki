## Core Argument
Polymathic thinking—connecting disparate domains—unlocks creative solutions but requires personal integrity to sustain impact.

## Key Points
- Oliver Sacks solved a neurology puzzle by integrating music and philosophy, not just medical literature alone.
- Jesse Harvey innovated harm-reduction policy but couldn't apply his principles to save himself from addiction.
- Genuine breakthroughs require both cross-disciplinary thinking and the humility to acknowledge personal limits.

## Open Question
Why can polymaths generate brilliant solutions for others' problems yet fail to solve their own—is self-knowledge the missing link?

## India Angle
Indian leaders and innovators must develop polymathic thinking to navigate complex challenges—from healthcare policy to social entrepreneurship—where conventional expertise alone proves insufficient; relevant to India's need for creative problem-solving in governance and social sectors.
