## Core Argument
Japanese companies survive crises by investing in community and controlling what's controllable, not chasing profits.

## Key Points
- Japanese firms prioritize creating lasting societal changes, resulting in 40% of 300+ year companies being Japan-based.
- Lawson CEO reopened stores in tsunami-ravaged areas to restore hope and normalcy, not immediate profits or shareholder returns.
- Permanent soft skills like resilience, acceptance, and adaptability matter more than technical skills in volatile environments.

## Open Question
How do Indian businesses balance quarterly earnings pressures and shareholder demands with the long-term, community-first philosophy that enables Japanese firms to thrive across centuries?

## India Angle
Indian family businesses and large enterprises often lack the long-term community-oriented mindset that keeps Japanese companies resilient; adopting stakeholder capitalism over shareholder primacy could help Indian firms weather crises and build sustained competitive advantage.
