## Core Argument
Speed-obsessed food systems harm ecosystems and pleasure; Slow Food offers a countermodel balancing sustainability with human flourishing.

## Key Points
- Industrialization normalized speed in eating, marketing convenience over nutrition; Slow Food reverses this by prioritizing local, seasonal, artisanal production.
- Food industry homogenization reduces biodiversity dangerously—Italy lost 94% of artichoke varieties in a century due to efficiency-driven farming.
- Individual action (buying local, learning to cook) can overcome reflexive convenience-seeking and reclaim pleasure in food culture.

## Open Question
How can Slow Food principles scale to feed India's 1.4 billion people without abandoning efficiency gains that prevent hunger, especially in price-sensitive markets?

## India Angle
India's rapid adoption of speed-based delivery models (Zomato, food tech) mirrors Western industrialization patterns; applying Slow Food principles could reshape India's food systems, support local farmers, preserve regional cuisines, and address sustainability without sacrificing competitiveness.
