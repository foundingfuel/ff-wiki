## Core Argument
Obsession with metrics inhibits creativity and innovation; unmeasurable risk-taking requires imagination over precision.

## Key Points
- Numerical metrics cannot capture complexity; they promise false precision and mislead decision-makers.
- True innovation requires imagination to envision unrealized possibilities, not analysis of past data.
- Practical judgment demands grasping unique particularities and feeling 'the whole,' which metrics cannot supply.

## Open Question
If organizations need both accountability (requiring metrics) and creativity (requiring freedom from measurement), how do leaders balance these competing demands without sacrificing either?

## India Angle
Indian companies increasingly adopt metrics-driven management imported from Silicon Valley and Western consulting; this creates tension with India's entrepreneurial ecosystem that traditionally relies on founder intuition, local knowledge, and relationship-based decision-making. Risk-averse metrics culture may stifle the very innovation India needs for global competitiveness.
