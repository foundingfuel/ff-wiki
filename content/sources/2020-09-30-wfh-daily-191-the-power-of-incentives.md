## Core Argument
Right incentive structures drive organizational outcomes more powerfully than most leaders realize or allocate resources to.

## Key Points
- Federal Express and Xerox cases show misaligned incentives sabotaged performance; realigning pay structures solved systemic failures.
- India's 238 SEZs occupy <500 sq km versus Shenzhen's 2000 sq km, limiting cluster effects, knowledge transfer, and spillovers.
- South Korea's pandemic preparedness improved after 2015 MERS failures through simulations; learning requires experiencing consequences first.

## Open Question
If leaders consistently underestimate incentive power (as Munger admits), how can organizations systematically audit and redesign their incentive structures without external crises forcing change?

## India Angle
India's SEZ strategy and pandemic preparedness reveal gaps in incentive design and learning from experience; Indian businesses competing globally need to match Chinese operational efficiency to attract manufacturing diversification.
