## Core Argument
China strategically orchestrated Uber's exit to consolidate digital markets under state-aligned monopolies.

## Key Points
- Chinese government uses regulation to shield domestic tech giants while allowing them entrepreneurial freedom within carved-out markets.
- Didi's merger with Kuaidi, then acquisition of Uber, was state-facilitated consolidation rewarding 'fittest' player willing to follow rules.
- Alibaba, Baidu, Tencent control 70% of China's internet traffic and cross-own stakes in Didi, creating impenetrable incumbent moat.

## Open Question
How can democracies protect domestic innovation while competing with state-coordinated tech ecosystems without abandoning liberal market principles?

## India Angle
India's tech ecosystem faces similar pressures—foreign companies seeking market dominance while regulators must balance openness with protecting domestic champions. The Didi-Uber model offers a template Indian policymakers may consciously or unconsciously adopt for ride-sharing, payments, and e-commerce.
