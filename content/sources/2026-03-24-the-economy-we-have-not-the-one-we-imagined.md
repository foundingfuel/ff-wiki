## Core Argument
India's digital convenience layer masked structural decay; crisis reveals the economy we actually have, not the one we imagined.

## Key Points
- Manufacturing share frozen at 14% despite a decade of Make in India policy; India skipped essential factory-driven middle-class formation phase.
- 46% of workforce in agriculture generating <20% of GDP reveals productivity mismatch; platform economy monetised top, lent to middle, bypassed rest.
- Private capex at decade low despite strongest corporate balance sheets in 15 years; bureaucratic friction deters investment conviction despite capital availability.

## Open Question
Crisis creates political will for reform, but does India have capacity to simultaneously address labour absorption, Chinese manufacturing competition, and bureaucratic efficiency without creating new distortions?

## India Angle
India's growth narrative masked structural weaknesses—stagnant manufacturing (14% GDP share since 2013), inability to transition workers from agriculture to industry, and bureaucratic friction deterring private investment. The Gulf crisis exposes this fragility and creates a reform window.
