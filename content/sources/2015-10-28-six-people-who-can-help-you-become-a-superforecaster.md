## Core Argument
Superforecasting skill comes from thinking patterns, not intelligence—adopt fox thinking, probabilistic reasoning, and constant updating.

## Key Points
- Fox thinking (multiple perspectives) beats hedgehog thinking (single narrative) for accuracy and foresight.
- Fermi-izing breaks complex problems into knowable sub-questions, enabling decisions under uncertainty.
- Constant updating using Bayesian logic and granular probability estimates improves forecast precision significantly.

## Open Question
If superforecasting is a learnable skill rooted in thinking patterns rather than expertise, why do experts typically underperform—and what structural incentives prevent institutions from adopting these methods?

## India Angle
Indian professionals and leaders can adopt superforecasting frameworks to improve decision-making in business, policy, and organizational contexts where uncertainty is high—critical for navigating India's dynamic markets and complex governance challenges.
