## Core Argument
Happiness emerges from meaning-making and perspective, not circumstances; leaders must help others embrace discomfort.

## Key Points
- Viktor Frankl's Holocaust account shows contentment comes from perspective, not external conditions.
- Leaders shape the future by helping others accept disruption to status quo, not just react.
- Cultural practices—even finger counting—reveal how meaning-making is embedded in systems and traditions.

## Open Question
If happiness is purely about perspective and meaning-making, how do leaders ensure systemic change doesn't become merely psychological reframing that avoids addressing material injustice?

## India Angle
Indian leaders like Indra Nooyi demonstrate how reframing adversity and discomfort—Viktor Frankl's core insight—enables transformational change in large organizations; relevant for Indian CEOs navigating disruption in traditional sectors.
