## Core Argument
Incumbents survive through longevity but fail to thrive; they lose competitive share through arrogance and inability to adapt to disruption.

## Key Points
- Data shows top global and Indian MNCs grew slower than GDP; survival ≠ thriving in competitive markets.
- Incumbents' 12 deadly sins include arrogance (Maruti missing SUV segment), inability to innovate, and organisational inertia.
- Individual sports trio (Federer-Nadal-Djokovic) exception proves adaptation possible; most incumbents fail when rules/technologies change.

## Open Question
If 40% of Fortune Global 500 companies remain after 25 years, does longevity actually indicate failure to thrive, or is market share loss inevitable in mature industries?

## India Angle
Indian incumbents (top 50 MNCs) grew at 4% vs India's GDP growth of 5.1% over 2010-2021, losing market share and contribution to GDP from 26% to 22%. Maruti's mistakes in SUVs and EVs illustrate how Indian market leaders fail to adapt. Contrasts with regulated exceptions like ITC.
