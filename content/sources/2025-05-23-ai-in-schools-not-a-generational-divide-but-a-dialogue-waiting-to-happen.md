## Core Argument
AI isn't changing schools; it's accelerating existing shifts from collective to individual learning without institutional dialogue.

## Key Points
- Students view AI as problem-solving tool, not cheating—seeking conceptual clarity over rote learning within rigid syllabus constraints.
- Teachers lack protocols, support, and discussions despite quietly using AI themselves; classroom conversation is hollowing out as students ask tools instead of peers.
- Schools must build frameworks for critical thinking and ethical AI use rather than ban or ignore; transformation is already underway, dialogue is missing.

## Open Question
How do educators balance student autonomy and AI access with meaningful classroom dialogue and conceptual ownership when institutional frameworks don't yet exist?

## India Angle
Indian schools across Tier 1 and Tier 2 cities face urgent need to build AI literacy frameworks and teacher-student dialogue before policy catches up; addresses shift from collectivist to hyper-individualist learning models specific to Indian education system.
