## Core Argument
Asking the right question reveals root causes; blaming convenient scapegoats masks deeper systemic problems.

## Key Points
- Predatory lending to taxi drivers, not Uber, caused medallion price collapse; tech was convenient scapegoat.
- Ather challenged low-price expectations by building superior products and supporting ecosystem infrastructure.
- Singapore's 'think space not land' approach reframes constraints as innovation opportunities, applicable to India's resource challenges.

## Open Question
How do entrepreneurs and policymakers distinguish between genuine technology disruption and technology as convenient villain for pre-existing systemic failures?

## India Angle
Indian policymakers and entrepreneurs must ask deeper questions before blaming technology (Aadhaar, Uber) for systemic failures; Ather Energy shows how Indian startups can build premium products and ecosystems rather than competing on price alone.
