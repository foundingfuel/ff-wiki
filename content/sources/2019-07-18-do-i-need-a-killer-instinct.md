## Core Argument
Organisations must move beyond gendered leadership stereotypes to unlock diverse styles and advance women leaders.

## Key Points
- Women face a double bind: judged as too soft or too aggressive, while masculine traits are rewarded as leadership default.
- Feminine competencies like collaboration and empathy are future leadership essentials but systemically undervalued in practice.
- Leaders must assess personal style skews, develop missing competencies contextually, and ensure visibility of strengths organisations claim to value.

## Open Question
If senior leadership genuinely believes in inclusive, diverse leadership styles, why do promotion decisions and manager feedback consistently reward only masculine traits?

## India Angle
Indian workplaces, particularly male-dominated industries and banking/BPO sectors, perpetuate masculine leadership stereotypes that undermine women's advancement despite stated inclusion commitments. The article addresses how Indian organisations can build genuinely inclusive cultures by recognising diverse leadership styles.
