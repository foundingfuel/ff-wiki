## Core Argument
India's reactive policy bans destroy entrepreneurship; Kerala shows alternative model of building quality brands and local prosperity.

## Key Points
- Kerala evolved unique entrepreneurship model where local brands (Johns, Popy umbrellas) transitioned commodities to premium products through quality, not marketing.
- Policy bans on tobacco/alcohol reduce vendor viability, driving them toward other products; unintended consequences worsen consumer access and market efficiency.
- Kerala's high wages and immigrant labor indicate economic strength; locals thrive differently than Mumbai's aspirational model based on opportunity.

## Open Question
Can India build entrepreneurship through constraint-driven innovation (Kerala model) while global disruption accelerates, or will policy paralysis and missed opportunities leave it unprepared for systemic change?
