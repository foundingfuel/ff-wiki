## Core Argument
Judging plans by outcomes alone blinds us to process quality; nature exposure offers measurable stress relief.

## Key Points
- Halo effect makes us conflate outcome success with strategic excellence, obscuring actual process quality and causation.
- Evaluating processes during execution, not retroactively via single outcomes, reduces cognitive bias and improves decision-making.
- Forest bathing (shinrin-yoku) measurably lowers cortisol and blood pressure, offering accessible wellness for high-stress professionals.

## Open Question
How do we build reliable decision-making systems when single outcomes provide insufficient data, yet most strategic choices happen only once?

## India Angle
Indian professionals and leaders must guard against halo effect bias in performance evaluations and business decisions; forest bathing and nature-based wellness practices offer underutilized pathways for stress management in high-pressure work cultures.
