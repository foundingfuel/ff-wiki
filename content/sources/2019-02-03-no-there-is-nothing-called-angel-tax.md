## Core Argument
Section 56 misapplication creates taxation friction that India's startup ecosystem cannot afford if it wants more entrepreneurs.

## Key Points
- Section 56 targets value transfers below fair market value, originally designed to prevent black money laundering, not inhibit angel investment.
- Startups face harassment through repeated notices, unreasonable FMV assessments, and pressure on friends/family investors despite raising on ideas, not business plans.
- Recent government amendments are insufficient; India needs regulatory evolution to distinguish between tax evasion and legitimate early-stage funding practices.

## Open Question
How can India distinguish between legitimate angel investment (which relies on subjective valuation of pre-revenue ideas) and actual tax evasion through asset undervaluation, without stifling entrepreneurship?

## India Angle
Angel Tax under Section 56 of Indian Income Tax Code is a uniquely Indian regulatory problem that discourages early-stage funding and threatens India's startup ecosystem ambitions; the government's recent amendments remain inadequate for addressing founder and investor concerns.
