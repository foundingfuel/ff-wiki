## Core Argument
Supreme Court's ambiguous Section 57 ruling leaves businesses uncertain whether Aadhaar can power digital identity innovation.

## Key Points
- Aadhaar eKYC reduced KYC costs from Rs 1,500 to Rs 20 and onboarding time from days to minutes, enabling financial inclusion.
- Privacy protection and data exploitation risks created legitimate tension between state surveillance safeguards and innovation enablement.
- Unclear court verdict creates two interpretations—whether private sector access is permanently closed or contingent on better legal/technical safeguards.

## Open Question
How do you balance enabling innovation through shared identity infrastructure with protecting citizens from data exploitation when the legal boundaries remain deliberately unclear?

## India Angle
India's Aadhaar platform sits at the intersection of innovation, financial inclusion, and privacy rights. The Supreme Court's ambiguous ruling on Section 57 directly impacts how Indian businesses can scale identity verification—critical for banking, telecom, and fintech serving India 2 and India 3. This shapes whether India's digital infrastructure becomes an enabler or barrier to inclusion.
