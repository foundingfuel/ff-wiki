## Core Argument
India's healthcare crisis is solvable through innovation and cross-subsidization models, not spending alone.

## Key Points
- Government spends only 1% of GDP on health; private out-of-pocket spending is inefficient due to information asymmetry.
- Proven low-cost, high-quality models exist: Aravind Eye Care, Tata Memorial, CMC Vellore demonstrate scalable solutions.
- Innovation in manufacturing (intraocular lenses at $2) and policy can bridge gap between current outcomes and potential.

## Open Question
If India has proven microcosms of affordable, high-quality healthcare (Aravind, Tata Memorial), why haven't these models scaled systemically across the country despite decades of knowledge?

## India Angle
India spends 5% of GDP on healthcare but achieves worse outcomes than lower-income neighbors; the gap between potential (low labor costs, proven models like Aravind) and performance (government dysfunction, poor preventive care) represents India's critical development opportunity.
