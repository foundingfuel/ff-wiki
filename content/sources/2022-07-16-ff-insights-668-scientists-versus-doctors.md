## Core Argument
Constraints force pragmatism and resourcefulness; India's educational crisis requires systemic rebuilding, not temporary bridge measures.

## Key Points
- Medicine's pragmatism—where outcomes are binary—trains better problem-solvers than pure science's theoretical detachment.
- Post-pandemic learning loss in India shows foundational gaps cannot be fixed in three months; structural rebuild needed.
- Resource scarcity breeds innovation: ants trained faster and cheaper than dogs for cancer detection; Indian constraints historically drove ingenuity.

## Open Question
Does resource scarcity genuinely develop better innovators and pragmatists, or does it simply mask systemic failures that wealthy systems don't tolerate?

## India Angle
India-specific challenges: pandemic learning loss in schools, resource-constrained medical education forcing innovation, systemic gaps in foundational literacy and numeracy that require structural rebuilding, not just catch-up measures.
