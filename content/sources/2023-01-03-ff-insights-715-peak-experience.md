## Core Argument
Peak-end rule shapes memory; China's data failures show regulation alone cannot protect citizens without cultural and institutional accountability.

## Key Points
- People remember experiences by peaks and endings, not duration—the 'peak-end rule' shapes perception more than actual length.
- China's extensive data protection laws failed due to systemic vulnerabilities; 1 billion citizens' data exposed despite strict regulations.
- Prestigious scientific figures spread debunked research on Twitter, showing how authority and reach bypass critical scrutiny in information ecosystems.

## Open Question
How can India design data protection regulations that address both technical vulnerabilities and the institutional/cultural gaps that allow laws to fail in practice, as seen in China?

## India Angle
India's withdrawal of the Personal Data Protection Bill creates an opportunity to learn from China's data surveillance failures and design better regulations balancing security, privacy, and freedom before reintroduction.
