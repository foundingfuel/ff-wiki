## Core Argument
AI automation threatens predictable jobs across all sectors; society lacks adequate adaptation mechanisms for mass displacement.

## Key Points
- Machines now perform intellectual tasks and learn—fundamentally different from past technological revolutions replacing muscle power.
- Jobs vulnerable if predictable and data-rich; creative and interpersonal work remain safer but eventually may face automation too.
- Average-skilled workers will struggle finding economic footing; historical job transition patterns won't apply universally this time.

## Open Question
If technological disruption is inevitable and job-retraining cannot keep pace with displacement, what economic and social model prevents mass unemployment from destabilizing society?

## India Angle
India's large workforce in routine, predictable jobs (BPO, manufacturing, data entry) faces acute automation risk. Policy must address reskilling massive populations lacking resources for transition to creative/interpersonal roles. Social safety nets inadequate for jobless futures.
