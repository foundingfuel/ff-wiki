## Core Argument
Nudge theory solves India's behavioral problems, but digital nudges risk becoming tools of corporate manipulation.

## Key Points
- Thaler's libertarian paternalism has proven effective in India—railway deaths dropped using fear triggers and design cues.
- India's digital infrastructure explosion creates opportunity for nudges but also vulnerability to attention-grabbing, profit-driven design.
- Behavioral economics can address India-specific challenges: TB drug adherence, open defecation, risky railway crossing behavior.

## Open Question
How can India harness nudge theory's benefits for public good without surrendering citizen attention and behavior to profit-maximizing tech companies?

## India Angle
India faces specific problems—railway deaths, toilet usage, TB medication adherence—where nudge theory could be applied. With 450M+ internet users and 300M+ smartphones, India is entering the age of digital nudges, but lacks guardrails against exploitative design by tech companies.
