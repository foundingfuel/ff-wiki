## Core Argument
We vastly overestimate our knowledge depth; this has policy implications for job disruption and knowledge democratisation.

## Key Points
- Illusion of knowledge: people exhaust understanding within one or two explanatory questions, unaware of their shallow grasp.
- Second wave job losses shifted from women to urban men, suggesting quality employment erosion and potential income collapse risk.
- Scientific publishers create artificial scarcity of publicly-funded research, throttling innovation and access in developing economies like India.

## Open Question
If policymakers and institutional leaders suffer the same illusion of knowledge as the general population, how can we design effective responses to complex crises like employment disruption or equitable knowledge access?

## India Angle
India-specific: Job loss patterns differ by gender across waves, with second wave hitting urban male employment harder—critical for social safety nets and income policy. Scientific knowledge access is particularly acute for Indian researchers facing expensive paywalls with publicly-funded research.
