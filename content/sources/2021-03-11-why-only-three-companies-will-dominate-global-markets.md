## Core Argument
Three dominant generalists inevitably emerge in mature markets; all others are specialists or fail—shaping strategy, innovation, and antitrust policy.

## Key Points
- Only three generalist companies dominate long-term; specialists and niche players can co-exist but face existential pressure.
- The No. 3 player drives most innovation due to survival pressure; monopoly (one player) or excessive competition (8+) both reduce industry-wide value creation.
- Strategic value lies in procurement economies (60-70% of costs), not manufacturing scale; modern competition is multidimensional—functional, social, affordability, accountability.

## Open Question
If only three generalists can survive long-term, how do entrepreneurs justify building companies knowing most will fail or be forced into unprofitable niche positions—and does conscious capitalism genuinely change this structural reality?

## India Angle
Indian companies across industries must understand whether they're positioned as generalists competing for market dominance or specialists finding niche survival strategies. Consolidation dynamics matter for Indian antitrust policy and startup ecosystem—knowing the Rule of Three helps founders avoid 'suicidal strategies' and guides policy on allowing consolidation.
