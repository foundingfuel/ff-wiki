## Core Argument
India can solve air pollution through indigenous microbial innovation, not imported technology.

## Key Points
- Pusa decomposer uses seven naturally occurring fungi to eliminate crop burning, reducing PM2.5 and enriching soil.
- Solution reduces chemical fertilizer dependence by 25% while delivering uniform field coverage through freely available machines.
- Agricultural research translating into scalable, farmer-friendly implementation with proven feedback loops and measurable pollution reduction.

## Open Question
While Pusa decomposer shows promise in field trials, the article doesn't address adoption barriers—farmer resistance, scaling infrastructure, or timeline to meaningful citywide air quality improvement.

## India Angle
India hosts 21 of world's 30 most polluted cities; Pusa decomposer represents India-led innovation to tackle agricultural emissions without external dependency, reducing fertilizer costs while addressing air quality crisis.
