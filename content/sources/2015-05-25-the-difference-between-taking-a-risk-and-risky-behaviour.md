## Core Argument
Distinguish risk-appetite (necessary for entrepreneurship) from risky behaviour (reckless endangerment of stakeholders).

## Key Points
- Risk-appetite is genetic and acquired trait; risky behaviour ignores consequences for employees, suppliers, investors.
- De-risking through strategy and bite-size steps beats glorifying risk; seek counsel from trusted advisors on significant bets.
- Reputational risk is non-negotiable—once lost, unlike money or material, reputation doesn't easily recover.

## Open Question
How do entrepreneurs maintain the boldness needed to create new paths while building safeguards for stakeholders who depend on them—especially when base camp counsel conflicts with visible opportunity?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs scaling from startup to mature enterprise must balance ambition with stakeholder responsibility—employees seeking stability, suppliers dependent on continuity, investors trusting capital allocation. The cultural context of family businesses and long-term stakeholder relationships makes this distinction particularly relevant.
