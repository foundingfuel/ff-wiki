## Core Argument
Clear writing requires discipline and practice, not talent; it drives action and leadership effectiveness.

## Key Points
- Good writing mirrors natural speech: use short words, sentences, and paragraphs to avoid pretension.
- Never send important communication same day; sleep on it, read aloud, edit ruthlessly with peer review.
- If you need action, abandon writing—go tell the person directly instead of memos.

## Open Question
Ogilvy advocates direct conversation for urgent action, yet remote work culture increasingly relies on written communication—how do these principles apply in asynchronous, distributed teams?

## India Angle
Indian professionals in high-growth sectors often prioritize speed and action over clarity; Ogilvy's principles apply directly to how Indian entrepreneurs and managers can communicate more effectively in remote-first teams.
