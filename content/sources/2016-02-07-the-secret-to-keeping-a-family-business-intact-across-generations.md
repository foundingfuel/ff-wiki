## Core Argument
Family businesses survive generations by separating family governance from business operations through professionalization and long-term planning.

## Key Points
- Generation one's autocratic entrepreneurship must evolve into inclusive, professionalized leadership by generation two to sustain growth.
- Family offices create structural boundaries between emotional family systems and financially-focused business logic, reducing conflict.
- Long-term (100-year) planning frameworks help families navigate generational transitions and embrace rather than resist necessary change.

## Open Question
How can family enterprises maintain the founder's entrepreneurial drive and single-minded vision while becoming inclusive and adaptive enough for subsequent generations to thrive?

## India Angle
Indian family businesses—from Tatas to Reliance to countless mid-market enterprises—face identical generational succession challenges. The family office model and governance structures described are increasingly relevant as Indian enterprises scale and professionalize across generations.
