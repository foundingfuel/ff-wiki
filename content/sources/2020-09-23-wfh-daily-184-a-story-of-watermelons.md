## Core Argument
Short-term profit maximization destroys generational value; India must prioritize long-term institutional thinking.

## Key Points
- Watermelon story shows how one generation's optimization (selling larger crops) degrades subsequent generations' potential by 200 years impact on humans versus 7 years in agriculture
- FinCEN Files reveal Indian entities implicated in suspicious financial transactions, exposing gaps in regulatory oversight and compliance systems
- Consumer research shows purchase decisions driven by cognitive biases in 'messy middle' between trigger and purchase, not just price or quality

## Open Question
If India's regulatory systems fail to prevent financial crimes (FinCEN evidence) and education systems under-invest in quality teaching, how can the nation recover from decades of compounding institutional degradation?

## India Angle
India's institutional capacity and long-term thinking: story critiques how short-term incentives (selling best watermelons now) destroy generational value, paralleling concerns about Indian education quality and financial regulation effectiveness shown in FinCEN Files revelations of Indian entities in suspicious transactions.
