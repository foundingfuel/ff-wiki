## Core Argument
Prejudices form through arbitrary markers that become meaningful through conditioning and developmental exposure.

## Key Points
- Arbitrary markers (clothing, symbols) become associated with values and can drive group identity stronger than original meaning.
- Children learn dichotomies naturally through exposure and language without requiring parental prejudice or ill intent.
- Trust in remote work requires understanding how individuals differently build trust, not monitoring or eliminating vulnerability.

## Open Question
If prejudices form naturally through arbitrary developmental exposure, how can organizations and societies intentionally reshape them without understanding each individual's trust-building mechanisms?

## India Angle
Understanding prejudice formation is crucial for Indian workplaces and society managing diversity, communal tensions, and inclusive hiring; road accident data shows unintended consequences of policy interventions unique to Indian context.
