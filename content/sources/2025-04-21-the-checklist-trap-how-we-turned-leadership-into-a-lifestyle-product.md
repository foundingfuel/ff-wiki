## Core Argument
Leadership development has become a performative industry selling checklists instead of enabling genuine, messy human transformation.

## Key Points
- Measurable ROI obsession obscures quiet, powerful shifts—pauses, questions, blind spots—that actually change leaders.
- Fast-food expectations (instant results, 48-hour programmes) contradict leadership's multi-quarter arc of reflection and invisible growth.
- Dashboards and LMS systems track activity, not development; real growth requires unstructured conversation and space to pause.

## Open Question
How do organisations measure and justify investment in leadership development that deliberately resists quick metrics and short-term evidence, especially under quarterly pressure?

## India Angle
Indian organisations increasingly adopt copy-paste Western leadership frameworks without contextual adaptation; FF's audience of large company leaders and entrepreneurs needs to question whether checklist-driven development serves India's specific organisational maturity and cultural landscape.
