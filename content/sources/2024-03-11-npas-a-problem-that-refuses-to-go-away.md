## Core Argument
NPA crisis persists because systemic solutions clash with industry power and political incentives.

## Key Points
- NPAs have plagued Indian banking since 1991, waxing and waning despite multiple reform attempts and schemes.
- Two NPA types exist: genuine defaults from external shocks and willful defaults by large industries exploiting weak legal processes.
- Gold-plating and siphoning emerged when capital-scarce economy met liberalization, enabling promoter extraction of excess borrowed funds.

## Open Question
Why do repeated legal and regulatory frameworks fail to resolve NPAs when the problem's causes—willful default, weak enforcement, industry bargaining power—are well-documented and understood?

## India Angle
NPAs remain a three-decade structural problem in Indian banking despite repeated policy interventions. The issue reflects deeper governance failures, industry capture, and weak enforcement mechanisms specific to India's financial sector and political economy.
