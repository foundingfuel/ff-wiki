## Core Argument
Rapid vaccine development creates unprecedented allocation challenges: rationing across populations in phases.

## Key Points
- WHO set 50% efficacy bar minimum; 70% preferred, but fastest vaccine ever took 4 years vs. mumps' record
- Vaccine rollout will happen in waves; initial scarcity means deciding who receives limited supply first
- Compressed timelines bypass normal bureaucratic processes, accelerating innovation but raising implementation questions

## Open Question
How do we ethically allocate limited vaccines across populations when supply cannot meet demand globally, and who bears responsibility for those left unprotected in early phases?

## India Angle
India's vaccine manufacturing capacity and distribution infrastructure will be critical for vaccinating 1.4B people; the allocation dilemma—who gets vaccines first—has acute implications for a country with constrained resources and deep inequality.
