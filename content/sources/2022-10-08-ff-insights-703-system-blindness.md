## Core Argument
System blindness causes leaders to solve problems incompletely, triggering worse recurrence when overlooking interconnected dynamics.

## Key Points
- Leaders mistake 'side effects' for failures to understand complex systems; causes and effects are distant in time/space.
- Nagaland's zero women legislators despite low crime rates shows customary law resistance blocks constitutional mandates without cultural change.
- Over-conformity to influences erodes individuality and innovation; societies need critical interrogation of who and what shapes behaviour.

## Open Question
How can policymakers implement systemic change when the very systems they're trying to reform (tribal bodies, energy grids, social norms) actively resist intervention from outside?

## India Angle
India's development challenges—from women's political representation in Nagaland to environmental policy—fail because leaders ignore system dynamics. Tribal customary laws blocking women's participation and coal-dependent energy grids exemplify how local contexts demand systems-aware governance, not top-down mandates alone.
