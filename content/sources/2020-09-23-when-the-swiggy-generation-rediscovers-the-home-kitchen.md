## Core Argument
Restaurant dining culture reshapes Indian millennials' perception of food, dismissing home cooking as ordinary despite cultural and nostalgic value.

## Key Points
- Urban Indian youth perceive home food as boring compared to restaurant dishes due to added flavoring, oil, and butter.
- Travel exposure and globalisation drive generational shift toward exotic cuisines over traditional home-cooked meals.
- Nostalgia-driven foods (grandmother's recipes) retain emotional value but are consumed infrequently, disconnected from everyday dining.

## Open Question
How can Indian food culture preserve its heritage and values when younger generations who have resources to eat at home instead choose restaurants, potentially severing intergenerational knowledge transmission of cooking and family dining traditions?

## India Angle
Indian urban millennials are rejecting home-cooked food despite cultural heritage, preferring restaurant dining and convenience. This reflects a broader pattern of how globalisation, travel, and lifestyle aspirations are reshaping Indian family food culture and domestic practices, with implications for food businesses and cultural transmission.
