## Core Argument
Leaders must balance personal ambition with institutional duty, knowing when to step in and when to step away.

## Key Points
- Nilekaki chose Infosys duty over Aadhaar focus despite personal preference, driven by shareholder responsibility and market confidence needs.
- Founder Murthy's inability to let go and parachuted CEO Sikka's imposed culture both failed—Indian context demands evolved, not imposed, systems.
- Indian tech leaders manage layered complexity: people, systems, government—far harder than Silicon Valley's people-only management paradigm.

## Open Question
Can a leader of Nilekaki's stature effectively stabilize a company while knowing he's already outgrown it and should exit quickly—without becoming another Murthy clinging to legacy?

## India Angle
Indian tech leaders face unique complexity managing people, systems, and government simultaneously—exemplified by Nilekani's Aadhaar and Infosys challenges. Indian companies must balance founder legacy, institutional growth, and stakeholder expectations differently than Silicon Valley peers.
