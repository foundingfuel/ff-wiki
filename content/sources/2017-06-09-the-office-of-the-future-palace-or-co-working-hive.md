## Core Argument
Future workplaces will blend luxury campuses and co-working spaces, driven by cost flexibility and employee identity needs.

## Key Points
- Tech giants build palatial offices as brand statements; co-working doubles annually, reflecting shift from fixed to variable costs.
- Workers increasingly value belonging and identity; traditional loyalty erodes as gig work becomes modular, shifting loyalty to personal networks.
- Young talent gravitates toward downtown urban offices for community, forcing companies to relocate from suburban campuses.

## Open Question
Can co-working spaces genuinely provide the sense of belonging and long-term community that traditional offices offered, or will mass freelancing leave workers isolated despite physical proximity?

## India Angle
Indian tech companies and startups must decide between emulating Silicon Valley 'palace' campuses (Infosys, TCS models) or embracing distributed co-working—particularly relevant as Indian metros see rising freelance/gig workers and real estate costs make sprawling campuses unviable.
