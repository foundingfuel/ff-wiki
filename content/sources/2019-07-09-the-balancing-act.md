## Core Argument
Technology and policy tools can manipulate behaviour, but who decides what's 'right' for others?

## Key Points
- Nudge assumes people are irrational; boost develops their decision-making capacity instead.
- Superhuman's privacy breach shows tech innovation can violate user rights despite good intentions.
- Monopoly's original message—critique of capitalism—was inverted; systems shape values inadvertently.

## Open Question
If behavioural interventions are necessary because people make poor choices, how do we prevent those designing interventions from imposing their own flawed judgments on the populace?

## India Angle
India's Economic Survey now endorses 'nudge' behavioural economics in government programmes; tension between paternalistic design and citizen autonomy is acute in India's regulatory context, especially as tech platforms gain influence.
