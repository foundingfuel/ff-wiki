## Core Argument
Business schools must teach ethics as lived practice, not classroom theory, requiring systemic rethink of what education values and measures.

## Key Points
- Business schools face existential dilemma: they are businesses reluctant to teach ethics unless corporations pay premium for ethical graduates.
- Current management education ignores non-financial impacts like social welfare and environmental degradation, distorting decision-making across sectors.
- New coalitions of social scientists, practitioners, and local governments must co-create inclusive growth models tested locally, not top-down business school curricula.

## Open Question
If business schools are themselves businesses, how can they teach ethics that prioritizes societal needs over shareholder returns without becoming commercially unviable?

## India Angle
Indian business schools must rethink ethics education beyond profit-maximization; examples like Bharat Biotech's vaccine pricing, farmer loan waivers, and local solutions in Sircilla and Maval show India's unique path to inclusive capitalism that contradicts Western MBA doctrine.
