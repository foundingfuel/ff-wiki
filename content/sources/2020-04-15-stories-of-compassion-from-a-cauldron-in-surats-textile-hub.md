## Core Argument
Small business owners become social safety nets when state fails migrant workers during crises.

## Key Points
- Ashish prioritizes worker survival over business continuity, advancing wages and housing during lockdown.
- Informal supply chains dependent on rolling credit leave micro-entrepreneurs cash-strapped yet morally obligated.
- Reverse migration crisis reveals structural vulnerability of India's textile hub reliant on migrant labor without formal protections.

## Open Question
How sustainable is informal employer-led welfare when small business owners themselves lack financial resilience and state support?

## India Angle
Exposes the precarity of India's informal textile sector and migrant worker ecosystem during lockdown—challenges to employer-worker relationships, reverse migration crisis, and informal credit systems that trap small entrepreneurs and workers alike.
