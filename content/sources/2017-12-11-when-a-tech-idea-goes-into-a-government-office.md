## Core Argument
Digital platforms as government infrastructure can empower millions, but only if designed for scale, diversity, and systemic change.

## Key Points
- Tech innovations mostly serve top pyramid; most apps default to English, excluding 90% of India's population.
- Platforms can scale, diversify, and lower transaction costs—but must address India's specific challenges in health, education, finance.
- Digital infrastructure alone fails without addressing structural inequalities and ensuring long-term systemic shifts.

## Open Question
How can government-designed digital platforms balance the democratic potential to empower masses with the privacy and autonomy risks they simultaneously create?

## India Angle
India's development paradox: despite digital infrastructure potential (Aadhaar), persistent inequality in health, education, and finance remains. Technology must address scale (1.2B population), diversity, and systemic structural forces specific to Indian context.
