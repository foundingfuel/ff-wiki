## Core Argument
Successful organisations stop asking questions, creating space for nimble competitors to disrupt them through curiosity.

## Key Points
- Success breeds deliberate ignorance; market leaders ignore low-end disruption because weak players ask fresher questions out of necessity.
- Four barriers to questioning: fear of looking stupid, time scarcity, cultures that discourage dissent, and lack of questioning skills.
- Good questions require psychological safety and time; they open 'known unknowns' and prevent future problems from exploding undetected.

## Open Question
How can large organisations maintain the questioning culture of startups without sacrificing the efficiency and decisiveness that made them successful?

## India Angle
Indian startups and growth-stage companies often replicate success playbooks without questioning changing market conditions; deliberate ignorance about emerging competitors and customer needs at the low end is a specific risk for Indian firms scaling rapidly without staying curious about disruption.
