## Core Argument
Corporate leaders struggle to transition to public life despite skills and intent; family exposure and life-stage readiness matter most.

## Key Points
- Dr Misra's corporate transformation track record—dismantling legacy policies, driving succession, attracting multinational talent—proves mastery through curiosity, not domain expertise.
- Electoral politics defeats even successful technocrats like Nilekani; institutional roles (UIDAI) suit business leaders better than electoral contests.
- Career comfort, status loss, family risk, and cultural skepticism of politics deter capable Indians from public service despite aspirational rhetoric.

## Open Question
If corporate leaders possess the transformation skills India needs, why do cultural barriers and institutional structures make their transition to public life so difficult and often unsuccessful?

## India Angle
Indian corporate leaders transitioning to public/political life face unique barriers—status, money, family risk, and cultural skepticism of politics—making talent drain from business to governance a persistent challenge for India's institutional development.
