## Core Argument
Indian entrepreneurs operate in hostile systems; lasting change requires engaging policy while building world-class software products.

## Key Points
- Indian entrepreneurs face contradictory pressures: consumers demand capitalist efficiency but expect socialist protectionism.
- Policy engagement and ecosystem participation—not avoidance—are necessary to reshape systems from within.
- India Stack and software policy represent watershed moments; execution and narrative-building by entrepreneurs are equally critical.

## Open Question
How can entrepreneurs sustain commitment and innovation while simultaneously wrestling with systemic constraints that are designed neither to support nor fully oppose them?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs face systemic headwinds—contradictory consumer expectations (capitalist efficiency + socialist protectionism), regulatory complexity, and public scrutiny—that test commitment far more than Western counterparts; policy reform and entrepreneurial resilience are both critical.
