## Core Argument
Chinese entrepreneurs demonstrate superior adaptability and innovation through continuous reinvention in harsh environments; India's business houses show organizational inertia.

## Key Points
- Chinese firms innovate through process/practice in hyper-competitive markets lacking infrastructure, regulatory clarity, and brand loyalty.
- Chinese private sector grew from zero (1976) to 75% of GDP; India's large companies concentrate in services, not consumer or tech-intensive sectors.
- China invests 1.5-2% GDP on R&D in strategic sectors; India remains below 1% and relies on imports for critical industries.

## Open Question
Can India's entrepreneurial dynamism in startups translate to large-company transformation and sustained R&D investment without state-directed strategic focus like China's?

## India Angle
India lags China in private sector dynamism, R&D intensity (below 1% vs 1.5-2%), and organizational agility. Indian companies lack presence in tech-intensive and consumer-driven sectors where Chinese firms dominate. Government support for strategic sectors is weaker, and entrepreneurial motivation differs—wealth-driven vs. nationalist pride.
