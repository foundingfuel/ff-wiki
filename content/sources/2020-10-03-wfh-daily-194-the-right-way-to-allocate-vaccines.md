## Core Argument
Fair vaccine allocation requires context-sensitive frameworks balancing health, economic, and social harms across phases.

## Key Points
- WHO's population-proportional approach ignores health system capacity and economic vulnerability differences.
- Fair Priority Model addresses three harm types: direct mortality, health system strain, and economic devastation sequentially.
- Platform cooperatives offer alternative to exploitative gig models through worker ownership and technology sharing.

## Open Question
How do we balance immediate life-saving vaccine distribution with addressing underlying economic inequality without perpetuating aid dependency?

## India Angle
India faces acute vaccine scarcity and distribution challenges across states with varied healthcare capacity and population density; the Fair Priority Model's context-sensitive approach aligns with Ramanujan's Indian thinking—prioritizing urgent harms based on local health systems, economy, and capacity rather than blanket population-proportional rules.
