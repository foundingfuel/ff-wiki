## Core Argument
Quitting decisions shouldn't be taboo; data suggests quitters aren't worse off, challenging cultural bias against transitions.

## Key Points
- 40,000+ people participated in coin-flip study on major life decisions, showing widespread decision paralysis.
- No data proves quitting causes misery; those who quit weren't measurably unhappier at 6-month follow-up.
- Cultural stigma around quitting prevents rational decision-making; reframing it as normal reduces decision anxiety.

## Open Question
If coin flips prove equally valid as rational deliberation for major life decisions, what does this suggest about our ability to make informed choices versus our need for permission to act?

## India Angle
Indian professionals face cultural pressure against quitting due to stigma around job transitions; reframing quitting as a rational decision-making tool challenges traditional career narratives in India's hierarchical workplace culture.
