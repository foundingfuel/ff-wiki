## Core Argument
Welch's numbers-first legacy weakened GE's foundations despite short-term success; culture and people ultimately matter more.

## Key Points
- Shareholder supremacy and forced rankings ('rank and yank') damaged collaboration and long-term innovation capacity.
- Successor Immelt's pivot toward safe environments for people and cross-functional work contradicted Welch's approach but proved necessary for sustainability.
- Context matters: leaders must understand why predecessors succeeded before imitating their playbooks without adaptation.

## Open Question
How do leaders balance short-term financial performance with long-term cultural health and innovation when markets demand quarterly results?

## India Angle
Indian businesses and leaders often emulate Western management models like Welch's without contextualizing them; this serves as a cautionary tale about blind adoption of short-term results culture over long-term capability building and employee welfare.
