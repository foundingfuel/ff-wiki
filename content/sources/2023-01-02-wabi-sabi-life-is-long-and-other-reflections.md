## Core Argument
Meaning and fulfillment come from intentional choice and purpose, not happiness or acquisition.

## Key Points
- Life is long if used meaningfully; we waste time through heedlessness, not scarcity.
- Fulfillment requires sustained effort and meaning-seeking, not fleeting happiness or material possession.
- Wabi Sabi—accepting impermanence and imperfection—counters algorithmic comparison culture and consumerism.

## Open Question
If all lives have meaning and we can choose our attitude toward any circumstance, why do systemic inequalities, access, and privilege still determine whose meaning-seeking is feasible?

## India Angle
Indian professionals and entrepreneurs often chase external validation and material success; Wabi Sabi and Frankl's meaning-making offer an alternative framework for defining success beyond acquisition, directly relevant to India's rapid consumption culture and career pressures.
