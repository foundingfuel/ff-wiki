## Core Argument
Selfie culture is killing formal wear demand; new business models emerge around aspirational personal branding across India.

## Key Points
- Millennials prioritize upper-body apparel for social media visibility, willing to finance trendy purchases via installment loans.
- Formal dress codes are relaxing globally (Goldman Sachs); workforce composition shift (engineers vs. finance professionals) drives workplace culture change.
- New grooming and styling businesses (Jawed Habib) capitalize on Tier II-III millennials' desire for consistent, aspirational personal image.

## Open Question
If the white shirt survives by morphing into casual/aspirational forms rather than disappearing entirely, does this represent true disruption or merely evolution of existing retail categories?

## India Angle
Indian millennials across Tier I-III cities are reshaping retail through selfie culture, driving demand for aspirational upper-wear brands and grooming services while disrupting traditional formal wear markets. New business models like Jawed Habib are capitalizing on middle-India's desire for consistent personal branding.
