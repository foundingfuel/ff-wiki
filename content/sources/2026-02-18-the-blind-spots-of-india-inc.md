## Core Argument
Indian organisations treat disability inclusion as compliance, not capability, limiting talent and leadership imagination.

## Key Points
- Leaders' unspoken hesitations about capability and control shape hiring outcomes more than explicit rejection.
- Even hired employees with disabilities are routinely denied challenging work, exposing systemic integration failure.
- Disability inclusion requires leadership mindset shifts, not just infrastructure—it demands managerial confidence and reimagined definitions of talent.

## Open Question
How can companies reconcile legal compliance with genuine capability-building when the root issue is leadership confidence and risk tolerance, not infrastructure or intent?

## India Angle
Indian companies systematically exclude persons with disabilities despite legal mandates (RPwD Act 2016), revealing a gap between compliance infrastructure and genuine inclusion—a structural problem across Sensex-listed firms that undermines India's talent pool and leadership credibility.
