## Core Argument
Leaders deceive themselves by attributing success or failure to people rather than changing context and industry dynamics.

## Key Points
- The halo effect: same IBM people praised in 1984 blamed for decline by 1992; context changed, not people.
- Good listening requires fighting similarity bias, embracing silence, and attending to non-verbal cues in remote settings.
- Progress against COVID extends beyond vaccines—understanding transmission, super-spreaders, testing, and treatment improves survival rates.

## Open Question
How do leaders distinguish between genuine cultural/people problems and genuine market/contextual shifts that make previously effective practices obsolete?

## India Angle
Indian leaders and managers must avoid the halo effect when evaluating organizational performance and people—especially critical as Indian businesses navigate rapid digital transformation and competitive pressures where yesterday's strengths become tomorrow's liabilities without honest self-assessment.
