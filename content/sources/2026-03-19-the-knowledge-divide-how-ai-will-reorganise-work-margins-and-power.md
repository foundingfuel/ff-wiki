## Core Argument
AI reorganises intelligence architecture; firms must choose between building, renting, or remaining human-centred.

## Key Points
- Three diverging intelligence architectures (agent-first, rented-agent, human-centred) will reshape work, margins, and competitive power.
- Knowledge lock-in creates closed systems where embedded institutional knowledge reduces labour mobility and widens firm divergence.
- Integration and contextualisation become core capabilities for majority of firms unable to build proprietary AI agents internally.

## Open Question
If proprietary AI knowledge becomes embedded infrastructure that firms won't share, how do most organisations avoid permanent disadvantage and avoid creating permanent underclasses of rented-intelligence dependent firms?

## India Angle
Indian enterprises risk falling behind without deliberate choices on AI adoption architecture. Small-to-mid-sized firms may struggle to build agent-first systems, forcing rent-dependent strategies. Policy must incentivise knowledge-sharing ecosystems to prevent concentration of AI power in few global players.
