## Core Argument
Illusion of control blinds us to randomness; recognizing both is essential for better decisions.

## Key Points
- Psychological need for control is vital to well-being but creates systematic bias toward overlooking randomness
- Ellen Langer's nursing home study shows exercised control correlates with better health outcomes and survival rates
- Effective decision-making requires actively seeking disconfirming evidence, not just validating existing beliefs

## Open Question
How do we balance the psychological necessity of feeling in control with the intellectual recognition that randomness dominates outcomes in business and life?

## India Angle
Indian policymakers and business leaders often overestimate their control and underestimate randomness, leading to rigid reform implementation and underestimation of ground-level complexity—as seen in agricultural vs. 1991 reforms debate.
