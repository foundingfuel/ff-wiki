## Core Argument
Shared narratives bind societies; India needs larger stories encompassing multiple perspectives to address polarization.

## Key Points
- Stories reinforce common values and reduce social friction by homogenizing societies around shared culture.
- Aadhaar's success required political patronage and protection from infighting—Pranab Mukherjee's air cover enabled Nandan Nilekali to navigate bureaucratic resistance.
- Government-civil society collaboration requires trust, moving beyond transactional relationships to recognizing nonprofits' proven evidence and expertise.

## Open Question
How can India create a larger unifying narrative when polarization deepens and different groups inhabit entirely different story worlds with no common reference points?

## India Angle
India's Aadhaar programme exemplifies how political will and bureaucratic navigation can enable transformative infrastructure; also highlights need for government-civil society collaboration in Indian social sector reform.
