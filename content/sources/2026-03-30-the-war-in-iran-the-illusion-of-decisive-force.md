## Core Argument
Military superiority alone cannot achieve decisive outcomes; systemic resilience and ideological entrenchment create permanent instability.

## Key Points
- Capability imbalance (US/Israel vs Iran) produces diminishing returns despite overwhelming conventional superiority.
- IRGC institutional embeddedness ensures regime resilience even under degradation; nuclear weaponization remains inevitable without structural dismantling.
- Dual maritime chokepoint disruption (Hormuz + Red Sea) reorders global trade vulnerability and energy security architecture.

## Open Question
How can military pressure force regime structural change when the IRGC is economically and socially embedded, yet leaving it intact guarantees perpetual conflict and nuclear proliferation?

## India Angle
India faces direct exposure through energy security (Hormuz dependency), trade route disruption via Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb, and GCC partnership risks. Indian refineries rely heavily on Iranian oil alternatives; maritime chokepoint disruptions spike shipping costs and inflation. Geopolitical fragmentation in the Middle East destabilizes India's West Asia strategy.
