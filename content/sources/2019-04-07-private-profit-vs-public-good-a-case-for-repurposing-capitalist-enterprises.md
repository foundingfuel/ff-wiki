## Core Argument
Shareholder capitalism prioritizing investor returns over employee and community welfare is morally bankrupt and economically fragile.

## Key Points
- JRD Tata's stakeholder model—aligning national interest with company success—outperformed post-1990s shareholder capitalism ideologically and practically.
- Private sector failures (financial crisis, airline collapses) expose risks of unfettered markets; public sector deserves reconsideration as societal value creator.
- CEO golden parachutes while employees suffer reflects governance immorality; equity must extend beyond investors to all stakeholders bearing company risk.

## Open Question
If stakeholder capitalism is morally and economically superior, why has shareholder capitalism dominated globally for 30 years, and what structural barriers prevent its reversal in India?

## India Angle
Indian companies like Tata historically embodied stakeholder capitalism; recent private airline failures (Jet, Kingfisher) versus Air India's resilience raise questions about whether India should recalibrate its post-1990s shareholder capitalism model toward inclusive value creation.
