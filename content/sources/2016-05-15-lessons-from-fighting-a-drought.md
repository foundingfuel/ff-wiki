## Core Argument
Drought solutions exist but require systemic coordination, policy reform, and treating farmers as partners, not beneficiaries.

## Key Points
- Simple, low-cost interventions like desilting dams deliver measurable results when actually implemented at scale.
- Policy-level coordination and institutional cooperation—not individual behaviour nudges—create transformational water management impact.
- Farmers as active participants, not passive recipients, drive adoption of technology and sustainable crop transitions.

## Open Question
Why do proven, simple solutions like desilting remain unimplemented despite decades of drought crisis, and what prevents policymakers from learning from successful international models?

## India Angle
India's drought crisis affecting 330M people demands systemic policy reforms, institutional coordination, and farmer-centric solutions—lessons from African and Singapore models show what large-scale governance can achieve.
