## Core Argument
Optimal entrepreneurship requires balancing energy and competence, not age; older founders bring human capital VCs undervalue.

## Key Points
- Average US entrepreneur starts company at 40, married with children—contradicting youth-obsessed narratives.
- VCs favor young founders for portfolio risk; older entrepreneurs less dependent on VC capital, bringing industry credibility.
- Human capital—education, training, experience, credibility—matters differently than raw technical ability for sustainable business building.

## Open Question
If competence and credibility correlate with age, why do VC-backed ecosystems structurally disadvantage experienced founders despite higher success potential?

## India Angle
Indian startup ecosystem often mirrors VC preference for young founders; understanding mature entrepreneurship could unlock founders with industry experience, capital, and networks already embedded in India's business structures.
