## Core Argument
Nations succeed by systematically reducing inequality through transparent institutions, not redistributive rhetoric or destructive populism.

## Key Points
- Bad billionaires signal system dysfunction: corruption, complacency, weak rules enable rent-seeking over productive entrepreneurship.
- Destructive revolts (Mugabe, Nyerere) redistribute wealth to cronies, collapse economies; transparency prevents this pattern.
- Effective inequality reduction: fair land laws, live public auctions for spectrum; systems matter more than political promises.

## Open Question
How can democracies implement transparent, merit-based systems when entrenched elites control both political and economic levers?

## India Angle
India faces acute inequality with rising 'bad billionaires' in extractive sectors; institutional weakness enables crony capitalism. Transparent systems (spectrum auctions, land laws) offer policy models to prevent destructive inequality-driven revolts.
