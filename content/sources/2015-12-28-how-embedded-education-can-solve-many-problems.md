## Core Argument
Embed education into existing service encounters to reach disadvantaged populations efficiently without formal education systems.

## Key Points
- Embedded education leverages existing touchpoints (microfinance, healthcare, workplaces) to educate populations lacking time or money for formal learning.
- Success requires creating urgency among stakeholders and combining incentives with moral appeals rather than coercion alone.
- Works best for time-poor populations, low-literate individuals, and those shy about seeking education on sensitive topics.

## Open Question
How to balance service providers' core business incentives with their willingness to embed education without compromising service quality or creating unsustainable operational burden?

## India Angle
India has vast populations with limited access to formal education due to poverty, time constraints, and social barriers. Embedded education through existing touchpoints—microfinance institutions, health clinics, workplace settings—could efficiently reach rural and urban poor for health literacy, financial awareness, and skill development without requiring separate education infrastructure.
