## Core Argument
Leaders must adopt a teenager's mindset: prewired for change, impatient with incremental progress, and willing to disrupt.

## Key Points
- Teenagers spot trends early, act fast, and reject incremental change—leaders must channel this into structured frameworks.
- Standing still is riskier than moving forward; waiting for obvious trends means you're already late.
- The next generation of leaders are curious, brave, and inherently disruptive; legacy leaders must learn to think like them.

## Open Question
How do leaders balance the teenager's reckless, impulsive instincts with the discipline and accountability required in established organizations?

## India Angle
Indian leaders and entrepreneurs must adopt a 'teenager mindset'—prewired for disruption and speed—to navigate rapid market transitions. This is critical as India's digital economy grows and legacy industries face disruption; leaders who wait for obvious trends will miss windows of opportunity.
