## Core Argument
Democracy requires respectful opposition and debate to strengthen, not weaken, institutional scaffolding.

## Key Points
- Vajpayee-Nehru era showed political opponents could debate fiercely yet maintain democratic institutions and personal respect
- Contemporary Indian politics marked by distrust and 'dog whistling' suggests erosion of democratic norms established in 1940s-50s
- Extended mind theory suggests tools and contexts shape cognition, raising questions about how digital ecosystems reshape political discourse

## Open Question
How can democracies maintain robust debate and institutional respect when political polarization and technological amplification of division are structural features of modern politics?

## India Angle
India's current political culture contrasts sharply with Vajpayee-Nehru era of robust debate with mutual respect; raises questions about institutional health and democratic norms in contemporary Indian politics
