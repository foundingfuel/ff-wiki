## Core Argument
Pandemic policymaking requires humility amid uncertainty; trade-offs are unevenly borne across societies.

## Key Points
- Epidemiologists and economists have conflicting incentives and forecasts; lockdowns hit poor countries disproportionately via nutrition loss.
- Vernacularizing medical education risks global competitiveness but could improve access; gradual implementation in paramedical courses is wiser.
- Non-Western nations, including India, resist Western pressure on geopolitical issues, reshaping Cold War dynamics.

## Open Question
How can policymakers balance expert forecasting humility with the need for decisive action during existential crises?

## India Angle
India faces unique pandemic policy trade-offs between health and livelihoods in poorer populations; language policy in medical education raises questions about global competitiveness vs. accessibility; India's geopolitical non-alignment limits Western pressure.
