## Core Argument
Complex systems designed from scratch fail; unintended consequences matter more than intentions.

## Key Points
- Well-intentioned interventions produce paradoxical results when system complexity is underestimated.
- Microcredit's simplicity for institutions masked complexity for borrowers with irregular income.
- Sustainable change requires addressing both upstream sources and downstream efficiency simultaneously.

## Open Question
How do policymakers balance the need for simple, scalable solutions with the reality that complex systems resist simplification?

## India Angle
India's development interventions—from microcredit crises to infrastructure projects—often fail due to ignoring system complexity. Policymakers must recognize unintended consequences in large-scale programs affecting agriculture, finance, and urban development.
