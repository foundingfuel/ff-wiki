## Core Argument
Indian startups die not from bad products but from fundamentally broken unit economics masked by unlimited funding.

## Key Points
- Rapid growth hides broken unit economics; founders often unaware of burn rates, cash runway, or profitability models.
- Hyperlocal delivery, e-commerce, and food-tech startups have unsustainable cost-to-revenue ratios that no amount of funding can fix.
- Startup failures are inevitable and create consumer behavior shifts that benefit future entrants, not current founders.

## Open Question
If startup failures are necessary and noble for market evolution, how should founders and VCs balance learning from inevitable failure versus being reckless with capital and stakeholder livelihoods?

## India Angle
Indian startups are burning cash on unsustainable unit economics while chasing valuations, driven by VCs betting on consumer behavior shifts; this is specific to India's hyperlocal, e-commerce, and food-tech sectors where unit economics fundamentally don't work at scale.
