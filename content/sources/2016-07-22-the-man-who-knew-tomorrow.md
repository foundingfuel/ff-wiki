## Core Argument
Narasimha Rao's reforms were neither inevitable nor ordained; they required rare political skill to navigate India's fractious democracy.

## Key Points
- Four previous PMs faced similar crises and favorable conditions but lacked Rao's ability to reform amid minority government constraints.
- Rao's 'jugaad reformer' instinct—fixing problems incrementally—matured into understanding that reform requires silent execution and political sequencing.
- He assessed opponent weaknesses precisely (license raj beneficiaries, Left intellectuals, unions) and tackled them individually rather than frontally.

## Open Question
If transformational economic reform requires such rare individual leadership skill to navigate India's institutional constraints, how can systemic change be sustained or replicated across administrations?

## India Angle
P.V. Narasimha Rao's 1991 economic liberalization was contingent on individual leadership skill within India's fractious democratic constraints, minority governments, and entrenched vested interests—a uniquely Indian puzzle of reform under political fragility.
