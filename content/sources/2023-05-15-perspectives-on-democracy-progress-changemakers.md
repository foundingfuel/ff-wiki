## Core Argument
Democracy functions through institutional listening structures; India must build pillars of deep dialogue, not just platforms.

## Key Points
- Democracy requires active listening infrastructure—Sweden's participatory surveys prove democratic quality matters more than governance form.
- Democratic bridge needs strong pillars (mutual respect, inquiry, self-awareness) to handle ideological traffic; internet provides span without foundation.
- India's diversity demands fraternity beyond equality—understanding differing histories and beliefs is prerequisite for inclusive economic progress.

## Open Question
If market systems and digital platforms can create efficient spans for dialogue, why do they correlate with declining trust and institutional faith rather than strengthening democracy?

## India Angle
India's extreme diversity across religions, languages, regions demands functional democracy grounded in listening and fraternity. Scandinavian and Costa Rican models offer institutional blueprints for Indian policymakers seeking inclusive economic progress and social cohesion.
