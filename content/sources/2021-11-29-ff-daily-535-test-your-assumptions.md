## Core Argument
Test critical assumptions cheaply and early; systems shape behaviour more than individual character.

## Key Points
- IBM's speech-to-text Wizard of Oz experiment revealed customer friction without expensive prototyping—spend little, learn a lot.
- Rajasthan's camel slaughter ban drove the market underground instead of preserving culture, showing unintended consequences of policy isolation.
- Power and corruption stem from system design, not inherent character—bees and humans behave differently based on institutional incentives.

## Open Question
How do we design policies and products that account for system-level incentives without over-engineering, when real-world feedback often arrives too late?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs and corporate leaders often skip rigorous assumption-testing in pursuit of scale; the camel economics example illustrates how well-intentioned policy without understanding market systems backfires—a recurring pattern in India's governance and business strategy.
