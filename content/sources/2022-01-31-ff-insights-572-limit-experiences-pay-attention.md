## Core Argument
Quality of attention and focused practice matter more than quantity of experiences or hours invested.

## Key Points
- Excess experiences confuse rather than enlighten; limiting input and deepening attention yields wisdom.
- Mastery requires focused, deliberate practice on weaknesses—not volume of hours or breadth of activity.
- India's water crisis stems partly from lack of metered data and political attention, not information overload.

## Open Question
How can decision-makers and leaders cultivate deep attention in systems designed for constant stimulation and rapid scaling?

## India Angle
India's water crisis exemplifies how attention and quality of thought—not quantity of data or experiences—drive policy. Leaders must develop contemplative capacity to discern systemic problems; the crisis shows what happens when we fail to truly pay attention to critical systems.
