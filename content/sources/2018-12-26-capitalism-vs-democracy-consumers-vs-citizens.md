## Core Argument
Capitalism and democracy operate on incompatible moral principles—property rights versus human rights—now surfacing as global conflict.

## Key Points
- Capitalism prioritizes property rights and capital's voice; democracy prioritizes equal human rights and one-person-one-vote.
- Post-Cold War institutions (IMF, WTO, UN) tried to balance both but increasingly favor capital over citizens.
- Global populism reflects citizens' rejection of capital's unchecked power over environment, rights, and national autonomy.

## Open Question
If capitalism and democracy are fundamentally incompatible, can any governance system truly resolve this, or must societies choose one over the other?

## India Angle
India's own strongman leadership model, push for 'Make in India' nationalism, and tension between FDI-driven growth and domestic protectionism reflect this deeper conflict. How India balances capital's demands with citizen welfare in governance will define its trajectory.
