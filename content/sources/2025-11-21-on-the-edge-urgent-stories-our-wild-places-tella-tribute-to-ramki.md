## Core Argument
Conservation photography must shift from documenting beauty to exposing uncomfortable truths about human impact on wildlife.

## Key Points
- Wildlife adapts to human proximity through desperation, not resilience: obesity, habitat loss, behavioral shifts.
- The Ramki Sreenivasan Award pushes photographers beyond aesthetics toward accountability and systemic environmental critique.
- India's conservation crisis is embedded in overlooked rituals and urban infrastructure: idol immersions, zoos, landfills.

## Open Question
How do we balance the need to make conservation visually compelling enough to drive action with the ethical imperative to avoid sensationalizing wildlife suffering?

## India Angle
India's wildlife crisis manifests through specific urban-wildlife conflicts—monkeys in hill stations, leopards in human-dominated landscapes, elephant crop raiding—requiring conservation approaches that acknowledge human-wildlife coexistence as an ethical and ecological urgency unique to India's density and development patterns.
