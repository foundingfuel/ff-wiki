## Core Argument
Iconic leaders' falls reveal that charisma and execution alone cannot sustain trust without ethical foundations.

## Key Points
- Ghosn's transformation of Nissan masked governance failures that eventually shattered his invincible aura.
- GE's decline mirrors Ghosn's—both iconic leaders/companies defined management models that ultimately failed.
- Leadership credibility is fragile; excess and unethical behavior destroy trust faster than accomplishments rebuild it.

## Open Question
How can transformational leaders balance the vision and confidence needed to drive change with the humility and transparency required to maintain legitimacy and prevent ethical drift?

## India Angle
Indian business leaders and organizations can learn from Ghosn's fall—the risks of personality-driven leadership, the importance of governance structures, and how global alliances (like Renault-Nissan in India) depend on trustworthy leadership cultures, not just individual charisma.
