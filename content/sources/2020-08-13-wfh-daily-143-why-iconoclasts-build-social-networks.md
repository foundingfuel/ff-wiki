## Core Argument
Iconoclasts succeed by building social networks that amplify their ideas beyond isolation.

## Key Points
- Picasso's success versus Van Gogh's poverty stemmed from social connectivity, not just talent.
- Digital tools have shrunk the world, making intentional network-building a competitive advantage for entrepreneurs.
- Being a 'connector' and 'persuader'—not just visionary—determines whether radical ideas gain traction.

## Open Question
If digital tools have made geography irrelevant, why do most successful entrepreneurs still cluster in physical hubs, and does remote work finally solve this or create new barriers?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs and professionals often operate within constrained social networks due to geography and institutional barriers; understanding how to build connector relationships—as Picasso did—is crucial for Indian founders seeking visibility and resources beyond traditional gatekeepers.
