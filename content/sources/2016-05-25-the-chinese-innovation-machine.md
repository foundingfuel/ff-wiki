## Core Argument
China evolved from imitation to innovation leadership through customer-driven R&D and speed; India risks disadvantage without similar commitment.

## Key Points
- China invests $162B annually in R&D (2% GDP); India invests <1%, reversing advanced-economy norms
- Chinese companies follow three-stage model: fit-for-purpose, world standards, global leadership through adaptive innovation
- Customer-market orientation and regional variation enable Chinese companies to outcompete MNCs lacking local adaptation

## Open Question
How can Chinese companies maintain innovation momentum without strong internal innovation processes, relying instead on top-down leadership ideas?

## India Angle
India's R&D spending at 0.8-0.9% of GDP lags China's 2%, with inverse government-to-business funding ratios. Indian companies must adopt China's market-driven innovation model and customer orientation to compete globally.
