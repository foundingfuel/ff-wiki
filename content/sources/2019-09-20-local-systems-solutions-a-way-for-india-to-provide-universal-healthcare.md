## Core Argument
Universal healthcare requires local systems solutions with autonomy within consistent frameworks, not centralized control.

## Key Points
- Complex systemic problems like healthcare cannot be solved by top-down command-and-control; local actors must design contextual solutions.
- India spends adequately on healthcare (Rs 70,000 crore in UP alone) but fails on outcomes due to rigid departmental budgeting that stifles local autonomy.
- Thailand's success model: trust local/regional divisions with capitated payments and protocol guidance while maintaining systemic consistency.

## Open Question
How can state governments grant genuine autonomy to local health providers while ensuring systemic consistency and preventing inefficiency or equity gaps across regions?

## India Angle
India's healthcare crisis requires decentralized, locally-responsive systems within state governments rather than top-down central control. States like UP spend substantial amounts (Rs 70,000 crore annually) but achieve poor outcomes due to rigid, centralized management—a distinctly Indian governance problem requiring constitutional empowerment of local actors.
