## Core Argument
Quick commerce solves 10-15% of grocery needs; sustainability requires profitable unit economics, not speed or investor hype.

## Key Points
- Quick commerce works for emergencies and impulse buys (60-90 min), not 10-minute delivery; customer willingness to pay delivery fees is key.
- Unit economics require balance: low last-mile costs, healthy margins, and delivery charges; larger delivery radius (3km) breaks even faster than tight radius.
- VC money drives expansion speed, but the business model only makes sense bundled with other formats; standalone quick commerce is unsustainable.

## Open Question
How can quick commerce operators justify expansion into Tier 2 cities where delivery economics worsen, while VCs and founders pressure for growth that customer demand doesn't support?

## India Angle
Indian quick commerce is being driven by VC money and global trends rather than customer demand; unit economics work only in specific use cases and geographies, making Tier 2 expansion challenging—critical for India's fragmented retail landscape.
