## Core Argument
Paytm's diversified super-app strategy masks fundamental business model failure amid regulatory crackdown.

## Key Points
- UPI's free, public digital infrastructure eliminated Paytm's wallet moat and its key revenue drivers—a strategic blind spot Sharma ignored until rivals captured growth.
- Paytm's services (wallet, payments bank, ecommerce, travel) faced entrenched competitors with superior offerings; no service generated meaningful margins for investors.
- RBI's Payments Bank enforcement action reflects regulatory concerns about compliance and governance, not anti-fintech bias—Paytm's financial struggles predate the directive.

## Open Question
If Paytm's core businesses cannot generate returns even with massive user scale and investor capital, does the fintech super-app model work in India, or does UPI's existence fundamentally prevent profitable digital payment intermediation?

## India Angle
Indian fintech startup's regulatory clash with RBI exposes a deeper question about business model viability in India's digital payments ecosystem shaped by UPI, where free infrastructure undermines margin-based ventures.
