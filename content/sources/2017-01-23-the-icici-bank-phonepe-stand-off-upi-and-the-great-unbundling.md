## Core Argument
Incumbents weaponize regulation to block disruptors; UPI unbundles banking, threatening traditional bank moats.

## Key Points
- Banks blocking fintech apps (PhonePe, Paytm) under security/guideline pretexts mirrors incumbent tactics globally—raising barriers, not competing.
- UPI abstracts away bank identity behind virtual payment addresses, commoditizing the bank-customer relationship core.
- Larger banks most threatened because they lose switching costs; smaller banks more open to UPI because they gain reach without legacy baggage.

## Open Question
If UPI truly democratizes payments and levels the field, why do even forward-thinking large banks feel compelled to block competitors rather than compete on service and innovation?

## India Angle
India's banking incumbents (SBI, ICICI) are using regulatory levers to block fintech challengers (PhonePe, Paytm) from leveraging UPI—a government-backed system meant to democratize payments. This reveals how even infrastructure designed to help all players can become a battleground between old and new India.
