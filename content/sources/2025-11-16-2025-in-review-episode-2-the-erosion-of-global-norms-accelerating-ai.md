## Core Argument
Global institutions, norms, and governance are fragmenting faster than technology races forward, creating systemic instability.

## Key Points
- Western consolidation under US influence erodes multilateralism; EU capitulating on AI regulation and technology sovereignty.
- US-China pragmatic coexistence focuses on tech supremacy, not conflict resolution; transactional short-termism dominates.
- International law weakens as countries prioritize short-term gains; Abraham Accords face grassroots Middle Eastern resistance despite elite adoption.

## Open Question
How can emerging AI governance address superintelligence risks when the international system itself is eroding and short-term incentives dominate decision-making?

## India Angle
India faces geopolitical repositioning as US prioritizes Pakistan through Middle East lens rather than South Asian strategy; India must navigate US-China technological competition, manage AI governance gaps, and adapt to eroding international institutions that shaped post-Cold War regional order.
