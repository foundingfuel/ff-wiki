## Core Argument
Engineering college boom in Odisha was speculative real estate play disguised as education, economically viable only through questionable funding and inadequate regulation.

## Key Points
- Colleges designed as land-value capture and black-money laundering vehicles with minimal teaching investment or faculty standards.
- Boom-bust cycle: 95 colleges opened in decade; 30,000 of 46,000 seats vacant post-2015 when mining sector collapsed.
- Regulatory failure: inadequate teacher ratios (1:30 vs 1:10 norms), low faculty salaries, and brokers replacing genuine student recruitment.

## Open Question
How can India's education regulator prevent such speculative booms without stifling legitimate private sector participation, especially given politics' role in approving colleges?

## India Angle
Odisha's engineering college boom reveals systemic dysfunction in Indian higher education regulation: how mining wealth laundering, weak accreditation oversight, and profit-driven expansion created unsustainable institutions that collapsed when commodity cycles shifted, leaving students and teachers exposed.
