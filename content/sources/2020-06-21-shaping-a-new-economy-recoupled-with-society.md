## Core Argument
Economic paradigm focused on growth and liberalization must shift toward human-centered, decentralized development for resilience and equity.

## Key Points
- India's low employment elasticity of growth created demographic vulnerability despite high GDP growth rates.
- COVID-19 exposed the failure of the trickle-down model, forcing urgent pivot to social security and domestic industry.
- Schumacher's 1968 warning about automation eliminating the poor from production remains prescient and unresolved.

## Open Question
How can India rapidly build resilient domestic industries and universal social security while still competing globally and attracting investment capital?

## India Angle
India's growth model prioritized GDP and trade liberalization over employment creation and social security, leaving 400+ million workers vulnerable—COVID exposed this fragility and forced a policy pivot toward domestic industry, rural development, and universal social security.
