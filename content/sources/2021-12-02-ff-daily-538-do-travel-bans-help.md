## Core Argument
Reactive policy solutions (travel bans, sanctions) often worsen problems by missing root causes and creating perverse incentives.

## Key Points
- Travel bans fail because viruses spread before bans are enacted; bans punish countries reporting transparently.
- Reactive governance creates no-go zones, attracts crime, destroys local economies—worse than original problem.
- Systems thinking reveals unintended consequences: rewards siloed problem-solving, ignores feedback loops and interdependencies.

## Open Question
How can governments design policies that address immediate threats without punishing cooperation, transparency, and creating worse downstream problems than the original crisis?

## India Angle
India faces similar policy challenges—travel bans and border controls that may miss the real problem, plus geopolitical stakes in Balochistan where China's Belt-and-Road ambitions compete with Indian security concerns.
