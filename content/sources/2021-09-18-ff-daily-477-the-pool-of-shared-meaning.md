## Core Argument
Organizations thrive when all voices safely contribute meaning; hierarchies that silence dissent create collectively stupid outcomes.

## Key Points
- Shared meaning pools enable smarter decisions; shallow pools where people fear speaking cause preventable failures.
- Seven hospital staff witnessed error but stayed silent; fear of authority figures suppresses critical information flow.
- Long-term government projects like passports succeed only after initial failures teach organizations to listen and adapt.
- Hierarchical bosses encourage silence; Indian workplaces must build psychological safety to unlock collective intelligence.

## Open Question
How can organizations build cultures of psychological safety when power hierarchies and accountability structures inherently discourage dissent?

## India Angle
Indian organizations across government, healthcare, and IT services struggle with hierarchical cultures that suppress dissent. The Infosys IT portal and hospital safety examples highlight how India's power-distance culture prevents the 'pool of shared meaning' from forming, leading to costly failures in critical projects and services.
