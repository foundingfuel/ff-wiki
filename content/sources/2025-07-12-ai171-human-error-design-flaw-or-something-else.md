## Core Argument
Design flaws, regulatory gaps, and pilot error blur accountability lines in catastrophic failures.

## Key Points
- Fuel switch design allows manual activation without fail-safes; FAA issued non-mandatory SAIB but no mandatory directive.
- Four competing theories—design flaw, pilot error, deliberate action, software fault—remain unresolved, each shifting accountability differently.
- Air India's non-compliance with SAIB advisory and FAA's reluctance to mandate redesign expose systemic governance gaps.

## Open Question
If root cause is design flaw, who bears ultimate responsibility—the designer (Boeing), regulator (FAA), operator (Air India), or pilots compensating for flawed systems—and why did non-mandatory guidance fail to trigger preventive action?

## India Angle
India's aviation regulator (AAIB) and Air India's compliance with international safety standards are under scrutiny; this highlights gaps in how Indian operators implement non-mandatory FAA advisories and the broader question of whether India's aviation safety culture relies on mandatory directives vs. proactive risk management.
