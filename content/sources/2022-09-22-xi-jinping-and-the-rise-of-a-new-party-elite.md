## Core Argument
Xi's third term and new generation leadership signal China's shift toward concentrated authority with global policy implications.

## Key Points
- Xi Jinping breaks post-Mao precedent by securing unprecedented third term and potential CCP Chairman title.
- New sixth-seventh generation leaders have modern education, foreign exposure, and SOE experience unlike predecessors shaped by Cultural Revolution.
- Leadership composition of PBSC, Politburo, and Central Committee reveals factional power equations and China's future domestic and foreign policy priorities.

## Open Question
Will Xi's consolidated personal power signal a break from CCP's collective leadership tradition, and how might new generation leaders' global exposure reshape China's foreign policy stance?

## India Angle
China's internal power consolidation and leadership changes have direct implications for India's foreign policy, regional security dynamics, and strategic positioning in Asia given China's geopolitical ambitions and border concerns.
