## Core Argument
Job loss catalyzed discovery that purpose-driven entrepreneurship beats secure mediocrity in India's economic reality.

## Key Points
- Middle-class Indian parents view entrepreneurship as preserve of select communities; employment is expected cultural norm
- Housing, education, and infrastructure costs make traditional salaried life economically untenable long-term in Indian metros
- Losing power and status revealed authentic relationships exist beyond transactional networking; mentors backed entrepreneurial pivot

## Open Question
If entrepreneurship is necessary escape from economic trap, not genuine choice, does pursuing 'purpose' mask structural inequality rather than solve it?

## India Angle
Indian middle-class cultural norms discourage entrepreneurship outside traditional communities; structural economic pressures (housing, education, infrastructure costs) make traditional employment unsustainable, forcing choice between entrepreneurship and poverty.
