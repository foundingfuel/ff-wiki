## Core Argument
India pragmatically separated boundary claims from ground positions to achieve predictable border stability despite unresolved territorial disputes.

## Key Points
- India rejected the Chinese LAC concept but pragmatically accepted it operationally to reduce military confrontation and enable negotiations.
- 1993 BPTA and 1996 CBM agreements created a jurisdictional framework minimizing unintended conflict while preserving India's territorial claims.
- Clarifying actual military positions through map exchanges became key to managing the boundary without settling competing sovereignty claims.

## Open Question
How can India sustain a framework that accepts ground reality without legal sanction to the Chinese LAC narrative, especially as China's assertiveness and disputed perceptions of the LAC itself shift over time?

## India Angle
India's strategic negotiation with China on border demarcation shaped decades of regional stability; understanding this history is critical for current Indian policymakers navigating renewed tensions and determining future boundary settlement approaches.
