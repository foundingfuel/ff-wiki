## Core Argument
Genius emerges from access to diverse inputs; golden ages cannot be designed, only enabled.

## Key Points
- Tagore's staying power proves genius by synthesis across Buddhism, Sanskrit, English, Sufism, Baul traditions.
- Genius clusters emerge from complex collision of cultures; alter one variable and the magic vanishes.
- Bengal Renaissance created a 'supermarket' of ideas; Tagore was creative consumer, not parochial thinker.
- Golden ages are emergent phenomena, unpredictable and unreplicable through top-down design or policy.

## Open Question
If genius clusters cannot be designed or replicated, what policy or institutional levers can India use to foster creative excellence today without artificially engineering the conditions that made Tagore's era possible?

## India Angle
Tagore as a model for how India's cultural and intellectual resources enable genius through synthesis; also questions whether India can deliberately create conditions for creative clusters in the post-independence era.
