## Core Argument
Economics fundamentally relies on rationality assumption; behavioral economics reveals humans systematically violate it.

## Key Points
- Rationality assumption is essential to economics' predictive power; removing it makes markets unpredictable.
- Behavioral economics emerged slowly through institutional support and accessible communication, not revolutionary revelation.
- Modern behavioral economics still uses rationality as baseline, adding exceptions for documented human biases.

## Open Question
If rationality assumption is abandoned entirely, economics loses its predictive power; yet maintaining it ignores systematic human irrationality—how do we balance explanatory accuracy with practical utility?

## India Angle
India's policy-making and regulatory frameworks increasingly rely on behavioral insights for governance, taxation, and market interventions. Understanding how Indians deviate from rational behavior in financial decisions, savings, and consumption is critical for designing effective policies and institutions.
