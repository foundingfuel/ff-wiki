## Core Argument
Focusing on 'what should be' instead of 'what is' prevents real personal and organizational change.

## Key Points
- De Mello argues surface-level solutions like checklists and ideals fail; underlying emotional reality must be addressed first.
- The priest's 'laziness' was actually depression from unprocessed disappointment and anger about a failed examination.
- Lasting behavior change requires insight into present reality, not imposition of external ideals or willpower.

## Open Question
How do leaders balance acknowledging present emotional/organizational reality with the need to inspire toward future vision and ambition?

## India Angle
Indian professionals and leaders often pursue ideals of productivity and success without addressing underlying emotional barriers—depression, disappointment, unresolved anger—that block real change. De Mello's approach challenges the Indian tendency toward willpower-based solutions and highlights need for psychological awareness in organizational and personal transformation.
