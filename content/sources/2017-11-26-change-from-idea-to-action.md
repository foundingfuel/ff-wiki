## Core Argument
70% of organizational changes fail because leaders underestimate habit's gravity and neglect personal belief-shifting.

## Key Points
- Status quo bias paralyzes even successful organizations; great leaders deliberately unsettle existing pathways to create new possibilities.
- The 'Marathon Effect' explains why employees lag leaders in accepting change; continuous communication and championing across levels are essential.
- Change implementation is boring and personal; leaders must help employees distinguish 'good pain' of transformation from 'bad pain' of market obsolescence.

## Open Question
How can leaders balance the urgency of change with the reality that individuals need time and repeated messaging to rewire beliefs—without the organization losing competitive momentum?

## India Angle
Indian leaders and enterprises must navigate organizational change while contending with the status quo bias prevalent in established companies. Kishore Biyani's Retail 3.0 strategy exemplifies how Indian entrepreneurs can overcome inertia through data, technology, and consumer insight to drive transformation in India's retail ecosystem.
