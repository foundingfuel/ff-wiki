## Core Argument
Measurements are political choices, not neutral truths; when unquestioned, they become weapons that reshape behaviour and reality.

## Key Points
- Numbers encode human choices about what counts; measurement is a moral and political act, not objective reflection of reality.
- Breakthroughs emerge from anomalies and 'beautiful mistakes'—not from eliminating noise but from curiosity about what data refuses to fit.
- Single metrics (GDP, poverty lines, AQI) flatten complex truths; feedback loops between measurement and behaviour create self-fulfilling prophecies.

## Open Question
If measurement is inherently political and distorting, how do we design metrics that inform policy without inevitably reshaping the reality they claim to measure—especially as AI scales these flawed metrics globally?

## India Angle
Critical for India: flawed metrics (poverty lines, GDP, crime statistics, rankings) have historically excluded populations and shaped policy. As India scales AI and digital governance, these inherited measurement biases risk automating exclusion at scale—affecting welfare eligibility, law enforcement, and resource allocation affecting hundreds of millions.
