## Core Argument
Imposter syndrome undermines capable leaders' ability to claim authority and expand their roles; recognising it is essential for organisational impact.

## Key Points
- Imposter syndrome causes high-performers to doubt accomplishments and act tentatively despite proven competence and earned positions.
- Self-limiting beliefs lead to missed opportunities—refusing to engage in strategic decisions, partnerships, or role expansion.
- Cultural and environmental factors (family conservatism, comparison with peers) compound imposter syndrome, particularly for Indian women leaders.

## Open Question
How can organisations distinguish between healthy humility and debilitating self-doubt when identifying and developing high-potential leaders?

## India Angle
Imposter syndrome particularly affects Indian women in male-dominated corporate hierarchies; cultural factors like conservative home environments and comparison culture exacerbate self-doubt, limiting women's leadership potential in Indian organisations.
