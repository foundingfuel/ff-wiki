## Core Argument
Educated women in small-town India face an insurmountable employment gap post-graduation due to family constraints and employer reach.

## Key Points
- India's 21.6% female workforce participation contradicts high literacy investments; GDP could grow 1.5% annually with 50% female participation.
- Critical intervention point is the first year post-graduation when women need institutional support to navigate family expectations and job market access.
- Employers treat women as 'reserve workforce' and rarely scout talent beyond urban centres, leaving qualified women stranded in tier-3 towns.

## Open Question
If education policy succeeds in reaching women in small towns but hiring infrastructure remains urban-centric, does female education without corresponding job access deepen frustration and undermine social progress?

## India Angle
India's low female workforce participation (21.6%) despite rising education levels is a critical economic and social challenge. The gap between education and employment is particularly acute in Tier 2/3 towns and rural areas, where family structures, geography, and employer reach create systemic barriers. This directly impacts India's GDP growth potential and development goals.
