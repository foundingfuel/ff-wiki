## Core Argument
Global elite narratives of progress ignore real lived inequalities; institutional legitimacy requires listening to common people's stories.

## Key Points
- Elite and common Indians live in fundamentally different mental worlds despite geographic proximity, with different information diets and aspirations.
- Core development dogmas—globalisation, technology, innovation—are crumbling because they failed to address deep structural inequalities in the world-system.
- History must be reframed from elite perspectives (dynasties, GDP) to common people's livelihoods and ecological patterns to understand real transformation.

## Open Question
If listening to aam admi is essential for shaping inclusive futures, how do institutions systematically incorporate these voices when structural barriers—education, access, representation—prevent their participation in decision-making?

## India Angle
India's inequality gap revealed through contrasting lived experiences—elite disconnection from aam admi realities threatens coherent nation-building and institutional legitimacy after 70 years of independence.
