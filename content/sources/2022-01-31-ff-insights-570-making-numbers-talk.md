## Core Argument
India's protectionist drift underestimates how small its consuming class is compared to global trade opportunities.

## Key Points
- India increased 3000+ tariffs affecting 70% of imports while exiting RCEP, reversing three decades of gradual opening.
- Only 1-2% of India's 1.4 billion population is middle class, vs 25% in China—insufficient for domestic-powered growth.
- Vietnam and smaller nations capture more from $28 trillion global trade than India's bilateral deals can compensate for.

## Open Question
Can India's large domestic market size justify turning inward, or does its small middle class make export integration strategically necessary?

## India Angle
India faces a critical choice between protectionist domestic-led growth (3000+ tariff increases, RCEP exit) and export-oriented integration. With only 1-2% middle class, domestic consumption alone cannot power $28 trillion global trade opportunity that smaller nations like Vietnam are capturing.
