## Core Argument
Tranquebar's refusal to modernize mirrors a deeper question about India's relationship with its colonial past and local identity.

## Key Points
- Tranquebar, a 400-year-old Danish trading port, remains deliberately underdeveloped unlike nearby Puducherry
- The town hosts India's first Protestant church and Zeigenbalg's still-functional printing press, vital to Tamil Protestant history
- Local residents face an ambivalent choice: preserve authentic heritage or commercialize for tourism like Puducherry

## Open Question
Can Tranquebar preserve its unique ozone-rich atmosphere and tranquility without economic stagnation, or does tourism inevitably destroy the very qualities that make it valuable?

## India Angle
Tamil Nadu's colonial heritage and contemporary identity crisis—Tranquebar represents a choice many small Indian towns face between preservation and commercialization, contrasting with Puducherry's tourist-driven model
