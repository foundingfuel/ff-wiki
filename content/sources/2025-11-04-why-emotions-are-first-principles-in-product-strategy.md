## Core Argument
Product strategy succeeds when built on emotional drivers, not rational user needs or investor hype cycles.

## Key Points
- Users make decisions driven by fear, guilt, pride—not logic. SilverGenie's real customer was guilt-ridden children, not seniors.
- Tech's role shifts from primary feature to ambient reassurance layer. Cameras comfort, not surveil; alerts settle anxiety, not drive engagement.
- Product brief transforms from platform metrics to emotional outcomes: reassurance without friction, presence without monitoring.

## Open Question
How do founders systematically surface and validate emotional drivers without relying on explicit user feedback, which masks the emotions people won't articulate?

## India Angle
Indian startups solving family care across geography must recognize emotional drivers—guilt of diaspora children—over tech-first solutions. Elder care in India's urbanized, geographically dispersed families is fundamentally an emotion management problem.
