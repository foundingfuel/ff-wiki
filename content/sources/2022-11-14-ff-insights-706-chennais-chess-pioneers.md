## Core Argument
Institutional mentorship and grassroots passion create sustained excellence; verify before you trust.

## Key Points
- Manuel Aaron's voluntary chess club mentorship created conditions for India's first Grandmaster, Vishy Anand.
- Chennai's chess culture expanded with each of Anand's achievements, showing how individual success inspires systemic change.
- Scientific fraud case reveals that institutional trust requires active skepticism and verification, not passive respect.

## Open Question
How do we encourage mentorship and institutional trust while building in enough skepticism to catch fraud before it spreads for 16 years?

## India Angle
Chennai's chess ecosystem demonstrates how institutional mentorship and grassroots passion can create world-class talent in India; also relevant to how Indian scientific institutions must build accountability and verification systems to prevent research fraud.
