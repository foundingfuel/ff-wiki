## Core Argument
Genetic engineering may be India's essential tool to secure food amid climate change, despite significant societal resistance.

## Key Points
- Green revolution saved India from famine in 1970s but climate change now erodes those gains by 15-18% this century.
- Mahyco pioneered hybrid seeds in India; now leads GM crop development for drought/heat/salinity tolerance using CRISPR and gene guns.
- Biotech researchers push ahead despite global activist opposition, betting engineered super-crops protect food security for millions of poor.

## Open Question
How can India balance the urgent need for climate-resilient crops through genetic engineering against legitimate public concern about ecological and health risks, especially without strong regulatory consensus?

## India Angle
India faces a critical fork: climate change threatens to reverse green revolution gains (15-18% farm output decline predicted), forcing adoption of genetic engineering to feed 15 million additional people annually—a technology deeply contested domestically and globally.
