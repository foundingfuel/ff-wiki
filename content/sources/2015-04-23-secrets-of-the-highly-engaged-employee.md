## Core Argument
Trust-based culture fulfills unmet psychological needs, dramatically improving engagement and organizational performance at zero cost.

## Key Points
- Maslow's hierarchy shows most Indian enterprises only meet physiological needs, leaving safety, belonging, and esteem unmet.
- Trust triggers oxytocin, enabling social cohesion, cooperation, and discretionary effort that cascade into customer loyalty and margins.
- Building trust requires walking the talk: job security, autonomy, fair systems, kept promises, open communication, and swift accountability.

## Open Question
How do enterprises distinguish between employees genuinely trustworthy versus those exploiting trust—balancing the Russian 'trust but verify' principle without creating surveillance culture that undermines the very trust being built?

## India Angle
India's employee engagement crisis is severe—only 9% engaged vs 60% disengaged—requiring urgent cultural shifts in how Indian enterprises build psychological safety, autonomy, and trust to unlock productivity and reduce attrition.
