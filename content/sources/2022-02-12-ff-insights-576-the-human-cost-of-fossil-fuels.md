## Core Argument
Nuclear power prevents more deaths than fossil fuels; society must choose between catastrophic risk avoidance and probabilistic harm reduction.

## Key Points
- Nuclear power prevented 1.84 million deaths 1971-2009 by replacing coal/gas; phasing it out will likely kill thousands more via pollution and climate effects.
- India's poorest 20% saw 53% income collapse 2015-16 to 2020-21, deepening inequality and constraining ability to absorb energy transition costs.
- Energy policy decisions hinge on risk perception: avoiding maximum hypothetical disaster vs. minimizing statistically likely deaths—a false binary requiring systemic thinking.

## Open Question
How can India pursue climate goals while protecting vulnerable populations from energy poverty and pollution, when global energy policy is driven by wealthy nations' catastrophe-avoidance rather than mortality-reduction logic?

## India Angle
India faces a critical energy policy choice: balancing decarbonization targets with immediate health/pollution impacts and poverty alleviation. Rising inequality (poorest 20% income down 53%) makes energy affordability and air quality core development imperatives, not just climate abstractions.
