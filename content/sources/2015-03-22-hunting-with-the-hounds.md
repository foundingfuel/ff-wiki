## Core Argument
Micromax pivots from hardware commoditization to software differentiation via Cyanogen partnership to avoid Nokia/BlackBerry fate.

## Key Points
- Hardware specs no longer differentiate; Samsung, Micromax hardware now indistinguishable, forcing software-first strategy shift.
- Google's Android One partnership with Micromax failed because OEMs realized they needed control over OS, not just Google services.
- YU/Yureka represents Micromax's strategic bet: custom Cyanogen OS allows brand control and customer lock-in versus pure Android commoditization.

## Open Question
Can an Indian phone brand sustainably differentiate through custom OS when Google and chipset makers (Qualcomm) control the core layers, or is this merely delaying inevitable commoditization?

## India Angle
Indian smartphone makers like Micromax face commoditization pressure and must differentiate through software/services strategy rather than hardware specs to survive in India's hyper-competitive 300M phone market.
