## Core Argument
Social and environmental purpose drives profitability by building stakeholder trust and unlocking competitive advantage.

## Key Points
- Millennials (75% of workforce by 2025) demand purpose-driven companies; conscious capitalism aligns business with this shift.
- Triple Bottom Line approach—economic, social, environmental—maximizes stakeholder value beyond shareholder returns alone.
- Unilever's Lifebuoy case: focusing on hand-washing education over marketing increased sales while improving child health in developing markets.

## Open Question
If conscious capitalism depends on millennial workforce preferences and consumer values, does it remain viable when profit pressures mount, or is it merely aspirational positioning by large corporations?

## India Angle
Indian companies—especially FMCG firms serving mass markets—can adopt conscious capitalism to build brand loyalty among price-sensitive, values-driven consumers while addressing social challenges like health and hygiene that directly impact India's development metrics.
