## Core Argument
WFH collapses home-work boundaries for women, but creates unexpected equity gains if household responsibility shifts materialize.

## Key Points
- Women in WFH struggle with 'dining table moms' syndrome—inability to separate home duties from professional work.
- Generational divide: younger women lose flexibility; older women gain autonomy and new skill exploration opportunities.
- Millennial men stepping up domestically; systemic promotion gaps remain if women reduce hours or visibility.

## Open Question
Can temporary pandemic-driven workplace flexibility policies translate into sustained career advancement for women, or will reduced hours/visibility trap them in lower-performing roles regardless of policy change?

## India Angle
Indian working women face unique gender equity challenges amplified by WFH—domestic help disappearance, traditional family structures, safety concerns limiting office presence, but also newfound opportunities for women in smaller towns and those previously excluded by location/marriage status.
