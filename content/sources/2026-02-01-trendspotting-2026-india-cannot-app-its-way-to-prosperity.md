## Core Argument
India's platform economy delivered convenience, not breakthroughs; a nation cannot IPO its way to sustainable prosperity.

## Key Points
- Digital dividend (payments, delivery, aggregation) solved surface-level friction but cannot substitute for institutional depth, industrial capacity, or productive employment.
- India monetised a hyper-consumptive elite (100M transactors, 1B scrollers) but failed to build bridges to the rest—creating a missing middle and structural inequality.
- Hardening geopolitical order demands real builders and industrial capacity; narrative prowess and convenience layers no longer suffice in competitive global environment.

## Open Question
If digital tools have solved consumer friction but cannot create employment or industrial capacity, what institutional and policy changes are actually needed—and who will build them?

## India Angle
India's tech boom has created a narrow elite convenience layer while failing to build broad-based prosperity, industrial capacity, and a missing middle class—exposing structural gaps as global conditions harden and demand real productive capacity, not just digital narratives.
