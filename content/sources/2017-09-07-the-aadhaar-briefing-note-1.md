## Core Argument
Aadhaar's constitutional viability depends on privacy tests; implementation risks remain unresolved.

## Key Points
- Privacy is a fundamental right but not absolute; petitioners must prove Aadhaar fails legal tests of necessity and proportionality.
- Security vulnerabilities in eKYC sandbox and inorganic seeding of Aadhaar numbers risk financial harm despite consent frameworks.
- Global context matters: surveillance-based business models of tech giants raise questions about India's data protection approach.

## Open Question
How can Aadhaar balance legitimate government authentication needs with genuine privacy protections when implementation gaps (sandbox vulnerabilities, wrongful seeding) persist despite consent mechanisms?

## India Angle
India's Aadhaar programme faces constitutional and practical scrutiny on privacy grounds, with implications for how consent, data security, and mandatory identification are implemented across government services and private sector adoption.
