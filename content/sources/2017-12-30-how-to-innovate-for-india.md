## Core Argument
Global innovators must use design thinking and ethnography to create commercially viable solutions for India's massive underserved populations.

## Key Points
- Global corporations like Medtronic Labs and Khan Academy are building long-term ventures tailored to India's specific healthcare and education challenges.
- Design thinking, ethnography, and lean principles enable creation of low-cost, disruptive solutions adapted to India's complex, fragmented ecosystems.
- India's size and diversity demand localized approaches: multiple languages, fragmented supply chains, mobile-first consumption patterns require deep market understanding.

## Open Question
How can global corporations maintain long-term innovation investments in India when Wall Street short-termism and quarterly result pressures incentivize immediate returns over transformative but slower social impact?

## India Angle
India presents unique innovation opportunities for global corporations willing to rethink business models for underserved segments; social innovation and tech-enabled solutions can address massive gaps in healthcare, education, and agriculture at scale.
