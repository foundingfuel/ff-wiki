## Core Argument
Millets offer India a path to nutritional security, climate resilience, and wellness without requiring pharma—reversing a post-Green Revolution narrative shift.

## Key Points
- Millets are nutrient-dense, climate-resilient crops requiring minimal inputs; lost cultural cachet but now align with health and sustainability trends.
- Switching to millets doesn't require abandoning wheat/rice entirely—gradual blending, sprouting, fermenting integrate them into existing eating patterns.
- Three drivers converge: resource efficiency, climate adaptation, and wellness-through-food movement accelerated by Covid awareness of micronutrients.

## Open Question
How do we scale consumer adoption of millets when decades of post-Green Revolution messaging positioned them as poor people's food, and most Indians lack knowledge of recipes, preparation methods, and taste preferences?

## India Angle
Millets are indigenous to India, lost ground post-Green Revolution, and now represent a path to nutritional security and climate resilience for Indian consumers and agricultural policy; directly addresses India's hidden hunger problem.
