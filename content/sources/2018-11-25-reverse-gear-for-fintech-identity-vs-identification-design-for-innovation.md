## Core Argument
Aadhaar Supreme Court verdict forced Indian fintech into reverse, halting digital transformation and clarifying authentication versus identification design limits.

## Key Points
- SBI's Yono app exemplified digital KYC innovation but had to revert to paper-based processes post-Supreme Court verdict, increasing customer friction.
- Aadhaar designed for authentication (one-to-one matching) not identification (one-to-many search) due to privacy and technological constraints.
- Regulatory uncertainty and election-season politics delay RBI clarity on KYC norms, leaving fintech players and banks in operational limbo.

## Open Question
How can India balance the innovation benefits of digital identity verification with the privacy and technical limitations that make Aadhaar unsuitable for broader identification purposes?

## India Angle
Indian fintech and banking sectors face regulatory whiplash post-Aadhaar Supreme Court verdict, forcing costly reversal from digital KYC to paper-based processes. RBI clarity delayed in election season, stalling innovation and increasing operational costs for Indian banks and telecom companies.
