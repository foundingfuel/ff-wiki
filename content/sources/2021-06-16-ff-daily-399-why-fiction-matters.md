## Core Argument
Fiction uniquely develops moral imagination essential for understanding human complexity and enabling inclusive societies.

## Key Points
- Fiction enables imaginative access to perspectives impossible through nonfiction alone, building empathy.
- Inclusion requires understanding nuance—recognizing common humanity beneath surface differences, as gay rights struggle shows.
- Institutional strength depends on protecting individual dignity from arbitrary power, a lesson embedded in constitutional design.

## Open Question
How do we scale the empathy and understanding fiction builds into institutional practices and policy without reducing complex human realities to data or rules?

## India Angle
Indian society must grapple with fiction's role in building empathy and moral imagination—critical for navigating diversity, inclusion, and bureaucratic accountability in a complex democratic system.
