## Core Argument
AI will reprice value capture, not eliminate tools; India must focus on productivity uplift, not technological prestige.

## Key Points
- Technologies concentrate profit pools upward through ecosystem control, leaving underlying tools as low-margin utilities.
- AI destroys scarcity—the labour arbitrage that powered Indian IT for 30 years is ending; repricing is inevitable.
- India's productivity gaps are so large that a decade of aggressive AI adoption won't exhaust opportunities across core sectors.

## Open Question
If AI productivity gains are so massive for India, why isn't the policy and business establishment mobilizing to capture them at scale—and what structural barriers prevent faster, wider deployment?

## India Angle
India's $250B software services industry faces repricing—not extinction—as AI destroys the labour-cost scarcity that enabled three decades of growth. The real opportunity lies in deploying AI for broad productivity uplift across India's inefficient sectors, not competing on frontier AI models.
