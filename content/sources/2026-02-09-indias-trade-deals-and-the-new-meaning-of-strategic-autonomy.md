## Core Argument
Strategic autonomy now means calibrating alignment with great powers rather than maintaining independence through refusal.

## Key Points
- India accepted US trade negotiation to avoid higher geo-economic costs, signalling willingness to align with Washington priorities.
- Budget 2026 deepens US ties in high-value sectors while opening data-centre tax holidays—benefiting American hyperscalers more than India.
- Energy strategy increasingly entangled with geopolitics; room for manoeuvre narrowing as sanctions, markets, and diplomacy converge.

## Open Question
How can India sustain growth and autonomy when the tools it needs—capital, technology, energy, market access—are controlled by competing great powers with conflicting interests?

## India Angle
India's trade deals with US and EU redefine strategic autonomy from refusal-based to calibration-based approach, forcing choices between market access and geopolitical alignment while managing Russia ties amid sanctions pressure.
