## Core Argument
Reshaping toys, role models, and narratives can rewire girls' relationship with STEM and redefine girlhood itself.

## Key Points
- Gender biases in STEM crystallize by age six; girls internalize perfectionism over bravery, limiting economic innovation.
- Toy design matters: narrative-driven construction toys (Goldieblox) outperform pink-washed alternatives by matching girls' verbal strengths.
- Role models, everyday STEM connections, and supportive environments (all-women spaces) unlock girls' participation in science careers.

## Open Question
How can we scale cultural shift in parental attitudes toward STEM toys and careers when 70% of construction toy users remain male despite product innovations?

## India Angle
India's education system perpetuates gender biases in STEM from childhood; girls self-censor in classrooms due to perfectionism culture, while toy markets (like Smartivity) show 70% male user bias. This directly impacts India's innovation economy and talent pool.
