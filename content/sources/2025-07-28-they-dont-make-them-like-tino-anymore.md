## Core Argument
Tino Puri's quiet integrity and mentorship redefined how Indian talent integrates into global institutions and leadership.

## Key Points
- Tino persuaded McKinsey that Indian professionals could serve global clients, opening doors for generations of Indian talent in consulting.
- He built McKinsey's India practice by betting on local IIM talent rather than expatriates, establishing a model for localizing global excellence.
- His mentoring style emphasized quiet integrity, restraint, and self-awareness over personal promotion—contrasting sharply with Rajat Gupta's later choices.

## Open Question
How do you reconcile Tino's belief in quiet integrity and humility with the reality that succeeding in modern capitalism often requires media savviness and personal brand-building, as Rajat Gupta attempted?

## India Angle
Tino Puri was McKinsey's first Indian hire who fundamentally changed global consulting by proving Indian talent could serve blue-blood clients; he built India's consulting ecosystem by betting on IIM talent, directly shaping Indian corporate leadership across consulting, banking, and executive search.
