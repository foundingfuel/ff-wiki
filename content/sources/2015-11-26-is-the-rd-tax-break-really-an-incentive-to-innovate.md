## Core Argument
R&D tax breaks may not drive innovation; global shocks and market pressures reshape competitive industries.

## Key Points
- Pharmaceutical and auto sectors became globally competitive despite structural shocks, not due to tax incentives
- Startups couldn't use tax breaks lacking profits; established firms used breaks but weren't incentivized by them to innovate
- Policy shocks (1970 process patents, 1991 liberalization) and competition mattered more than fiscal incentives

## Open Question
If R&D tax breaks didn't create India's competitive advantage in pharma and autos, what happens if withdrawn—will companies exit, or will they stay because location is already determined by other factors?

## India Angle
Questions whether India's R&D tax break has been the actual driver of competitiveness in pharma and auto sectors, or merely a post-hoc benefit; relevant as policymakers debate withdrawal of the incentive.
