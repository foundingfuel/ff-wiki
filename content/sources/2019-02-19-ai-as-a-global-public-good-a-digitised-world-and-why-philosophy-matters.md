## Core Argument
AI should be treated as global public good through cooperation, not competitive arms race between nations.

## Key Points
- US-China AI competition frames technology as national security issue; other nations scramble to catch up.
- AI's global impact requires philosophy and ethics frameworks to guide deployment across cultures and contexts.
- India can lead dialogue on AI as shared infrastructure, paralleling space exploration and climate cooperation models.

## Open Question
How can nations pursue legitimate AI capability-building and security interests while simultaneously treating AI as a cooperative global public good without one side exploiting the other's transparency?

## India Angle
India can position itself as a voice for AI as global public good rather than competing in an arms race; leverage existing digital infrastructure like Aadhaar, UPI, and Ekal Step to shape international AI governance norms.
