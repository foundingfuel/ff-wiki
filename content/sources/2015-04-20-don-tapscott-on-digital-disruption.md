## Core Argument
Digital revolution promised democratization but enabled power concentration; institutions must evolve to distribute benefits equitably.

## Key Points
- Promise unfulfilled: mobility and platforms centralized power among tech giants and governments, not distributed it.
- Data as currency: individuals create value via digital crumbs but have no ownership stake or control over monetization.
- Institutional lag: societies lack new frameworks for education, safety nets, monopoly control as they did during Industrial Age transition.

## Open Question
How can societies harness digital innovation's collaborative potential while preventing power concentration and ensuring equitable benefit distribution without stifling disruption?

## India Angle
India faces similar digital disruption challenges—wealth concentration among tech giants, data privacy gaps, platform-driven job precarity (Uber, gig economy), and weak regulatory frameworks. The transition from agrarian to digital economy mirrors India's own development arc, requiring urgent institutional innovation in education, social safety nets, and data governance.
