## Core Argument
Understanding human behavior, not just technology, separates failed innovations from disruptive businesses.

## Key Points
- Simputer was technologically ahead but failed commercially, revealing gaps between innovation and market readiness.
- Kunal Shah's philosophy background enabled people insights that created FreeCharge's competitive advantage.
- Entrepreneurs succeed by focusing on controllable actions iteratively, avoiding overthinking and rabbit holes.

## Open Question
Why do intellectually rigorous, well-intentioned innovations fail in India while unconventional founders from non-technical backgrounds succeed?

## India Angle
Traces India's tech entrepreneurship journey from Simputer's failed promise to FreeCharge's success, highlighting what Indian entrepreneurs and policymakers must learn about market-fit, timing, and human-centered business building.
