## Core Argument
Technology amplifies homophily, eroding democratic consensus and institutional trust faster than ever before.

## Key Points
- Homophily (bonding with similar people) is natural but dangerous when it leads to social atomization and democratic breakdown.
- Social networks algorithmically reinforce homophily for business reasons—targeting advertisers with clustered, similar audiences.
- Divided societies struggle to reach consensus decisions, lose institutional trust, and in extreme cases, resist proximity to political opponents.

## Open Question
How can societies preserve the comfort and efficiency of homophily while preventing the institutional collapse that extreme clustering produces?

## India Angle
India's democratic institutions and social cohesion face similar homophily risks as Western societies, but with added complexity: caste, religion, region, and language create deeper historical clustering. Tech platforms amplifying these divisions could undermine India's plural consensus-building and institutional trust.
