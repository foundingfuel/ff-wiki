## Core Argument
Tragedy teaches CEOs humility, resilience, ethical limits on ambition, and cyclical rather than linear growth.

## Key Points
- Tragedy provides vicarious crucible experiences that build resilience and wisdom without personal ruin.
- Western entrepreneurship's 'grow or die' ethos risks moral collapse; Eastern cyclical growth model prioritizes sustainability.
- Uncontrolled ambition (Macbeth, Satyam, Housing.com) leads to ethical erosion and eventual catastrophic failure.

## Open Question
How can entrepreneurs balance the real market pressure to grow aggressively with the philosophical wisdom that unchecked ambition leads to downfall—especially in India's hyper-growth startup ecosystem?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs often operate in high-growth, investor-driven environments (Housing.com example cited) where Western 'bigger-faster' mindsets dominate; Satyam scandal shows local relevance. Article argues for Eastern philosophical approach—cyclical growth, sustainability, ethics—directly countering dominant startup culture in India.
