## Core Argument
Gati Shakti promises integrated infrastructure planning, but bureaucratic silos and data reluctance may limit transformational impact.

## Key Points
- Gati Shakti uses geospatial data to break silos and identify bottlenecks across 18 ministries involved in infrastructure.
- Systemic barriers—ministry resistance to data sharing and lack of integrated monitoring systems—threaten delivery on paper promises.
- Real-world example: Dedicated Freight Corridor underutilised due to poor inter-ministry coordination, which Gati Shakti aims to fix.

## Open Question
Can top-down mandates and PMO oversight overcome the institutional resistance to transparency and data-sharing that has historically undermined similar initiatives like GSTN?

## India Angle
Gati Shakti is a critical attempt to solve India's infrastructure coordination crisis across 18 ministries, but success depends on bureaucratic culture change and data-sharing willingness—fundamental challenges in Indian governance.
