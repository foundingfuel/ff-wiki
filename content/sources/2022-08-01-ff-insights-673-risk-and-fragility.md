## Core Argument
India's fragile safety nets and competitive disadvantage risk driving capital exodus and perpetuating household vulnerability.

## Key Points
- Absence of health insurance and social security systems forces families to deplete savings during crises, constraining mobility.
- UAE's rapid rise in attracting HNIs through agile visa and immigration policies signals India's regulatory rigidity as a competitive liability.
- Women's constrained economic participation due to societal biases compounds family vulnerability when primary earner faces catastrophe.

## Open Question
If India improves safety nets and governance to retain HNIs, will redistribution of wealth through taxation conflict with attracting private capital inflows?

## India Angle
India faces dual risks: fragility of social safety nets forcing families into financial ruin (as Nooyi's story illustrates), and capital flight as HNIs seek jurisdictions with better infrastructure and governance. Both signal systemic vulnerabilities in state capacity and institutional frameworks that must be addressed for sustained development.
