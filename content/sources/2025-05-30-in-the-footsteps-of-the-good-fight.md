## Core Argument
Witnessing war sites transforms abstract historical narratives into visceral encounters with sacrifice and moral conviction.

## Key Points
- Standing at Normandy cemetery confronts visitors with the human cost of fighting for ideals like freedom and justice.
- WWII's framing as the 'Good Fight' embedded moral and religious dimensions into how democracies justified defending against tyranny.
- Childhood exposure to WWII stories shaped a generation's values; pilgrimage to battlefields deepens understanding beyond film and literature.

## Open Question
If WWII was truly a morally clear 'Good Fight,' why does witnessing its sites evoke sobering ambiguity rather than affirmation of victory?
