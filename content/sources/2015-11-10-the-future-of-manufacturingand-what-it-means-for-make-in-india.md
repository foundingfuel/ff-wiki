## Core Argument
India's Make in India strategy ignores automation's disruption of the manufacturing ladder to prosperity.

## Key Points
- Automation driven by AI, robotics, and falling computing costs is reshaping global manufacturing fundamentally.
- Jobs India targets through FDI are precisely those most vulnerable to automation and offshoring.
- Employment hasn't grown with GDP post-liberalization; automation will worsen this decoupling.
- Even developed economies face jobless growth and wage stagnation despite automation productivity gains.

## Open Question
If automation eliminates the jobs that traditionally enabled developing nations to climb the economic ladder, what alternative pathways exist for India to create mass employment and prosperity?

## India Angle
Make in India assumes traditional labor-intensive manufacturing will drive growth, but automation threatens this strategy before India can scale. India risks competing in a manufacturing model that's becoming obsolete, while employment gains fail to materialize.
