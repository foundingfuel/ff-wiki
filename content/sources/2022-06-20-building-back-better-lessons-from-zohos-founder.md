## Core Argument
Established companies, not VCs, drive long-term innovation; India must build core capabilities to avoid becoming an economy of assemblers.

## Key Points
- Zoho serves 50M employees as bootstrapped company, rivaling Big 4 IT services combined, without VC pressure or public markets.
- Challenge obsession with EPS and 'core competence'—Amazon's retail-to-cloud transformation shows imaginative companies span sectors.
- Deep tech (MRI, 5G, batteries, semiconductors) requires 5-10 year R&D cycles only established companies can fund; VC seeks 3-year exits.

## Open Question
How can India simultaneously encourage VC-driven consumer innovation (which attracts global talent and capital) while protecting space for long-reach R&D projects that build strategic capabilities but lack venture returns?

## India Angle
India needs bootstrapped, R&D-focused companies building core capabilities (semiconductors, cloud, deep tech) rather than copying Western VC-driven consumer models. Smaller cities like Gwalior offer talent and opportunity to build scale without metros' peer pressure.
