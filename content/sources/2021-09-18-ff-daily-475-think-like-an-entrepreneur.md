## Core Argument
Entrepreneurship is both character and capability; understanding what makes people willing to assume entrepreneurial challenge requires examining regulations, education, risk mitigation, and social structures.

## Key Points
- Ewing Kauffman's foundation model: education + entrepreneurship access transforms lives and entire communities through multiplier effects.
- Covid exposed vulnerabilities in Indian education: private schools serving low-income families face bankruptcy; systemic cascade effects on teachers, lenders, student dropouts.
- Holmes trial raises definitional tension: where lies the line between acceptable startup failure (trying hard, iterating) and fraudulent deception of investors and users?

## Open Question
Should entrepreneurial risk-taking be mitigated to encourage broader participation, or heightened to filter out likely failures—and how do we distinguish genuine iteration from deliberate fraud?

## India Angle
Indian private schools face existential crisis post-Covid; entrepreneurship as a vehicle for social mobility and economic transformation is critical for India's underserved populations, especially in rural/semi-urban areas where access to capital and education remain barriers.
