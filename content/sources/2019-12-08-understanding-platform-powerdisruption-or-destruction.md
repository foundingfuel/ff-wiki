## Core Argument
Platforms wield unprecedented market power through capital, network effects, and cross-subsidies, suppressing competition and choices despite appearing pro-consumer.

## Key Points
- Platforms distort markets via VC funding, network effects, and cross-subsidization, suppressing entrants and limiting real consumer choice.
- Most platforms lack profitable core businesses, relying on ancillary revenue streams and inefficient resource allocation despite scale.
- Indian incumbents must undergo deep mindset transformation—digital presence alone insufficient; they must rethink customer experience and embrace competitive multiplicity.

## Open Question
How can regulators intervene to ensure fair competition without picking winners, subsidizing incumbent complacency, or stifling the innovation platforms enable?

## India Angle
India's platform ecosystem differs fundamentally—no incumbent dominance, ongoing market disruption, and unique opportunity for Indian businesses to build capacity to compete on multiple platforms while serving both domestic and global markets. Offline retailers' digital transformation requires mindset change, not just technology adoption.
