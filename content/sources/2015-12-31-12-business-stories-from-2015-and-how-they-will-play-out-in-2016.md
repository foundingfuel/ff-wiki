## Core Argument
Indian businesses must prioritize customer experience and technology-driven efficiency over cost-cutting to compete in digital-first markets.

## Key Points
- Air India's advertising-reality gap reveals cost-cutting undermines brand promise without operational excellence.
- Uber's asset-light, data-centric model with minimal staff outpaces traditional operators like Ola despite vastly different scale.
- Successful strategy requires entering adjacent markets with existing capabilities while maintaining consistent customer experience through technology.

## Open Question
Can Indian legacy companies like Air India and Ola fundamentally restructure their cost-heavy, people-dependent models fast enough to compete with tech-first platforms, or are they structurally locked into decline?

## India Angle
Analyzes Indian companies (Air India, Ola, Flipkart) competing globally and domestically; highlights how Indian firms must adopt tech-enabled, asset-light models to survive against players like Uber in increasingly digital markets.
