## Core Argument
Decentralized ward-based systems outperformed centralized command-and-control in pandemic response.

## Key Points
- BMC replaced single control room with 24 decentralized ward war rooms, each 24/7 staffed with doctors and resources
- Positivity rate fell from 30% to 12% as localized dashboards enabled proactive patient monitoring and hospital transfers
- Jumbo hospitals designed as full public health centers, not just COVID facilities, adding 9,000 beds with multi-sector support

## Open Question
Early success in controlling second wave cases raises questions about sustainability: can decentralized systems maintain effectiveness if resources become scarce or fatigue sets in among staff?

## India Angle
Mumbai's BMC decentralization during COVID-19 second wave offers a case study in how Indian government institutions can adapt operational structures to handle crisis at scale—moving from centralized control rooms to ward-level autonomy.
