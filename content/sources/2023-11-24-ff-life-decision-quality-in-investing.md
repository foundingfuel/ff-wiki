## Core Argument
Decision quality—independent of outcomes—matters more than results in uncertain domains like investing.

## Key Points
- Good decisions can produce bad outcomes; bad decisions can get lucky. Conflating them corrupts judgment.
- Momentum investing appears successful until volatility hits; value investing is boring but structurally superior.
- Leverage and short-term tactical plays corrupt long-term processes; discipline requires resisting immediate opportunities.

## Open Question
If good decision-making processes don't guarantee good outcomes, what actually motivates disciplined investors to maintain them when lucky bad decisions reward recklessness?

## India Angle
Indian retail investors increasingly chase momentum stocks and leverage for quick returns; building decision quality frameworks is critical as markets mature and volatility hits unprepared investors.
