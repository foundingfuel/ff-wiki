## Core Argument
Remote work success requires mutual trust and respect for privacy, mirroring timeless leadership principles.

## Key Points
- Data privacy and communication discretion matter in leadership, even in informal contexts.
- Managers must trust autonomous teams while team members reciprocate by delivering quality work.
- Business education should teach holistic thinking and values alignment, not just specialist skills.

## Open Question
How do organisations maintain accountability and oversight in remote work without eroding the trust-based autonomy that makes WFH effective?

## India Angle
Indian business leaders—from Tata legacy to modern entrepreneurs—must balance transparency with discretion; applies to WFH governance where trust replaces surveillance in Indian organizational contexts.
