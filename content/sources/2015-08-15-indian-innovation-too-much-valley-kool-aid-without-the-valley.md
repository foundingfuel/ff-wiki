## Core Argument
India absorbs Western innovation doctrine uncritically while ignoring homegrown intellectual traditions and frameworks.

## Key Points
- Indian scholars adopt Western models (Christensen, Prahalad) over equally rigorous Indian frameworks without critical examination
- Country-of-origin bias—India devalues its own ideas while overweighting Western intellectual capital due to colonial psychology
- Gap between India's technical talent and actual innovation output suggests systemic cultural barriers, not capability deficits

## Open Question
How can India build confidence in its own innovation frameworks and intellectual traditions without rejecting legitimate global knowledge exchange?

## India Angle
India's innovation ecosystem is constrained by one-directional ideological flow from the West and deep-rooted colonial-era inferiority about Indian intellectual contributions; systemic undervaluation of homegrown frameworks like Anil Gupta's honeybee model versus Western paradigms like Christensen's disruptive innovation.
