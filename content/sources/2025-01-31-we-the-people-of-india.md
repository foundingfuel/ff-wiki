## Core Argument
Citizens must actively practice constitutional values to translate democratic ideals into lived equality and justice across India.

## Key Points
- Universal adult suffrage was radical: India included all citizens from day one, unlike most democracies.
- Constitutional literacy in rural/marginalized communities enables grassroots problem-solving aligned with constitutional values.
- "We, the People" framing shifts power from state to citizens—ownership and collective responsibility become actionable.

## Open Question
How can constitutional values like equality gain traction in contexts where structural inequalities (caste, gender, literacy) remain deeply embedded and limit people's ability to claim their rights?

## India Angle
India's constitutional commitment to universal adult suffrage and equality remains foundational but requires citizens to actively practice and own their constitutional role—especially in rural and marginalized communities where constitutional literacy is low.
