## Core Argument
Speed without direction is futile; velocity—purposeful progress toward a clear goal—separates visionaries from busy noise-makers.

## Key Points
- Speed measures distance per unit time; velocity factors direction—running fast in circles achieves nothing.
- Team Indus lost the race but gained velocity; critics celebrate failure while the team persists toward its moonshot.
- Investors seek founders obsessed with velocity, not speed—disciplined focus on one thing executed relentlessly better than competitors.

## Open Question
How do founders maintain long-term velocity and investor faith when critics, media, and institutional pressures demand immediate visible progress and results?

## India Angle
Team Indus represents India's capacity for moonshot entrepreneurship; the article critiques India's media and governance tendency to fixate on short-term failure rather than long-term velocity, limiting ecosystem support for ambitious ventures.
