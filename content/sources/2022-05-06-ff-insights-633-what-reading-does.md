## Core Argument
Reading and relationships cannot be optimized for short-term gain; they require long-term commitment for genuine fulfillment.

## Key Points
- Deep reading builds intellectual fellowship and maturity that enable better leadership and decision-making over time.
- Optimization mindset, while efficient, can erode human bonds and relationships essential for grounded, fulfilled lives.
- Economic crises expose second-order systemic fragility: Sri Lanka's healthcare collapse stems from import dependency and currency shortage, not medical capacity.

## Open Question
How do we balance pragmatic optimization of resources and time with preserving non-optimizable human and intellectual practices that define a meaningful life?

## India Angle
India's education system emphasizes optimization and material gains; reading as a practice for philosophical grounding is undervalued. Indian healthcare systems, like Sri Lanka's, depend heavily on imports—a systemic vulnerability worth examining.
