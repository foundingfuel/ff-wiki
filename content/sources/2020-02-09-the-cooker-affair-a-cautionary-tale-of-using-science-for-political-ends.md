## Core Argument
Using science for political ends without rigorous validation destroys institutional credibility and stifles innovation for years.

## Key Points
- Nehru-Prasad-backed solar cooker lacked feasibility: expensive (Rs 50), impractical for rural users, no scientific basis.
- Scientists amplified unproven product for publicity; when it failed, scientific community retreated from public accountability.
- Episode set back Indian applied science credibility by a decade, making institutions risk-averse toward real innovation.

## Open Question
Were Bhatnagar and NPL leadership genuinely deceived about the cooker's viability, or knowingly complicit in political theater—and does the answer matter for rebuilding trust in Indian science?

## India Angle
Critical lesson for Indian policymakers and scientific institutions: when state, science, and private sector align around unproven technologies for political optics, credibility and innovation ecosystems suffer for decades. Reflects India's early-independence struggle to balance aspirational tech narratives with ground realities.
