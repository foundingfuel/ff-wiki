## Core Argument
Market cycles repeat when narratives justify absurd valuations; structural inequalities determine outcomes across systems.

## Key Points
- Harshad Mehta popularized 'replacement cost' theory to justify inflated stock prices, causing bubbles that bankrupted most investors
- India's vaccination context differs critically from the West due to lower coverage rates and inadequate health infrastructure
- Venture capital operates like Formula 1—structural disparities (capital access, resources) determine outcomes more than merit alone

## Open Question
How can India's regulatory and institutional frameworks prevent recurrence of market manipulation and irrational valuations when the same narratives keep resurfacing across contexts (stocks, vaccines, VC)?

## India Angle
India's stock market history (Harshad Mehta case) mirrors repeated patterns of irrational valuations; India's lower vaccination coverage and health infrastructure make immunity-source decisions more consequential than in the West; India's VC ecosystem concentrates capital among top-tier firms, limiting opportunities for smaller entrepreneurs
