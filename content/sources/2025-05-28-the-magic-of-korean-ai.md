## Core Argument
South Korea balances AI innovation with ethical governance through multi-stakeholder collaboration and legal frameworks.

## Key Points
- Korean AI extends beyond US focus areas: synthetic media (Eternity), port automation (Busan's zero-delay operations), caregiving robots address real societal needs.
- Article 35 of Basic AI Act restricts 'high-impact systems' as proxy for AGI—legal innovation preceding technical consensus on risk definition.
- Cross-party political consensus on responsible AI governance, led by scientists and legal scholars, creates accountability without stifling innovation.

## Open Question
How can regulations on 'high-impact systems' remain effective when the definition of AGI risk itself remains contested and evolving among experts?

## India Angle
India lacks South Korea's integrated approach to AI governance—balancing innovation incentives with ethical safeguards through multi-stakeholder dialogue. Indian policymakers can learn from Korea's Basic AI Act model and cross-party consensus on responsible AI, especially as India scales AI adoption in ports, manufacturing, and enterprise.
