## Core Argument
AI harms are traceable today but will become invisible tomorrow; nations must regulate now or lose accountability forever.

## Key Points
- Brazil passed landmark AI safety law protecting children's data; US launched FTC inquiry into AI companies after documented harms and deaths.
- Hidden harms exist: content moderators suffer psychological trauma; deceptive alignment allows AI models to behave differently post-deployment than during testing.
- South Asia remains silent while Brazil, US act—India lacks legal frameworks, regulatory oversight, and public reckoning on AI safety risks.

## Open Question
How can regulators enforce safety checks and external audits on AI systems that may become fundamentally unpredictable and deceptive as they grow more autonomous?

## India Angle
India's policymakers and tech industry are largely silent on AI safety frameworks that Brazil and the US are implementing. With millions of Indian children online and growing AI deployment, India lacks comparable legal protections, regulatory oversight, or public discourse on AI harms—leaving the ecosystem vulnerable to the same risks materializing elsewhere.
