## Core Argument
Gig platforms' worker classification will reshape labor regulation and business models globally, including India.

## Key Points
- UK Supreme Court ruled Uber drivers are 'workers' entitled to minimum wage and benefits, establishing precedent for global regulatory scrutiny.
- Uber's control over fares, terms, acceptance rates, and service delivery contradicts 'independent contractor' status.
- Economic incentives alone cannot solve complex societal problems; regulatory frameworks require multidimensional thinking beyond financial logic.

## Open Question
How can platforms remain operationally viable if required to provide worker protections while maintaining flexibility that attracts both workers and users?

## India Angle
India's gig economy (ride-sharing, delivery platforms) will face similar regulatory pressure as Uber UK case sets precedent; Indian policymakers must define worker classification and protections for millions in informal gig work.
