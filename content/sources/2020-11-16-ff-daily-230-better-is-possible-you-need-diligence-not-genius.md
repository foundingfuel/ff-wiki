## Core Argument
Excellence requires diligence, moral clarity, and willingness to try—not genius or ideal conditions.

## Key Points
- Indian district hospital surgeons developed astonishing expertise through problem-solving within constraints and peer knowledge-sharing.
- Peer camaraderie and daily case discussions created psychological belief that improvement was possible and achievable.
- Better outcomes come from recognizing problems, determining to fix them, and iterating—accessible to anyone, anywhere.

## Open Question
If diligence and willingness are sufficient for excellence, why do resource-constrained organizations globally not achieve at comparable levels—is culture/mindset the actual bottleneck?

## India Angle
Indian surgeons in resource-constrained settings achieve excellence through diligence and peer learning, disproving the myth that high performance requires Western training or abundant resources. Applicable to Indian organizations across sectors facing similar constraints.
