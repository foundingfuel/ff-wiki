## Core Argument
Population growth is a solvable logistical challenge; nations that embrace it gain geopolitical and economic advantage.

## Key Points
- America's declining relative power requires tripling population to 1 billion to compete with China and India economically.
- Secondary problems like housing, pollution, and infrastructure are manageable compared to the strategic imperative of population scale.
- Policy levers—immigration, family support, welfare state design—can architect population growth systematically rather than allow demographic drift.

## Open Question
If population growth is so strategically valuable, why do developed nations resist immigration and natalist policies despite recognizing their long-term advantage?

## India Angle
India grapples with managing 1.4 billion people and poverty reduction; this raises the inverse question—whether India's demographic scale is asset or constraint. For Indian policymakers, the piece inverts the typical framing: density as problem becomes density as solution for economic dominance.
