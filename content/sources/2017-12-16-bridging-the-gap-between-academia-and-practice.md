## Core Argument
Business schools must bridge academia-practice gap through practitioner engagement and field-based learning, not just publication incentives.

## Key Points
- Academic research is increasingly disconnected from management practice, published for academics rather than practitioners.
- Incentivizing publication volume without practitioner engagement fails; real learning requires field exposure and live case studies.
- Management consultancies and business leaders face similar rigour-versus-relevance tensions; famous research often lacks empirical foundation.

## Open Question
How can business schools simultaneously maintain academic rigour and ensure practical relevance when incentive systems reward publication quantity over impact?

## India Angle
India's business schools lag in research productivity and practitioner engagement; exemplified by figures like CK Prahalad and Tarun Gupta who bridged gaps but faced institutional resistance. Indian context offers fresh cases for learning, yet academy-practice divide persists.
