## Core Argument
People are assets only if managed as humans, not resources; industrial-era talent practices destroy the trust and innovation entrepreneurial ventures need.

## Key Points
- Bell-curve performance management kills collaboration and risk-taking despite evidence it damages culture and innovation.
- First-generation entrepreneurs spend 30% of time finding talent but lack evidence-based frameworks for managing and retaining them.
- Traditional annual surveys mask reality; transparency tools like Glassdoor and continuous feedback systems reveal authentic workplace culture.

## Open Question
If entrepreneurs intellectually recognize talent's criticality but lack practical people management skills, and existing corporate models are proven toxic, what specific frameworks should entrepreneurial ventures adopt to compete for and retain top talent?

## India Angle
Indian first-generation entrepreneurs instinctively value talent but lack evidence-based people management frameworks; industrial-era HR practices persist in Indian enterprises despite being proven counterproductive, limiting innovation in a competitive global market.
