## Core Argument
Markets have moral limits; not all goods should be commodified or sold, even when financially expedient.

## Key Points
- Sandel's police car advertising case shows how financial necessity can push governments toward compromising institutional integrity.
- Public opposition and moral concerns can override market logic, as evidenced by the campaign that killed the North Carolina scheme.
- Broader tension exists between using markets to solve social problems and preserving non-market values in public institutions.

## Open Question
If governments are cash-strapped and markets offer efficient solutions, what principled framework should determine which public goods remain non-commodified?

## India Angle
India faces similar pressures to commodify public goods and government functions through privatization/advertising. Questions about marketization of state capacity, inequality widening, and moral hazards in governance are directly applicable to Indian municipalities and public institutions under fiscal stress.
