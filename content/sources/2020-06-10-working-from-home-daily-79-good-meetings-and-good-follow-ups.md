## Core Argument
Effective leadership depends less on meetings than on disciplined follow-up documentation and accountability mechanisms.

## Key Points
- Alfred Sloan's success came from immediate written memos summarizing discussions, decisions, and deadlines post-meeting.
- Virtual work environments amplify need for structured follow-ups since informal hallway conversations no longer occur naturally.
- Follow-up quality—clarity of assignments, accountability, deadlines—determines whether meetings translate into organizational action.

## Open Question
How can leaders scale personalized follow-up discipline across large, distributed teams without creating administrative overhead that negates time savings from efficiency?

## India Angle
Indian executives and managers adopting remote-first work need structured follow-up disciplines to maintain decision clarity and accountability across distributed teams.
