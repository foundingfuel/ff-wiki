## Core Argument
Institutional rigidity and tribal psychology drive irrational outcomes; attention becomes currency in broken systems.

## Key Points
- Us-versus-Them bias leads societies to view outgroups as threatening, homogeneous, and morally inferior regardless of evidence.
- Empty flights show how legacy regulation (use-it-or-lose-it slot systems) creates environmental damage when confronted with unprecedented disruption.
- Spotify dominates not through product superiority but by capturing attention in a fragmented attention economy.

## Open Question
If tribal psychology and institutional rigidity are deeply embedded in human nature and systems, how can societies design better outcomes without dismantling the structures that enable coordination?

## India Angle
India faces similar in-group/out-group dynamics in communal and business contexts; empty flight regulations echo India's inefficient slot allocation at congested airports like Delhi and Mumbai; attention economy dynamics mirror challenges for Indian startups competing globally.
