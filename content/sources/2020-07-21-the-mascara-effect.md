## Core Argument
Beauty consumption during crises reflects human dignity, not vanity—a lesson for understanding post-pandemic consumer behaviour.

## Key Points
- Cosmetics sales surge during economic downturns because beauty fulfils psychological needs beyond indulgence.
- Mask-wearing shifts consumer demand from lipsticks to eye cosmetics, forcing Indian manufacturers to adapt product strategies.
- The urge to look good is primal and human, even in extreme hardship—Holocaust survivors prioritised personal appearance as an act of resistance.

## Open Question
If self-presentation through beauty is fundamentally human and necessary for dignity, how do we reconcile it with critiques of vanity and conspicuous consumption in economically stressed societies?

## India Angle
Indian cosmetics manufacturers are shifting focus to eye cosmetics as mask-wearing suppresses lipstick sales; India's consumer behaviour mirrors global patterns seen during crises, revealing deeper insights about household spending priorities.
