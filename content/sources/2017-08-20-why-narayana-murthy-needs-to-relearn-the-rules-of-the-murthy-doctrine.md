## Core Argument
Founder activism without shareholding power risks eroding institutional governance through moral leverage rather than legitimate stakeholder rights.

## Key Points
- Promoter label carries disproportionate brand equity in India, distorting governance norms and shareholder expectations.
- Murthy seeks influence without proportional shareholding, using media and moral authority instead of capital power like Ratan Tata.
- Board independence and fiduciary duty remain poorly understood in Indian corporate culture despite regulatory progress since 2015.

## Open Question
Can a founder legitimately wield governance influence through moral outrage and institutional legacy without corresponding shareholding, or does this undermine the very professional board independence they claim to champion?

## India Angle
Challenges traditional Indian corporate governance norms where promoters hold unchecked power; exposes tension between founder legitimacy and board independence in Indian public companies, a systemic issue unique to India's corporate landscape.
