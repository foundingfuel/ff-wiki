## Core Argument
State-collected data ignoring local knowledge creates well-intentioned but harmful policies that override community autonomy.

## Key Points
- States gather statistical information missing crucial local details, customs, and complexity that matter on ground.
- State power translates flawed perceptions into physical interventions—modernist schemes that stifle autonomy and local knowledge.
- India's marriage age reform, while progressive, shows unintended consequences when top-down policy ignores ground-level realities.

## Open Question
How can states collect necessary data for governance while preserving local autonomy and incorporating ground-level knowledge into policy design?

## India Angle
Indian state institutions collect data on citizens (land, marriage, demographics) that often misses local context and ground reality, leading to flawed policy implementation that harms communities—particularly evident in rural governance and recent marriage age reforms.
