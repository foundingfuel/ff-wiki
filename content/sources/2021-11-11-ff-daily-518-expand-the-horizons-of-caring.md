## Core Argument
Systems thinking requires expanding caring horizons beyond immediate interests to recognize interconnected realities.

## Key Points
- Systems thinking rejects rigid control; learning happens through small steps, monitoring, and course correction.
- Account Aggregator framework prioritizes consent management over data aggregation, reflecting systems-designed architecture.
- Interconnected world demands expanded moral and practical horizons—individual success depends on ecosystem health.

## Open Question
How do organizations and policymakers genuinely expand their caring horizons when immediate incentives still reward narrow self-interest?

## India Angle
India's digital public infrastructure (Aadhaar, UPI, Account Aggregator) demonstrates systems thinking in practice; the consent management framework shows how interconnected systems require expanded moral and practical horizons in policymaking and business operations.
