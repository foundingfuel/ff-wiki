## Core Argument
Organizations thrive when designed around mutual benefit, not zero-sum competition; doing good and self-interest align naturally.

## Key Points
- Best deals and systems are win-win: higher quality, lower costs, better satisfaction for all parties.
- Institutions wrongly organized around competitive scarcity myth; cooperation is our nature, not exception.
- Under uncertainty, teams need fluid plans for 2-3 scenarios to build confidence, not rigid forecasts.

## Open Question
If doing good naturally feels good due to mutual benefit, why do so many institutions persist with competitive, extractive models that benefit only few?

## India Angle
Indian businesses and policymakers must shift from zero-sum, competitive mindsets (common in post-colonial catch-up economy) to win-win frameworks; applies to corporate governance, regulatory design, and how institutions are structured around cooperation rather than extraction.
