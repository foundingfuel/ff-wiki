## Core Argument
Technology disruption requires bridging technologists and policymakers through small pilots, not grand plans.

## Key Points
- X moonshot factory bridges tech and policy by running small-scale community pilots before scaling, reducing unintended consequences.
- Insurance fraud across geographies (China step-counting, India cancer death scheme) reveals system design vulnerabilities when action layers are distant from decision-making.
- DEPA architecture could democratize Indian data ownership like UPI democratized payments, but regulatory uncertainty and implementation messiness remain unresolved.

## Open Question
If personal data is valuable as 'new oil,' will India's regulatory push protect citizens or simply transfer data control from tech giants to large conglomerates like Reliance?

## India Angle
India's emerging data protection framework, fintech ecosystem (UPI/DEPA), and insurance fraud vulnerabilities raise critical questions about who controls personal data and how regulatory uncertainty will shape tech adoption and citizen protection.
