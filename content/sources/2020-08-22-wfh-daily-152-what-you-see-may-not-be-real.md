## Core Argument
Perception shapes reality in remote work; organizations must build cultures acknowledging invisible struggles.

## Key Points
- Psychosomatic symptoms are real pain without physical cause—employees' invisible struggles are legitimate, not attention-seeking.
- CleverTap's four-point WFH policy (flexibility, paid time off, connectivity support, mandatory leave) acknowledges holistic employee needs.
- Big Tech companies face choice: pursue profit maximization or create genuine value; behavior reveals true organizational vision.

## Open Question
How can organizations distinguish between genuine employee needs and performative wellness policies without deepening the trust gap in remote work environments?

## India Angle
Indian companies navigating WFH must address perception vs. reality (somatization of workplace stress), while Indian tech platforms face regulatory scrutiny for ethical lapses; domestic organizations need proactive culture-building policies like CleverTap's model.
