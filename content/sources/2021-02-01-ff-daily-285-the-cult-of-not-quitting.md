## Core Argument
Quitting strategically, not persisting stubbornly, is rational when resources are finite.

## Key Points
- Three biases prevent quitting: cultural narratives of failure, sunk-cost fallacy, and ignoring opportunity costs.
- Concorde fallacy: continuing investments because of past spending, not future potential.
- Opportunity cost—what you sacrifice elsewhere—is harder to measure but often more important than concrete costs.

## Open Question
If quitting is rational, why do high-achieving individuals and cultures universally valorize persistence, and how do we distinguish between quitting too early and knowing when to let go?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs and professionals face intense cultural pressure to 'never give up'—a narrative embedded in business media and startup culture. This article challenges that mindset, relevant for Indian founders deciding between pivoting, persisting, or exiting; and employees trapped in unfulfilling careers due to sunk-cost thinking.
