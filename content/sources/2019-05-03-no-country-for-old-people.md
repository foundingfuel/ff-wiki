## Core Argument
India's rapid digitization excludes older citizens despite prosperity, creating systemic friction and social isolation.

## Key Points
- Digital interfaces designed without consideration for older users create friction in basic tasks like saving contacts or understanding OTP protocols.
- Older citizens face social pressure to adopt smartphones despite discomfort, conflating technology adoption with social status and dignity.
- Policy-driven digitization (Aadhaar, banking systems) assumes universal digital literacy, leaving vulnerable populations stranded without human alternatives.

## Open Question
How can India achieve financial inclusion and digital transformation without abandoning populations unable or unwilling to adapt, especially when policy mandates remove human intermediaries that previously bridged the gap?

## India Angle
India's digital transformation and financial inclusion policies (Aadhaar, OTP mandates) are leaving behind older citizens in even prosperous, literate states like Kerala, creating a digital divide that threatens social cohesion and institutional access.
