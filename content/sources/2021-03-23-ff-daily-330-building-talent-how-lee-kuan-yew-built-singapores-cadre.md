## Core Argument
Identifying and channelling talent to leadership is crucial for governance transformation, but homogeneity risks stifling diverse perspectives.

## Key Points
- Lee Kuan Yew built Singapore's success by systematically identifying 'talent' and funneling elite students into government through PSC scholarships.
- The system worked institutionally but created unintended homogeneity—elites from same schools, class, background reinforced groupthink and narrow worldviews.
- Building talent requires mechanisms for identification and development, but also safeguards against insularity and echo chambers in leadership cadres.

## Open Question
How can governments build meritocratic talent pipelines without creating self-reinforcing elites who lack diverse perspectives and lived experiences?

## India Angle
India's civil service and political leadership face similar talent identification and development challenges; Lee Kuan Yew's model offers lessons for building institutional meritocracy while avoiding echo chambers that plague Indian bureaucracy and governance.
