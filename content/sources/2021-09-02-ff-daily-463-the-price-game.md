## Core Argument
Price competition creates short-term gains but unsustainable margins, trapping businesses in destructive downward spirals.

## Key Points
- Price-based competition is addictive: easy entry but hard exit as consumers expect lower prices perpetually.
- Margin compression forces volume growth, which paradoxically requires further price cuts—a self-defeating cycle.
- Walmart exemplifies the exception: scale enables low prices, but at reputation and ethical costs from cost-cutting pressures.

## Open Question
If price competition is economically destructive, why do so many retailers—especially in India—default to it despite understanding the long-term damage?

## India Angle
Indian retail and e-commerce increasingly compete on price; this dynamic is particularly acute in India's discount-driven market where brands struggle to build premium positioning and consumer loyalty beyond price points.
