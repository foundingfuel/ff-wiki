## Core Argument
Elder care requires systemic innovation: professional services, family adaptation, and enabling autonomy rather than overprotection.

## Key Points
- Professional home care agencies (Care Continuum model) bridge gap between distant children and aging parents' autonomy.
- Elder isolation in nuclear/mobile families demands proactive engagement: tech literacy, hobbies, community participation.
- Overprotection paradoxically harms elders mentally; care must preserve dignity and enable new learning at any age.

## Open Question
How do we balance protecting vulnerable elders from COVID and isolation while preserving the autonomy and purpose that gives their lives meaning?

## India Angle
India's aging population faces acute challenges: distant adult children, fragmented elder care infrastructure, language/cultural barriers in new cities, and limited institutionalized care services outside metros. The article highlights solutions emerging from Indian entrepreneurs (Care Continuum) and family-driven adaptation.
