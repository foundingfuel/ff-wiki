## Core Argument
Building focused work capacity through structured micro-intervals trains mental endurance more effectively than unbroken sprints.

## Key Points
- Start with 5-minute focused intervals, 1-minute breaks; build endurance gradually via tiny increments, not dramatic jumps.
- Framework prioritizes consistency and habit formation over intensity; treat focus like marathon training, not sprinting.
- Physical breaks from sitting are non-negotiable; email batching at hour-end reduces reactive interruptions throughout day.

## Open Question
The article prescribes a five-minute framework as universally applicable, yet simultaneously admits it won't work for everyone and requires customization—so when does the rule apply, and when does personalization override it?

## India Angle
Indian knowledge workers, often managing high workload and context-switching across hierarchical organizations, need practical productivity frameworks that fit their work culture rather than importing Western productivity ideologies wholesale.
