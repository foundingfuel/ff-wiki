## Core Argument
Reforming colonial-era mental hospitals through structural and systemic change can reintegrate severely mentally ill people into society.

## Key Points
- India's mental health infrastructure is broken: understaffed, inequitably distributed, and shrouded in human rights violations.
- Community-based mental health policy (DMHP) failed due to weak primary care fabric; hospitals still essential but need complete reform.
- Udaan programme redesigns hospitals across four domains: physical spaces, processes, staff upskilling, and reintegration pathways.

## Open Question
How can hospital-based reform scale across India's underfunded mental health system when the DMHP community model has already failed to reach two-thirds of districts?

## India Angle
India's mental health crisis affects 10 million people with severe mental disorders, yet only 25% access care. The country has 0.3 psychiatrists per lakh people vs global average of 2. Colonial-era mental hospitals remain dysfunctional; community-based DMHP operates in only one-third of intended districts. This is uniquely an Indian institutional and capacity challenge.
