## Core Argument
Technology enables new possibilities; humans decide outcomes. Intent and design matter more than the tool itself.

## Key Points
- Technological determinism is a fallacy—fax, telegraph, printing press were tools shaped by human use, not destiny.
- Workspace redesign requires thinking in bundles: companies must consciously choose which functions return to offices post-pandemic.
- Digital education scaled without addressing infrastructure widens inequality; bottom-up innovation (reading rooms, loudspeakers) needs systemic support.

## Open Question
If technology is neutral but possesses affordances that shape behavior, how much responsibility lies with designers versus users to ensure equitable outcomes?

## India Angle
India faces acute digital divide in education—poor students lack devices/connectivity; village-level innovations (loudspeakers in Jharkhand) show grassroots solutions, but require government funding and scaling. WFH debate similarly skews toward privileged workers with home setups.
