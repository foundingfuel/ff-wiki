## Core Argument
Complex urban problems need local systems solutions addressing interlinked governance, capacity, representation, and transparency—not silver bullets.

## Key Points
- Top-down centralized programs fail because systemic problems manifest differently across localities and require local agency voice.
- Garbage example shows four interlocked dimensions: planning/design, capacities/resources, political representation incentives, transparency/participation.
- Capacity-building and empowerment of local actors requires systems thinking frameworks applied to practical, everyday problems iteratively.

## Open Question
How can central governments and institutions genuinely devolve power and trust local agents to solve problems when institutional incentives, political corruption, and historical failures create justified skepticism about local capacity?

## India Angle
Indian cities face systemic urban challenges (garbage, water, pollution) that require localized systems solutions rather than top-down central programs; India's constitutional devolution of governance provides a framework for this, but implementation gaps persist due to capacity, political incentives, and participation deficits.
