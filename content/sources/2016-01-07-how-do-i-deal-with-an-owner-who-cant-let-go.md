## Core Argument
Owner-founder withdrawal from operational control rarely succeeds; professional CEO must exit gracefully rather than reform the owner.

## Key Points
- Founders struggle to transition away from operational control; withdrawal symptoms typically emerge within 18 months of hiring professional CEOs.
- The professional CEO's role is to execute, not reform the owner or facilitate his personal transformation—these are separate challenges.
- Exit strategically with dignity, crediting team success rather than positioning as turnaround hero, preserving reputation for future opportunities.

## Open Question
How can boards or governance structures better identify and address founder-founder-CEO conflicts before they require executive departure, given that good intentions often precede failure?

## India Angle
Reflects the Indian context of owner-managed companies and family businesses struggling with professional management transitions—a widespread governance challenge in Indian enterprises.
