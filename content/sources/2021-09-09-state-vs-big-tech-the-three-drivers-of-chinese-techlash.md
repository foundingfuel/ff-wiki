## Core Argument
China's shift from tech encouragement to strict regulation stems from political, economic, and social imperatives, signalling a global realignment between states and Big Tech.

## Key Points
- Chinese state reversed from 'mass innovation' policies to aggressive antitrust, data security, and monopoly regulation targeting BAT and others.
- Chinese tech lost $1 trillion in market value (10% of stocks) in ten months; Tencent alone dropped $411 billion since January 2021.
- Three regulatory drivers: political centralization, economic control of tech-led growth, and social concerns about 'capitalist excesses' and youth protection.

## Open Question
If heavy regulation stifles China's tech competitiveness globally and pushes companies to expand abroad, how can the state achieve both technological dominance and political control simultaneously?

## India Angle
India faces similar Big Tech regulation pressures globally; understanding China's approach to antitrust, data security, and controlling 'capitalist excesses' is critical for Indian policymakers designing its own tech regulation framework amid growing state-tech tensions.
