## Core Argument
Poverty robs individuals of fundamental freedoms, rendering economic class essential to understanding violence and human capability in India.

## Key Points
- Kader Mia's forced choice between starvation and communal risk reveals poverty's power to eliminate agency.
- Communal violence in India is fundamentally a class problem, not merely a religious one.
- Development must be measured by freedoms gained, not just income growth or GDP metrics.

## Open Question
If poverty is the root cause of both communal violence and inequality, can economic growth alone restore freedoms, or does inequality require active redistribution and social protection to prevent recurring crises?

## India Angle
Amartya Sen's framework of development-as-freedom is foundational to understanding India's persistent inequality, communal violence, and class-based vulnerabilities. The article illustrates how poverty strips Indians of agency—even the freedom to avoid life-threatening risk—a critical lens for Indian policymakers designing welfare and social protection systems.
