## Core Argument
Moonlighting reflects a fairness shift in employment: from ownership to access, from 24/7 availability to flexible time use.

## Key Points
- Employment contracts have become transactional and one-sided, creating unfairness around availability expectations.
- Moonlighting signals whether psychological contracts exist—indicating engagement gaps, burnout, or passion-driven growth.
- Gig work necessity differs by class: survival for unorganised workers, competitive earnings and innovation for knowledge workers.

## Open Question
How do organisations balance employee autonomy and fair time-use with legitimate business confidentiality and focus concerns—especially when burnout stems from primary work, not moonlighting?

## India Angle
Indian IT companies resist moonlighting while facing wage stagnation at entry levels; gig work necessity in unorganised sector post-pandemic; employment contracts remain one-sided, reflecting broader fairness gaps in Indian labour practices.
