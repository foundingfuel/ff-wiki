## Core Argument
Indian policy thinkers must ground decisions in real-world observation and human networks, not just data analysis.

## Key Points
- Data scarcity in India makes qualitative research and field experience essential for effective policymaking.
- Learning requires going to 'the real place, looking at real things, talking to real people'—a principle proven by Honda and Sumant Moolgaokar.
- Preemptive data release and public funding datasets in machine-readable form can progressively improve India's policy infrastructure.

## Open Question
How can India build scalable, reproducible policy frameworks when success depends on irreplaceable individual field experience accumulated over years?

## India Angle
Indian policymakers lack comprehensive data infrastructure; success in policy work requires deep field experience, human networks, and direct engagement with ground realities—a distinctly Indian constraint that demands a different approach than data-rich Western contexts.
