## Core Argument
Business leaders must climb a responsibility ladder beyond profits—from shareholders to society's political health and human rights.

## Key Points
- Five-rung ladder: investors → employees/customers → environment → public welfare → political/democratic health of society.
- India's 2% CSR law treats responsibility as tax, not genuine accountability for harm caused by business operations.
- Leaders claiming to inspire employee purpose must examine their own: shareholder value or societal betterment?

## Open Question
If businesses have rights as citizens (property, legal protection, ability to sue), do they have corresponding duties to society—and can they fulfill these within frameworks designed to maximize shareholder returns?

## India Angle
India's CSR law (2% spending mandate) misses the deeper question of how businesses create harm through operations; real responsibility requires Indian leaders to navigate divisiveness, communal tensions, and societal damage caused by their own business models.
