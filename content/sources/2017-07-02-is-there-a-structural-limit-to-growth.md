## Core Argument
Businesses hit structural growth limits when counterbalancing forces emerge; data control demands may constrain tech optionality.

## Key Points
- Virtuous cycles eventually hit balancing forces—supply delays, competition, or systemic constraints that slow growth.
- Tech firms are valued for optionality (expansion into adjacent markets) but this depends on customer data exploitation.
- User demand for data control could create a structural limit to how fast businesses can scale and innovate.

## Open Question
If customers and citizens demand greater control over personal data collection and use, will that fundamentally limit the ability of tech firms to pursue optionality strategies that investors currently reward?

## India Angle
India faces dual structural limits: digital economy growth constrained by data privacy demands, and jobless growth despite GDP expansion. Aadhaar/India Stack digitisation creates systemic tensions between government data control and individual privacy rights that will shape how Indian businesses can leverage optionality.
