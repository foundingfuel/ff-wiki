## Core Argument
Chinese firms see India as the next growth frontier but face complex market dynamics, stiff competition, and political headwinds.

## Key Points
- Chinese smartphone brands captured over 50% of Q1 2017 mobile sales; Xiaomi, Vivo, Oppo collectively hold 34% market share versus Samsung's 28%.
- India's demographic dividend—young workforce entering labour market annually—contrasts sharply with China's aging, shrinking population post-one-child policy.
- Modi's Make in India and FDI liberalisation attracted 300+ Chinese companies, but success requires navigating regulatory complexity, local competition, and geopolitical sensitivities.

## Open Question
How can Chinese companies balance aggressive market capture (evident in cricket sponsorships and brand visibility) with building trust among Indian consumers who harbour deep-rooted quality and geopolitical concerns?

## India Angle
India's young demographic (median age 31 vs China's 43 by 2030) and growing consumer market make it the next battleground for Chinese companies seeking growth as China's own market saturates. Modi's pro-business policies have opened entry barriers, but Chinese firms face intense competition and political sensitivities unique to India.
