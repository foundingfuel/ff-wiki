## Core Argument
Remote work success depends on manager capability and trust culture, not technology or office design alone.

## Key Points
- Office design must match work type and worker demographics; one-size solutions fail across generations
- Remote work productivity hinges on managerial trust and skill, not surveillance or presence-based commitment signals
- Virtual teams need periodic face-to-face contact to build cohesion; technology cannot fully replace in-person interaction

## Open Question
Most workers want flexibility to choose when/where they work, yet many organisations and managers fear remote work signals lack of commitment—creating a trust gap that technology alone cannot bridge.

## India Angle
Indian companies navigating hybrid work post-pandemic need culturally-aligned management skills; concerns about commitment and trust in remote work reflect hierarchical management traditions that must shift for flexible work to succeed
