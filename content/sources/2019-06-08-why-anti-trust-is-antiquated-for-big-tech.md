## Core Argument
Antitrust laws are obsolete for Big Tech; better frameworks must balance privacy, competition, and free speech without stifling innovation.

## Key Points
- Breaking up aggregators like Facebook won't solve monopoly problems—separated parts would reconsolidate in networked economies.
- Aggregators control consumer access, manipulate behaviour through data, and extract disproportionate profits from dependent vendors.
- India risks poorly-designed regulation (like Australia's post-Christchurch law) that curbs free speech and entrepreneurship without protecting citizens.

## Open Question
How can India regulate Big Tech aggregators to protect vendors and consumers from exploitation without choking innovation or enabling authoritarian control over speech?

## India Angle
India must avoid both EU's innovation-stifling protectionism and China's authoritarianism; local aggregators (Swiggy, Zomato, Paytm) already exhibit problematic behaviours that ill-conceived regulation could worsen without solving core issues.
