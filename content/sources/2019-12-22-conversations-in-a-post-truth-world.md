## Core Argument
Post-truth dominates public discourse; deliberate, diverse conversations and critical consumption are essential antidotes.

## Key Points
- Emotional appeals and personal belief now outweigh objective facts in shaping public opinion globally.
- Passive consumption of social media content amplifies misinformation; active curation and diverse perspectives are necessary counterbalances.
- Institutions must facilitate curated conversations and critical literacy to help citizens navigate information ecosystems.

## Open Question
How can institutions scale curated, high-quality conversations when the economics of social media reward emotional engagement and passive consumption?

## India Angle
India's nascent digital public sphere is particularly vulnerable to post-truth dynamics given high social media penetration, low digital literacy, and polarized political discourse. Regulatory frameworks like the Personal Data Protection Bill must address misinformation while protecting free speech—a uniquely acute challenge for Indian institutions.
