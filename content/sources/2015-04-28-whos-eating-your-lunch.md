## Core Argument
Incumbents fail to disrupt themselves because short-term market pressure erodes conviction to cannibalize profitable legacy businesses.

## Key Points
- Leaders intellectually understand digital disruption but lack courage to fund it over legacy revenue streams
- Stock market analyst obsession with quarterly results paralyzes strategic bet-making on emerging platforms
- Hotstar's success proves consumers adopt digital faster than incumbents predict; waiting invites startup competition

## Open Question
How can public company leaders balance shareholder/analyst pressure for short-term results with the long-term investment required to disrupt their own business before competitors do?

## India Angle
Indian incumbents—banks, TV networks, software services firms—face existential threat from digital disruption but are constrained by stock market pressure, legacy business models, and risk-averse leadership; only those with conviction (like TCS's Chandrasekaran initially, Star's Hotstar pivot) can self-disrupt before startups eat their lunch.
