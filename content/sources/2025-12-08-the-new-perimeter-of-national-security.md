## Core Argument
National security now sits at the boardroom table; corporate strategy must integrate geopolitical resilience alongside growth.

## Key Points
- Digital infrastructure (payments, logistics, data) is strategic assets vulnerable to state-sponsored exploitation, not neutral business tools.
- Chinese civil-military fusion creates structural asymmetries; Indian firms must choose between speed-via-dependency or resilience-via-localization.
- India's 85% dependence on foreign-owned ad platforms and cyberattack frequency (2,146/week) expose gatekeeper risks executives have ignored.

## Open Question
How can Indian companies achieve competitive speed and scale while building the localized, sovereign supply chains that national security now demands—without accepting permanent cost and innovation disadvantages?

## India Angle
Indian corporations must reconceptualize strategy beyond markets to include geopolitical resilience, data sovereignty, and supply-chain autonomy—especially given asymmetries with China and dependence on foreign digital infrastructure.
