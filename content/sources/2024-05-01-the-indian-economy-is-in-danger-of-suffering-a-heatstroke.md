## Core Argument
Extreme heat threatens India's growth trajectory; companies must redesign operations, energy models, and supply chains.

## Key Points
- Heat stress could cost India 4.5% GDP ($250B) by 2030, affecting 81% informal workforce concentrated in vulnerable sectors.
- Companies must adopt adjusted work hours, automation, renewable energy, and captive generation to survive rising operational costs.
- Energy demand peaks stress coal imports and LNG volatility; efficiency standards and cool-roofing could reduce peak demand by 15GW.

## Open Question
How can India simultaneously pursue growth ambitions and labour-intensive development while making existential investments in heat adaptation that may accelerate automation and reduce job creation?

## India Angle
Rising heat stress threatens India's $250B annual GDP loss potential, undermines 3rd-largest economy ambitions by 2030, and disproportionately affects 81% informal workforce—requiring systemic economic restructuring across supply chains, energy systems, and labour practices.
