## Core Argument
Founders defending organizational values through power plays paradoxically undermine the very values they claim to protect.

## Key Points
- Murthy conflates legitimate governance concerns with demands for control, blinded by values absolutism to his own power play.
- Echo chambers and ideological conviction prevent self-awareness; values become weapons to legitimize personal will imposition.
- Negotiation and trade-offs, not ideological purity, preserve trust and institutional health in complex organizations.

## Open Question
Can founders genuinely uphold institutional values after relinquishing control, or does values advocacy inherently mask a struggle for continued influence?

## India Angle
Indian business leaders—particularly founders—struggle to transition from autocratic control to institutional governance; Murthy's paradox reflects broader tensions in Indian corporate culture between founder vision and board independence.
