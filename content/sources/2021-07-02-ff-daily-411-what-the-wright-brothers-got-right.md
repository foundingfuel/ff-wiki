## Core Argument
Problem reframing, not resources or credentials, determines innovation success.

## Key Points
- Wright brothers spent $1,000 vs Langley's $1.4M; succeeded by questioning assumptions about balance, not just propulsion.
- Humble background with high school dropouts outperformed credentialed professor with government backing through different framing.
- Bicycle experience taught Wright brothers to ask 'what critical factors are we missing?' rather than 'how do we execute faster?'

## Open Question
If resource constraints force better thinking, do well-funded teams systematically underperform by relying on scale rather than innovation?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs and innovators often face resource constraints similar to Wright brothers; success comes from reframing problems and asking different questions rather than just having more capital or credentials—relevant for India's startup ecosystem competing globally.
