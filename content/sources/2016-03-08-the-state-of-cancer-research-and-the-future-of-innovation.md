## Core Argument
Cancer research needs theoretical breakthroughs and economic model innovation, not just incremental drug development.

## Key Points
- India treats only 15% of cancer patients annually; survival rates lag despite excellent hospitals due to access and cost barriers.
- Pharmaceutical economics incentivize marginal improvements at huge costs; $300B spent on drugs yielding only 2.2 months median longevity gain.
- Paradigm shifts like TOFT challenge dominant SMT, suggesting theoretical foundations matter as much as data-driven pattern hunting.

## Open Question
How can cancer research break free from incremental commercial innovation when fundamental theoretical breakthroughs remain elusive and public funding subsidizes private profits?

## India Angle
India's cancer care crisis: 850,000 patients annually untreated despite world-class hospitals like TMC; 12% breast cancer survival vs 80% in Finland reveals systemic access and affordability gaps that demand innovation beyond drugs.
