## Core Argument
Long-term business success requires actualizing goodness, not performing it; ROI on ethical action is personal and systemic.

## Key Points
- Being perceived as good is economically rational; CEO misconduct erodes company value by 10-15%.
- Distinguishing aspiration from ambition determines responsible credit extension and value creation.
- Personal ROI on goodness—self-respect and alignment with values—overrides transactional calculations.

## Open Question
If goodness is ultimately economically rational, does that reduce it to another form of self-interest, or can business ethics transcend calculation altogether?

## India Angle
Indian businesses and leaders must navigate the tension between aspirational credit-driven growth and ethical responsibility; crucial for emerging markets where credit expansion fuels both opportunity and moral hazard.
