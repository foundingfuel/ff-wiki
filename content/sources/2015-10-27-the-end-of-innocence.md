## Core Argument
Greed is systemic across Indian institutions; post-liberalization inequality rationalizes corruption as normal practice.

## Key Points
- Corruption operates at four distinct levels: political, corporate, bureaucratic, and managerial, each with different drivers and justifications.
- Government servants and middle-class managers rationalize corruption by witnessing wealth gaps post-liberalization that their salaries cannot bridge.
- Corporate fraud is now industry-normalized: VAS operators demand under-table payments as standard practice, indicating systemic acceptance.

## Open Question
If greed is universal and inevitable across all societies and income levels, what structural changes—beyond moral exhortation or punishment—can actually reduce it?

## India Angle
India's governance challenge: greed transcends sectors and hierarchies, from politicians to government servants to corporate managers. The post-liberalization wealth gap has created rationalization for corruption across socioeconomic strata, threatening institutional trust.
