## Core Argument
Entrepreneurial restlessness requires discipline and selective focus; saying no is harder but more valuable than saying yes.

## Key Points
- Constant ideation without execution creates noise without progress; prioritization and focus outperform perpetual pivoting.
- The ability to say no—to external ideas and internal impulses—separates great entrepreneurs from mediocre ones.
- Channeling restlessness productively requires knowing what to exclude, mirroring how great photography works through omission.

## Open Question
How do entrepreneurs maintain the 'don't settle' hunger that drives innovation while simultaneously developing the discipline to say no and stay focused on one vision?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs, particularly in startup ecosystems, often exhibit high restlessness and rapid pivoting; this article offers pragmatic guidance on disciplined execution—critical for India's scaling challenges where execution beats ideation.
