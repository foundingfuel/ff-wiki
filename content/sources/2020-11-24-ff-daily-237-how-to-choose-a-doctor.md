## Core Argument
Patient outcomes improve when doctors communicate openly, explain options, and respect patient autonomy over credentials alone.

## Key Points
- Knowledge asymmetry between expert and layperson demands transparency, explanation, and willingness to dialogue.
- Paternalistic models dominate India; deliberative relationships incorporating values and preferences yield better care outcomes.
- Similar qualifications masked by communication style: willingness to explain matters as much as technical skill.

## Open Question
How can professionals balance expertise-based authority with stakeholder autonomy when the layperson cannot fully evaluate technical decisions?

## India Angle
India's predominantly paternalistic doctor-patient relationship reflects broader power asymmetries in hierarchical institutions; deliberative models require cultural shift in how professionals engage stakeholders.
