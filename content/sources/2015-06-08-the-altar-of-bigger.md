## Core Argument
Bigger is not inherently better; optimal size depends on context, form, and human experience.

## Key Points
- Square-Cube law shows size changes form fundamentally, not linearly; scaling requires structural transformation.
- Human-scale metrics (happiness, quality, intimacy) don't follow growth curves; Delhi isn't 'better' than Singapore.
- Different businesses have different optimal scales; excellence at one exceptional restaurant outweighs mediocrity at scale.

## Open Question
If certain businesses genuinely require gigantic scale to exist (automobiles, solar panels), how do we distinguish between growth driven by necessity versus growth driven by unchecked ego and venture capital incentives?

## India Angle
Applies to Indian entrepreneurs and businesses obsessed with scaling at any cost; challenges the venture capital-driven mindset that dominates Indian startup culture; relevant to how Indian companies measure success and growth
