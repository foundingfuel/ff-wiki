## Core Argument
India's broken public health system and political apathy allow preventable encephalitis deaths despite documented protocols and neighbouring nations' success.

## Key Points
- India promised increased public health spending over 10 years ago but remains at ~1% GDP allocation.
- Sri Lanka and Nepal have drastically reduced Japanese Encephalitis through sustained government health programmes.
- Only 42% of vulnerable children in Gorakhpur received immunisation despite established clinical protocols since 1978.

## Open Question
How should we reconcile the author's provocative claim that poor children who die from encephalitis may be 'better off dead' with advocacy for a functional public health system?

## India Angle
India's public health system failure to deliver on a decade-old promise to increase healthcare spending, allowing preventable diseases like Japanese Encephalitis to kill children in eastern Uttar Pradesh while wealthier nations have eradicated it.
