## Core Argument
Indian media faces structural decline because readers won't pay for news, enabling politically-motivated misinformation to replace quality journalism.

## Key Points
- Indian print media depended on newsstand sales and advertisers rather than subscriptions, creating incentives for sensationalism.
- Social media narratives weaponizing victimhood and hatred outcompete substantive journalism in influencing Indian audiences.
- Digital transition failed in India because newspapers were given away free, making paid subscriptions culturally unviable.

## Open Question
If quality journalism becomes economically unviable and social media fills the gap with politically-motivated lies, who holds power accountable and how does India maintain informed democratic discourse?

## India Angle
Indian media's transition from print to digital is hampered by free-news culture and advertiser-driven models, unlike Western counterparts. Social media's role in spreading political narratives of victimhood and hatred poses unique challenges to India's information ecosystem.
