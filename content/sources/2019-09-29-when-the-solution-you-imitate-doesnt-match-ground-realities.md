## Core Argument
Premature policy imitation from the West fails in India due to misaligned state capacity and ground realities.

## Key Points
- Indian elites copy Western policies without considering India's lower enforcement capacity and development stage
- Solutions must match context—driving a Ferrari on potholed Bengaluru roads exemplifies how good ideas fail in wrong contexts
- Both product and policy innovators need bifocal vision: telescope for future aspirations, microscope for present ground realities

## Open Question
How can policymakers balance adopting aspirational global standards with acknowledging India's current enforcement capacity without becoming complacent about development?

## India Angle
Indian policymakers and elites blindly imitate Western regulations without accounting for India's lower state capacity and ground realities—plastic bag bans when basic infrastructure fails. This creates laws that cannot be enforced and solutions misaligned with development stage.
