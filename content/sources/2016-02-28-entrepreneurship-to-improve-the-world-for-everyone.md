## Core Argument
Social entrepreneurs should catalyze people's agency to improve their own lives, not accumulate wealth themselves.

## Key Points
- Social enterprises measure success by impact on people's lives, not founder wealth or organisational size.
- Catalysts release constraints on people's agency; solutions must come from people themselves, not imposed by experts.
- Scaling social impact requires people-centric design and listening first, not replicating corporate growth models.

## Open Question
How can social enterprises scale impact rapidly when their success is measured by community empowerment rather than organisational size—the opposite metric of traditional business scaling?

## India Angle
India's social enterprise sector must scale impact on bottom-of-pyramid populations faster; distinguish itself from wealth-creation entrepreneurship by measuring success through lives improved, not capital accumulated.
