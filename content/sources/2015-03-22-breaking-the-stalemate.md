## Core Argument
India's regulatory framework stifles entrepreneurship; shifting from jail-logic rules to trust-based systems is essential.

## Key Points
- Socialist India distrusted enterprise; 1991 reforms failed to dismantle restrictive regulatory legacy built over decades.
- Oligopolistic tendencies and state-industry-bureaucrat linkages force entrepreneurs into workarounds or capture regulators.
- Regulatory agencies incompetent and fragmented; NSEL exploited gaps while appearing in plain sight of multiple regulators.

## Open Question
How can government balance enabling enterprise through trust-based regulation while preventing fraud and protecting citizens without reverting to restrictive control mechanisms?

## India Angle
India's post-liberalization entrepreneurship constrained by legacy regulatory architecture built for control, not enterprise; the NSEL collapse exemplifies how uncoordinated regulation, political patronage, and regulatory capture undermine legitimate innovation.
