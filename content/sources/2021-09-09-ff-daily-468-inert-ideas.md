## Core Argument
Ideas without discernible impact are inert; what matters is leaving actionable footprints in the world.

## Key Points
- Dennett's inert ideas concept: facts with no observable consequences remain unknowable and irrelevant regardless of logical truth.
- Keshav Desiraju transformed abstract health policy into the Mental Healthcare Act—embedding ideas in institutions, people, and systems.
- Xi Jinping's worldview shaped by 1989 events drives current Chinese strategy; geopolitical analysis requires understanding decision-makers' formative contexts.

## Open Question
How do we distinguish between ideas that are genuinely inert versus ideas whose impact is simply delayed or hard to measure?

## India Angle
Indian policymakers and civil servants like Keshav Desiraju exemplify how principled leadership translates abstract ideas into concrete social impact—the Mental Healthcare Act shows how individual integrity shapes national policy; also relevant to understanding how India interprets geopolitical events through its own lens versus Western frameworks.
