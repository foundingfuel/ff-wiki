## Core Argument
India's distance from radical technological innovation stems from capital market myopia, not entrepreneurial inability or talent shortage.

## Key Points
- Indian companies have thrived via incremental innovation and tech licensing, but face vulnerability as competitors withhold technology access.
- Capital markets undervalue IP and R&D, discouraging firms from investing in indigenous technological capabilities despite competitive necessity.
- India's low IP filing rates and reverse-engineering paradigm contrast with Japan, Korea, and China's deliberate transitions to innovation-led growth.

## Open Question
If reverse engineering was a normal early-stage development path for successful economies, why hasn't India's maturing market and competitive pressures naturally triggered the shift toward indigenous innovation that other nations made?

## India Angle
India's innovation gap is structural—capital markets don't value IP, competition hasn't forced technological investment, and companies have relied on reverse engineering rather than building indigenous capabilities. Unlike Japan, Korea, and China, India hasn't transitioned from imitation to innovation despite 25 years of economic liberalization.
