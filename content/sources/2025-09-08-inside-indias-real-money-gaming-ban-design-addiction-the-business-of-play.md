## Core Argument
India's gaming ban exposes the tension between skill-based product design and addictive gambling mechanics embedded in culture.

## Key Points
- Fantasy gaming exploited psychological hooks—competition, community, validation—that made skill-based play highly addictive despite no explicit gambling framing.
- Policy vacuum (1867 Act, skill-vs-chance legal grey zones) allowed rapid expansion until aligned state governments enforced a sudden, drastic shutdown.
- IPL's 2008 launch and UPI's 2016 rollout catalyzed mainstream adoption, embedding gaming in India's sporting culture from metros to small towns.

## Open Question
How should regulators distinguish between legitimately skill-based competition (like FPL) and predatory real-money games using identical psychological mechanics, especially when both drive engagement through identical emotional levers?

## India Angle
India's ban on real-money gaming reflects a critical policy inflection—forgoing Rs 20,000 crore in GST to address addiction and social harm. This reveals how Indian states prioritize social welfare over revenue, and how regulatory uncertainty destroys business models overnight, affecting millions of users and investors.
