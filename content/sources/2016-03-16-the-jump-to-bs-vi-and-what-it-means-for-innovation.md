## Core Argument
Regulatory leaps like BS-VI drive innovation across entire automotive ecosystems, but mask deeper structural challenges in transportation.

## Key Points
- BS-VI mandates post-engine chemical processing, requiring new skills and threatening traditional service models
- Simultaneous nationwide fuel transition differs from phased rollouts, demanding ecosystem-wide coordination
- IC engines persist despite advances; volume growth threatens roads regardless of emission improvements

## Open Question
If technology solves emissions but vehicle volumes triple by 2050, do we fix the symptom while ignoring the disease of unsustainable transportation systems?

## India Angle
BS-VI regulatory leap forces Indian automotive OEMs and suppliers to innovate rapidly in emission control, affecting the entire ecosystem from fuel producers to neighborhood mechanics. India's R&D capability in auto sector is tested, but structural challenges (universal fuel transition, service infrastructure gaps) remain.
