## Core Argument
Acknowledging emotional difficulty is legitimate; leaders improve by returning to craft and staying connected to ground realities.

## Key Points
- Anxiety and worry in volatile times are rational responses, not personal failures requiring shame.
- Leaders lose credibility when disconnected from practitioner realities; returning to craft rebuilds authentic leadership.
- Emotional acknowledgment is a starting point, not an endpoint—requires moving toward constructive action.

## Open Question
How do leaders balance acknowledging legitimate emotional distress with the responsibility to project confidence and direction to teams who need stability?

## India Angle
Indian leaders in high-pressure, volatile environments (startups, policy, large corporations) face acute anxiety and must move beyond toxic positivity culture; reframing emotional authenticity as professional strength is especially critical for Indian workplaces where hierarchies discourage vulnerability.
