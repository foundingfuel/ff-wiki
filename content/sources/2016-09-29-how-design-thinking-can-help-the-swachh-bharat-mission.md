## Core Argument
Infrastructure alone won't fix India's waste crisis; design thinking and behavioral change targeting cultural beliefs are essential.

## Key Points
- Indians hold deep cultural beliefs about cleanliness linked to ritual purity, personal ownership, and shame around manual cleaning work.
- Reframing waste as economically valuable and sanitation work as non-derogatory can shift belief systems and drive behavioral adoption.
- Design thinking's multi-stakeholder perspective can identify intervention points beyond infrastructure to address India's collective action problem.

## Open Question
How can design thinking-driven behavioral change overcome centuries-old caste and class hierarchies that stigmatize sanitation work without addressing systemic inequality?

## India Angle
India's Swachh Bharat Mission requires reframing cleanliness through Indian cultural beliefs about purity, ownership, and shame—not just infrastructure. Design thinking must address deep-seated behavioral barriers rooted in caste, class, and scarcity mindsets unique to Indian society.
