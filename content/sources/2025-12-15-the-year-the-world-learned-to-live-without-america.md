## Core Argument
US withdrawal from global leadership creates a multipolar world where old systems persist but lack guarantor, forcing others toward strategic autonomy.

## Key Points
- Trump's tariff policy and institutional withdrawal signal US prioritizes hemispheric dominance over post-WWII order stewardship.
- Europe capitulated quickly to US pressure, buying weapons and energy while weakening own industrial/digital sovereignty.
- Post-WWII institutions remain functional but orphaned—the world must operate systems it didn't build without their architect's commitment.

## Open Question
If US-built systems persist but lack American commitment, who ensures their legitimacy and function—and can emerging powers reshape rules without destabilizing the entire order?

## India Angle
India faces a multipolar world with weakened US commitment to global order—requiring strategic autonomy, diversified partnerships, and navigation of great power competition without traditional security guarantees. The diffused geopolitical landscape creates both risks (instability, reduced US support) and opportunities (increased strategic leverage).
