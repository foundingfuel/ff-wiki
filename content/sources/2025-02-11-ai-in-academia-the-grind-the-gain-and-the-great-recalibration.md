## Core Argument
AI forces academia to recalibrate: move from knowledge-testing to teaching students how to think, question, and navigate complexity.

## Key Points
- AI threatens deep learning not through replacement but by removing productive struggle; efficiency without purpose erodes intellectual development.
- Three unresolved tensions: who does intellectual work, who owns faculty-created content, and whether efficiency optimizes for the wrong learning outcomes.
- Complex problems require experimentation and adaptation, not blanket bans; educators must redefine what learning means when knowledge retrieval is no longer scarce.

## Open Question
If AI removes the 'grind' that builds thinking skills, can institutions redesign learning to preserve deep intellectual struggle while leveraging AI's efficiency—or is the struggle itself irreplaceable?

## India Angle
Indian academia faces similar pressures as institutions adopt AI; unlike the US, Indian universities have fewer resources to experiment safely, making the recalibration question more urgent—balancing access to AI tools with building foundational thinking skills in resource-constrained settings.
