## Core Argument
Corporations control digital remains after death; legal frameworks and data hygiene practices must evolve globally and in India.

## Key Points
- Digital data is fragile and profit-driven: Big Tech will eventually choose who gets remembered based on commercial viability.
- India lacks normative practices around data inheritance; wills addressing social media accounts and passwords should become standard.
- Personality rights matter: CGI resurrection and digital legacy use require consent frameworks, with precedent in Delhi High Court orders.

## Open Question
How can individuals and societies preserve digital heritage without relying on profit-driven corporations whose incentives align with erasure rather than preservation?

## India Angle
India needs data hygiene frameworks and inheritance laws; digital legacies of Indians may face erasure due to low commercial value; Delhi High Court orders on personality rights offer precedent for protecting deceased persons' digital assets.
