## Core Argument
Online learning platforms can solve India's higher education access crisis if faculty and learners embrace self-directed, community-based learning models.

## Key Points
- Swayam allows 20% university credits via MOOCs, injecting choice into rigid system and enabling rural access to elite faculty.
- IIM-B's scrappy startup approach yielded 300,000 enrollments in two years with minimal resources, proving scalability without brick-and-mortar expansion.
- India's learning culture relies on faculty-driven instruction; MOOCs require cultural shift toward self-discipline, peer accountability, and honour-code systems.

## Open Question
Can India's traditional hierarchical learning culture, accustomed to faculty-led instruction, genuinely transition to self-directed, peer-accountability-based online learning without diluting quality or equity?

## India Angle
India faces acute shortage of quality higher education institutions and trained faculty; MOOCs and Swayam platform could democratize access across geographically dispersed regions like Arunachal Pradesh, but require cultural shift from passive to self-directed learning and faculty pedagogy redesign.
