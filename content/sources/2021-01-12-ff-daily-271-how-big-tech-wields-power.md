## Core Argument
Big Tech companies conduct thousands of randomized experiments daily on users without consent, wielding unprecedented power over behavior.

## Key Points
- Digital experiments cost near-zero compared to offline studies (thousands to hundreds of thousands of dollars), enabling massive scale.
- Tech companies run A/B tests continuously without user knowledge or consent, measuring clicks and behavioral responses automatically.
- Facebook conducts 1,000 A/B tests daily—more experiments than pharmaceutical industry starts yearly—on unsuspecting populations.

## Open Question
How can societies regulate or govern the conduct of continuous, invisible behavioral experiments on digital populations when the experiments are cheap, fast, and often legal?

## India Angle
Indian policymakers face a critical challenge: Big Tech companies can conduct large-scale behavioral experiments on Indian users at near-zero cost, with minimal oversight or transparency. Given India's weak regulatory capacity and vast digital population, the asymmetry of power is acute—companies like Google and Facebook can test interventions on millions of Indians without consent or accountability.
