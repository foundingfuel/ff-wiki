## Core Argument
Economic growth metrics ignore unpaid care work that enables growth, creating hidden social costs.

## Key Points
- GDP ignores millions of Indian women doing unpaid agricultural and domestic labor for centuries
- Care arrangements that boost two incomes shift unpaid burdens to vulnerable workers across geographies
- Aging populations and fewer young workers create unsustainable pension and elder-care systems without addressing care as economic value

## Open Question
How do societies measure and value care work that enables formal economic participation without reducing humans to economic units or abandoning growth imperatives entirely?

## India Angle
India's informal economy makes millions of women workers invisible in GDP models; care work remains unpaid despite enabling formal workforce participation. Pensions and elder care crises parallel China's demographic burden but with weaker state capacity.
