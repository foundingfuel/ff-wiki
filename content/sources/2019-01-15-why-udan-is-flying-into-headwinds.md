## Core Argument
UDAN's regional connectivity vision faces structural headwinds: infrastructure gaps, metro airport congestion, and flawed network design.

## Key Points
- Metro airports lack landing slots and capacity; UDAN routes compete for scarce resources against high-demand mainline flights.
- Remote airports like Pakyong lack instrument landing systems, causing cancellations that prevent sustainable traffic building.
- Hub-and-spoke network design conflicts with point-to-point UDAN routes; India's runway capacity hasn't scaled with 18-20% annual traffic growth.

## Open Question
How can UDAN create truly sustainable regional connectivity when India's metro airports are fundamentally capacity-constrained and competing countries are consolidating mega-hubs instead?

## India Angle
UDAN's success depends on solving India-specific infrastructure and capacity constraints—congested metro airports, weather challenges in remote regions, and network design challenges that China and Turkey are addressing more aggressively.
