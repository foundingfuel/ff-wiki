## Core Argument
First-generation media entrepreneurs face structural disadvantages against well-capitalized industrial conglomerates in India's regulated media landscape.

## Key Points
- Roys' 2009 loan agreement with Ambani/Reliance locked them into indefinite warrant exercise by creditor, regardless of repayment.
- MIB's 51% Indian ownership requirement for news channels limited Roys' financing options during 2008 crisis, forcing reliance on Ambani.
- Adani group's resource advantage makes legal contestation by Roys costly and protracted despite contractual violation claims.

## Open Question
How can India's media regulatory framework protect editorial independence and founder stakes when growth capital forces dependence on industrial houses with political connections?

## India Angle
Reflects India's media sector power dynamics, regulatory constraints on foreign/concentrated ownership of news channels, and how political proximity shapes M&A outcomes between India's largest industrialists.
