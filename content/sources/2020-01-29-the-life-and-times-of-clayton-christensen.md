## Core Argument
Clayton Christensen's frameworks for understanding innovation and purposeful leadership transcend management classrooms to shape how we work and live.

## Key Points
- Not all innovations are equal; understanding 'jobs to be done' reveals what customers actually need, not what data suggests.
- Management is a noble profession when leaders integrate personal integrity and purpose with professional ambition, not compartmentalizing the two.
- Teachers who influence beyond classrooms are generous learners who continuously evolve their thinking and share insights generously.

## Open Question
How can management thinkers maintain intellectual humility and avoid their own ideas becoming orthodoxy that blinds practitioners to emerging realities?

## India Angle
Christensen's frameworks—disruptive innovation, jobs-to-be-done—are directly applicable to Indian entrepreneurs navigating market disruption in sectors like fintech, agritech, and media. His emphasis on integrity and purpose beyond profit resonates with India's growing startup culture seeking sustainable growth.
