## Core Argument
Indoor air pollution is invisible yet critical; systemic behavior change requires measurement before purchase decisions.

## Key Points
- Air pollution is the fourth leading cause of premature death but remains invisible, causing delayed perception and inaction.
- Indoor air is 2-5x more polluted than outdoor air; most Indians spend 80-100% of time indoors since pandemic.
- Start with low-cost air evaluation (₹1,300) rather than expensive purifiers to drive awareness and informed decision-making.

## Open Question
How do you create urgency and behavior change around an invisible problem that only reveals harm years later, especially when water contamination triggered reform only after a visible outbreak?

## India Angle
Indoor air quality is a widespread but underappreciated health crisis across Indian urban centers (Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, Chennai). Unlike water purification, which became normalized post-cholera, air quality remains invisible and neglected despite being a leading cause of premature death. The article addresses India's unique context: high humidity in coastal cities breeding mold, sewage pollution, construction dust, and burning of garbage—problems specific to Indian infrastructure and urban development.
