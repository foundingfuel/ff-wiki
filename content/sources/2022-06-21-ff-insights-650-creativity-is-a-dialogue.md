## Core Argument
Creativity emerges from deep problem definition, not epiphany; India's transformation demands rethinking underlying economic and governance theories.

## Key Points
- Problem-finding precedes solution-finding; respecting frustration and failure is essential to innovation.
- Systemic problems cannot be solved with the same thinking that created them; India@100 needs new theories.
- Dyson, Masking tape, ATM—innovations all responded to real, lived pain points of users.

## Open Question
If creativity requires real problems and failures, how do leaders balance the need for ambitious forward-thinking vision with grounding solutions in current ground realities?

## India Angle
India@100 vision requires reimagining how Indian businesses, leaders and policymakers approach problem-solving—moving beyond incremental growth to systemic transformation that addresses persistent inequalities and economic security gaps.
