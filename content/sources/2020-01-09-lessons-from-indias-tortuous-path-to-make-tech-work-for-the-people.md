## Core Argument
India's digital infrastructure experiment balances technological promise against inevitable risks at unprecedented scale.

## Key Points
- India Stack reached 1.25 billion Aadhaar enrollments and 1.3 billion UPI transactions monthly, enabling 30+ percentage point rise in bank account ownership among poor and women.
- Even 80% satisfaction leaves 260 million Indians behind; scale amplifies both benefits and risks of fraud, security breaches, and exclusion.
- Success requires shifting mindsets: biometrics for inclusion not surveillance; data-sharing for empowerment not protection; iterative think-do-think cycles not predetermined templates.

## Open Question
How can India scale digital welfare to a billion people while managing inevitable exclusions, inequalities in access, and implementation failures that leave tens of millions behind?

## India Angle
India's India Stack (Aadhaar, UPI, eKYC) offers a scalable model for financial inclusion and digital public infrastructure that other developing nations are adopting, but raises critical questions about implementation risks and equity in a context of massive inequality.
