## Core Argument
Pitroda's telecom revolution, enabled by Rajiv Gandhi's backing, democratized telephony access and created India's cellular economy.

## Key Points
- STD/PCO booths made basic telephony accessible to millions while creating employment for otherwise jobless individuals
- Political patronage and bureaucratic navigation were critical; without Rajiv Gandhi's support, the transformation wouldn't have succeeded
- Pitroda's US experience, personal capital, and willingness to challenge the system allowed him to work around entrenched obstacles

## Open Question
How much of India's telecom success was attributable to visionary policy and entrepreneurship versus political patronage and personal connections—and can either model be replicated without the same conditions?

## India Angle
Chronicles how Indian telecom infrastructure was built through political patronage and entrepreneurial vision in the 1980s-90s, establishing the foundation for India's current status as a 'cell phone nation' and creating employment through STD/PCO booths.
