## Core Argument
Plain language and storytelling make strategy communication credible, sticky, and spreadable across organizations.

## Key Points
- Business jargon undermines credibility; research shows concrete, definitive language is perceived as more trustworthy.
- The 'curse of knowledge' and co-creation processes transform simple ideas into impenetrable corporate speak.
- Stories structure strategy for universal comprehension, retention, and viral retelling across organizational levels.

## Open Question
How do leaders balance the institutional pressure to sound sophisticated with the evidence that plain language builds greater credibility and organizational alignment?

## India Angle
Indian organizations, especially post-MBA leaders and consulting-influenced companies, suffer from excessive jargon in strategy communication—a particular problem given India's diverse workforce with varying English proficiency levels and educational backgrounds.
