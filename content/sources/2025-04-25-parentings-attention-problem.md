## Core Argument
Parental absence delegated to nannies and gadgets stunts children's cognitive and emotional development through lost free play.

## Key Points
- Nuclear family proliferation (34% to 50% since 2008) has replaced joint-family parenting with isolated, stressed individual parents.
- Nannies often lack attention, compassion and sensitivity; they manage children as tasks rather than nurture development.
- Free play deprivation impairs social, cognitive and emotional growth; children need adult presence, not just supervision.

## Open Question
How can working parents in high-cost Indian cities provide adequate attention and free play opportunities when childcare infrastructure is inadequate and cultural safety concerns often preclude unsupervised outdoor play?

## India Angle
Indian shift from joint families to nuclear families, combined with urban work pressures and inadequate childcare infrastructure, is creating an 'attention deficit' in parenting—particularly acute in expensive metros like Mumbai where both parents must work.
