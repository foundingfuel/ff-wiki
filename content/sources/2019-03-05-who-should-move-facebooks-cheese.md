## Core Argument
Technology platforms need community-centered governance and public-domain ownership models, not just individual user consent or corporate self-regulation.

## Key Points
- Facebook extracts behavioral data through design and lobbying against privacy regulation, making individual consent meaningless.
- Technology impacts minds and behavior more than time/data consumption; regulation must address psychological manipulation.
- Communities must shape technology governance through local solutions with national support, not centralized top-down policies.

## Open Question
If communities should govern technology platforms locally, how do global platforms with billions of cross-border users get regulated without fragmenting into incompatible systems?

## India Angle
India must develop regulatory frameworks for data privacy and social media ownership models before these technologies become entrenched. The example of UPI shows public-domain network infrastructure can work; India should consider similar approaches for social platforms rather than defaulting to corporate control.
