## Core Argument
Corporate structures systematically prioritize profit over long-term safety, exploiting regulatory gaps and suppressing internal warnings.

## Key Points
- 3M buried PFOS toxicity evidence for decades despite scientist discoveries, treating warnings as threats rather than signals.
- PFAS regulation lags science globally; India's incomplete standards create opportunities for companies externalizing harm to weaker jurisdictions.
- Pattern repeats across industries—Boeing, Purdue Pharma, Big Tech—revealing systemic failures in corporate governance and accountability.

## Open Question
How can regulators close gaps between scientific evidence and enforceable standards before industries exploit weaker jurisdictions, given capture risks and business pressure?

## India Angle
India lacks enforceable PFAS standards, creating regulatory vacuum that attracts polluting industries relocating from stricter jurisdictions—exemplified by Laxmi Organic acquiring defunct Italian PFAS producer with convicted managers.
