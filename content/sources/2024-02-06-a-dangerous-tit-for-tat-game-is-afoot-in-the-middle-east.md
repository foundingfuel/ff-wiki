## Core Argument
Middle East tit-for-tat escalation risks destabilizing global economy; miscalculation by any actor could shatter fragile post-pandemic trade equilibrium.

## Key Points
- US-Iran proxy warfare is intensifying through non-state actors with unpredictable independent tendencies, making de-escalation mechanisms unreliable.
- Red Sea Houthi attacks directly disrupt global shipping and trade; Bab-el-Mandeb bottleneck threatens oil and goods flow to Europe and Asia.
- China hedges interests by maintaining relationships with both Iran and Saudi Arabia while avoiding diplomatic heavy-lifting, maximizing strategic optionality.

## Open Question
How can regional powers and global actors prevent miscalculation-driven escalation when tit-for-tat cycles accelerate faster than diplomatic channels can operate?

## India Angle
India's critical dependence on Middle Eastern oil, Red Sea shipping routes for trade, and exposure to supply chain disruptions makes regional escalation an existential economic risk. India must navigate between US-Iran tensions while protecting energy security.
