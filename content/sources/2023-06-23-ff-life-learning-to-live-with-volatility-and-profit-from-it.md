## Core Argument
Volatility is temporary; selling during crises converts paper losses into permanent losses and destroys wealth.

## Key Points
- Volatility reflects temporary price dislocations, not fundamental business risk for long-term investors.
- Timing markets—selling high, buying low—is psychologically impossible even for professional investors.
- Selling during downturns converts quotational losses into real losses, destroying long-term wealth creation.

## Open Question
If volatility creates genuine psychological distress even for experienced investors, how can behavioural discipline alone overcome the gap between intellectual understanding and emotional action?

## India Angle
Indian retail investors, increasingly participating in stock markets post-liberalisation, face psychological and behavioural challenges during volatility—particularly relevant given India's market cycles (2008 crisis, demonetisation, Covid) and growing middle-class participation in investing.
