## Core Argument
Unlimited potential paradoxically paralyzes high-achievers into choosing safe, conventional paths over authentic ambitions.

## Key Points
- Elite students delay commitment by pursuing 'stem cell' careers—prestigious yet non-foreclosing roles like consulting that maintain future optionality.
- Conditioning from childhood to jump through hoops makes well-worn paths feel safe, while foggy directions toward meaningful work feel risky.
- Over-personalisation and mind-reading biases limit emotional and behavioural options, increasing distress and preventing authentic choice-making.

## Open Question
How can high-potential individuals break free from the psychological conditioning and social structures that make unconventional, meaningful careers feel impossibly risky?

## India Angle
Indian students aspiring to elite US institutions face the same 'paradox of potential' — conditioning toward safe, prestigious paths (consulting, banking) rather than pursuing meaningful but uncertain ventures. India's emerging higher education landscape and brain drain reflect this tension between potential and actual career choices.
