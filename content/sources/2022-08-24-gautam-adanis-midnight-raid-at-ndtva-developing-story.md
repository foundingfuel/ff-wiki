## Core Argument
Adani's NDTV raid exploits a 14-year-old financial instrument to bypass founder consent and regulatory safeguards.

## Key Points
- Reliance's 2009 interest-free loan used convertible warrants as latent takeover mechanism with indefinite exercise rights.
- Warranty ownership transferred through HFCL to Adani, circumventing founder approval despite explicit 26% consent clause.
- Roys retain 61.45% stake but face open offer obligation triggered by warrant conversion—a unique structural vulnerability.

## Open Question
How can financial instruments designed as 'safety nets' for creditors become hostile takeover mechanisms, and what governance reforms are needed to protect founder-led companies from latent control transfers?

## India Angle
A watershed moment in Indian corporate governance—how a regulatory loophole (convertible warrants) enabled a hostile takeover of a premier media house, raising questions about founder protections, regulatory oversight, and the concentration of media ownership among industrial conglomerates
