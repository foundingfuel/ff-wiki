## Core Argument
Effective communication requires three-minute clarity; our brains cannot multitask, only switch tasks with cognitive cost.

## Key Points
- The 3-minute rule forces speakers to identify and lead with statements of value, naturally organizing ideas by importance.
- Human brains have severely limited conscious capacity; multitasking is actually task-switching that degrades performance measurably.
- Attention theft through distraction is structural; fixing it requires deliberate systems, not willpower alone.

## Open Question
If our brains naturally organize information by value when forced to be succinct, why do we persist in hour-long presentations and constant multitasking despite knowing the costs?

## India Angle
Indian professionals and entrepreneurs need better communication skills in compressed timeframes—critical for pitches to investors, client presentations, and remote work where attention spans are fractured across multiple platforms and time zones.
