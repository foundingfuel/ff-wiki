## Core Argument
Rational people resort to superstitions under uncertainty; policy must acknowledge cognitive biases in crisis decisions.

## Key Points
- Expert traders develop 'gamblers ticks'—spurious causal beliefs—despite knowing probability theory
- Government restrictions carry hidden costs beyond health: economic, psychological, democratic rights erosion
- Policy decisions require binary frameworks; waiting itself is costly, not neutral

## Open Question
How can institutions design systems that acknowledge human irrationality while maintaining principled decision-making during crises?

## India Angle
India's policymakers face similar trade-offs between health, economic activity, and democratic rights during crises; superstition-driven decision-making in Indian bureaucracy and markets undermines rational institutional responses
