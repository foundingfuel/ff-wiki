## Core Argument
Founders must protect deep work and user focus against organizational noise and bureaucratic drift post-acquisition.

## Key Points
- Big tech acquisitions dilute founder autonomy through legal complexity and meeting overhead, drowning signal.
- Deep observation and conscious attention to detail—whether painting or building—reveals insights invisible to rushed work.
- Creating lasting work requires resisting both corporate bureaucracy and the pressure to optimize for metrics over meaning.

## Open Question
How can founders maintain their original mission and creative autonomy inside large organizations that inevitably add layers of governance and risk mitigation?

## India Angle
Indian founders acquiring or being acquired by tech giants face similar signal-to-noise ratio challenges; Paul Graham's lessons on deep observation and craftsmanship apply to Indian startup culture emphasizing sustainable building over rapid scaling.
