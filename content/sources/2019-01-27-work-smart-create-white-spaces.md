## Core Argument
Creating unstructured time for deep thinking drives better outcomes than perpetual busyness.

## Key Points
- Busyness has become a status symbol masking low-quality work and diminishing returns.
- Deep work and reflection are as valuable as execution but systematically squeezed out.
- Designing intentional white spaces in calendars is a conscious choice, not a luxury.

## Open Question
How do we create white spaces in systems that structurally reward visible busyness and penalize unavailability?

## India Angle
Indian professionals are particularly susceptible to the 'busyness trap'—long work hours, calendar congestion, and always-on culture are normalized in Indian corporate environments; creating white spaces requires rethinking how success is defined locally.
