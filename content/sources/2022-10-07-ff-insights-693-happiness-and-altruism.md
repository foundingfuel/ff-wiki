## Core Argument
Spending money on others reliably increases happiness more than self-spending, across all economies.

## Key Points
- 63% predict self-spending brings more happiness; research proves the opposite consistently true.
- Focus and confidence management separate elite performers from good ones in high-pressure roles.
- Altruism's happiness benefits hold across rich and poor economies—universal principle, not context-dependent.

## Open Question
If altruism reliably increases happiness, why do most people predict and choose self-spending instead—and what prevents knowledge from changing behavior?

## India Angle
Indian workplace culture often emphasizes self-reliance and individual achievement; understanding altruism's happiness benefits could reshape how Indian leaders build team culture and employee well-being.
