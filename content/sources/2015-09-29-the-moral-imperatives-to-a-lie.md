## Core Argument
Strategic deception for others' benefit may be moral; leaders must balance honesty against benevolence.

## Key Points
- Mahabharata shows Krishna sanction lies when dharma demands it, contrasting Western absolutes like Kant's.
- Schweitzer & Levine research reveals people object to selfish deception, not deception itself that helps others.
- Managers must balance benevolent feedback with honest criticism; context determines which builds lasting trust.

## Open Question
When does benevolent deception strengthen relationships versus undermine trust by robbing people of truth needed for growth?

Uses Mahabharata's moral complexity to reframe how Indian leaders should think about truth-telling in business contexts, challenging Gandhi's absolutism with situational ethics.
