## Core Argument
Economic decisions reshape Indian politics through structural changes, but electoral outcomes hinge on political trust and welfare narratives, not economics.

## Key Points
- Major economic shocks (1966, 1991, 2016) trigger 10-15 year political realignments, creating new voting coalitions and power structures.
- Electoral outcomes driven by political trust and welfare benefit distribution, not economic performance metrics or policy intentions.
- Rising joblessness and manufacturing underperformance haven't prevented electoral victories, suggesting welfare populism trumps macroeconomic outcomes.

## Open Question
If economic performance matters less than political trust and welfare distribution for electoral outcomes, can any government credibly commit to structural economic reform when voters reward short-term benefit distribution?
