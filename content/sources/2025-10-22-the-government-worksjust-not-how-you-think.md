## Core Argument
Government works quietly; corruption is social seduction; real change comes through empathy-driven leadership accepting systems before reforming them.

## Key Points
- Government invisibly functions during crises; India's educated class must respect public systems instead of dismissing them as broken.
- Corruption stems from citizen behavior and social seduction, not just bureaucracy—transparency requires character, not just punitive rules.
- Skill-driven human transformation, measured by role models and dignity, rewrites social hierarchies and creates intergenerational change.

## Open Question
How can systemic reform happen when leaders must first accept and love dysfunctional systems—does acceptance enable change or entrench status quo?

## India Angle
India's educated class must reframe its relationship with government systems, recognizing their silent functionality and integrity as societal rather than bureaucratic issues—critical for nation-building amid poverty and aspiration.
