## Core Argument
Brands must balance digital attention demands with ethical boundaries; creative risk-taking requires governance, not just eyeballs.

## Key Points
- Digital marketing's 'always-on' nature pressures agencies to manufacture viral moments, often crossing ethical lines.
- Brands must develop clear governance frameworks for creative approvals, especially when associating with sensitive socio-health topics.
- Social media empowers consumers to amplify backlash instantly; brands cannot escape accountability through structural distance or intermediaries.

## Open Question
How can brands maintain relevance in a cluttered digital attention economy without sacrificing ethical guardrails, when the very system rewarding viral content incentivizes boundary-pushing and emotional manipulation?

## India Angle
Indian brands and agencies operating in a hypercompetitive digital market face mounting pressure to create viral content, but the Poonam Pandey case exemplifies how stunts exploiting sensitive issues (health, death, religion) can trigger backlash in India's increasingly woke, socially conscious consumer base—requiring clearer ethical guardrails.
