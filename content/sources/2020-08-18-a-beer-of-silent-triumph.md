## Core Argument
Financial independence—earning enough to cover your own expenses—marks entry into adulthood, but Covid is dismantling the predictable trajectory that enabled it.

## Key Points
- Financial independence means paying all monthly expenses comfortably (~$50k/year in US, Rs 50k/month in India) and maintaining autonomy from family economic control.
- Post-liberalization globalization enabled young Indians to leave home seeking work, internalizing upward earning trajectories as cultural norm and marker of adulthood.
- Covid's economic fallout is forcing millennials back into multi-generational households and erasing entry-level job prospects, particularly for debt-laden international students.

## Open Question
How do young people in India maintain psychological and emotional independence from family when forced back into economic dependence by systemic shocks, and does financial independence remain a viable cultural aspiration?

## India Angle
India's post-liberalization generation faces a collision between Western individualism (financial independence as personal autonomy) and traditional joint-family economics; Covid threatens the upward earning trajectory that enabled this transition, hitting Indians particularly hard given currency depreciation and limited social safety nets.
