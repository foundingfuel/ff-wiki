## Core Argument
Indian MBAs must prioritize execution and implementation skills tailored to family businesses and complex stakeholder environments, not just decision-making theory.

## Key Points
- Indian B-schools account for 25% of global business schools but only 3 have AACSB accreditation, limiting global influence.
- Indian MBA curricula should emphasize implementation, managing without formal structures, diversity management, and government stakeholder relations.
- India offers unique research opportunities in innovation, entrepreneurship, outsourcing, microfinance, and health management with global learning potential.

## Open Question
How can Indian B-schools develop distinctive, locally-relevant curricula that prepare students for India's unique challenges while maintaining global credibility and accreditation standards?

## India Angle
Indian B-schools must develop distinctive curricula focused on execution, implementation in complex environments, family business management, and stakeholder navigation—leveraging India's unique business context rather than importing Western MBA models wholesale.
