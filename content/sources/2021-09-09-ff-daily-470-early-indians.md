## Core Argument
India and China diverge on edtech: India allows private capacity; China centralizes state control over information and behaviour.

## Key Points
- China's edtech crackdown reflects CCP's need to monopolize control over citizens' psychological and behavioural data.
- India's openness to private education creates parallel institutional capacity outside state purview.
- Venture capital functions like F1 racing—structural advantages matter more than individual talent or effort.

## Open Question
If private edtech accumulates vast behavioural data on citizens, does India risk a future regulatory reckoning similar to China's, or does democratic institutional design prevent such concentration?

## India Angle
India's permissive stance on private edtech contrasts sharply with China's state control—raising questions about whether India's fragmented approach to education regulation serves entrepreneurship or creates oversight gaps that could invite future crackdowns.
