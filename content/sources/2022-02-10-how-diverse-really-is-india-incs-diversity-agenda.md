## Core Argument
India Inc's diversity agenda prioritizes appearance over understanding disability's complex lived reality.

## Key Points
- PWDs represent 2.21% of India's population but only 0.5% of employees in top companies, revealing policy-practice gap.
- Disability inclusion requires systemic change addressing environmental and social barriers, not just humanitarian sentiment or compliance.
- Intersectional challenges—gender, caste, privilege, rural-urban divide—compound disability discrimination, demanding nuanced policy redesign.

## Open Question
If companies genuinely recognize the economic value PWDs add, why does indifference—not hostility—remain the primary barrier to meaningful inclusion?

## India Angle
India Inc's disability inclusion policies remain performative; only 0.5% of employees in top companies are PWDs despite 2.21% of population being disabled. Policy gaps, lack of understanding, and intersectional barriers (gender, caste, rural-urban divide) uniquely impede disabled individuals' integration into Indian workplaces and economy.
