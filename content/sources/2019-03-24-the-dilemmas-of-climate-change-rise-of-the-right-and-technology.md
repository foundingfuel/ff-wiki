## Core Argument
Climate action requires public imagination and political will, not just scientific consensus, to overcome resistance from affected citizens.

## Key Points
- 1.5M schoolchildren in 71 countries struck for climate action, marking unprecedented mobilisation despite decades of scientific warnings.
- France's diesel tax backfired—Macron's climate policy alienated middle/rural citizens, sparking Yellow Vest protests and exposing policy-implementation dilemma.
- Technology (EVs) and culture change are necessary but insufficient without addressing how climate policy redistributes economic burden across society.

## Open Question
How do governments implement necessary climate policies without triggering backlash from citizens whose livelihoods or costs are directly affected, and how does this relate to the rise of right-wing political movements?

## India Angle
India participated in the global climate strike but faces its own climate-policy tensions similar to France's Macron—balancing environmental action with livelihood impacts on middle and working classes. Electric vehicle adoption (Ather example) shows India's tech entrepreneurship, but requires policy support and infrastructure to scale.
