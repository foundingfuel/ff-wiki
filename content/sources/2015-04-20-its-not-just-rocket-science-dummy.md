## Core Argument
Audacious ambition, participatory ecosystems, and multidisciplinary expertise enable India's private space team to compete globally.

## Key Points
- Non-aerospace founders leveraged confidence and tight timelines to achieve what traditional aerospace experts deemed impossible.
- Participatory model with ISRO alumni, L&T, Tata Communications created shared ownership beyond transactional partnerships.
- Multidisciplinary deep expertise across hardware, software, communications exceeded typical startup focus in single discipline.

## Open Question
How can private teams without aerospace backgrounds sustain momentum in a field where success depends on untestable factors and years of specialized expertise?

## India Angle
Team Indus represents India's first private attempt at lunar soft-landing, competing globally while building a participatory ecosystem with Indian institutions like ISRO, L&T, and Tata Communications—demonstrating how Indian startups can tackle moonshot problems through collaborative networks.
