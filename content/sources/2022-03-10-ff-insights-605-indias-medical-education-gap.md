## Core Argument
India's medical education shortage drives students abroad; expanding public medical colleges is essential infrastructure policy.

## Key Points
- 16.1 lakh NEET aspirants but only 8.7 lakh qualify; massive supply-demand gap forces students to seek education abroad.
- Private medical colleges cost Rs 1 crore vs Rs 30-40 lakh abroad, making foreign education financially attractive for many.
- Government must expand public medical colleges using district hospital infrastructure rather than relying on private enterprise alone.

## Open Question
How can India simultaneously expand medical college capacity while ensuring quality education and equitable access across socioeconomic groups?

## India Angle
India faces a critical supply-demand mismatch in medical education—16.1 lakh NEET registrants but only 8.7 lakh qualify, forcing students abroad. Private colleges cost Rs 1 crore vs foreign universities at Rs 30-40 lakh, revealing both access and affordability gaps that require government-led expansion of public medical colleges.
