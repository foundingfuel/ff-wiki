## Core Argument
State regulation of Big Tech is inevitable, driven by geopolitical competition for tech sovereignty rather than shared values.

## Key Points
- US, EU, and China regulate Big Tech through different lenses: competition, privacy, and state control respectively.
- Nationalism and cyber-sovereignty concerns, not just privacy/competition, now drive state-Big Tech conflict globally.
- Tech companies lack clarity on rules while states reassert control over commercially-developed technology with national security implications.

## Open Question
How can innovation flourish if states fragment the internet for sovereignty, yet restrict R&D investment to domestic actors only?

## India Angle
India must navigate between US-EU regulatory models and China's state control approach while building its own tech sovereignty framework. Critical for Indian startups scaling globally and policymakers defining India's tech governance stance amid great power competition.
