## Core Argument
Corporate short-termism is dismantling the institutional leadership development model that built generational talent pipelines.

## Key Points
- Crotonville symbolized CEO commitment to systematic leadership development; its sale signals outsider CEOs prioritize shareholder value over cultural transmission.
- Indian corporate universities (TMTC, Gyanodaya, Four Acres) modeled themselves on Crotonville; its demise may weaken their institutional relevance.
- Tension between expensive in-house programmes requiring long tenures versus expanding external executive education industry and distributed learning models.

## Open Question
If companies cut long-term leadership development investment to boost short-term returns, how will they build the institutional knowledge and cultural cohesion needed for sustained competitive advantage?

## India Angle
Indian companies like Tata, Unilever, Aditya Birla, and Infosys built their own corporate universities inspired by Crotonville. Crotonville's closure signals a shift in how Indian firms may approach leadership development—from long-term institutional investment to short-term cost-cutting, affecting talent pipeline quality.
