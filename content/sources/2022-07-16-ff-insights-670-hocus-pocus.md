## Core Argument
Older workers and unconventional spaces unlock value when we abandon false assumptions about decline and 'wonder tissue.'

## Key Points
- Older workers (40+) are three times more likely to build successful companies due to patience and collaborative maturity.
- Expertise and knowledge increase beyond age 30 and 80; mental decline is offset by accumulated wisdom and continued learning capacity.
- Malls function as vital community infrastructure—safe, accessible spaces for identity and belonging—yet remain culturally dismissed.

## Open Question
If evidence clearly shows older workers outperform younger ones as entrepreneurs, why do organizational systems remain structurally biased against hiring them?

## India Angle
Indian companies often discriminate against older workers despite evidence they make better entrepreneurs; malls serve similar community functions in Indian cities where public spaces are congested or unsafe, yet are undervalued as social infrastructure.
