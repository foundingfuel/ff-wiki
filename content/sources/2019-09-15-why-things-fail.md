## Core Argument
Technologies create unintended consequences; transparency and societal engagement must precede deployment.

## Key Points
- Utilitarianism without transparency—like MIT's Epstein funding—damages trust and legitimacy.
- Complex systems (mosquitoes, roofs) violate reversible software logic; consequences cascade unpredictably.
- Gene editing, neural interfaces, global platforms need early societal engagement, not post-hoc justification.

## Open Question
How can society meaningfully engage with rapidly advancing science when the loudest voices claiming to represent society lack legitimacy or broader perspective?

## India Angle
Indian policymakers and technologists must grapple with unintended consequences of adopting global tech solutions (like biometric systems, gene editing) without local context; India's role as a testing ground for global experiments raises ethical questions about consent and impact.
