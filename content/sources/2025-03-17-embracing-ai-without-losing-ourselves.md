## Core Argument
AI's rise forces us to actively choose humanity—create, struggle, think—or risk surrendering intelligence itself.

## Key Points
- Keynes predicted technology would free us; instead it multiplied busyness and inequality.
- Baudrillard warns we may surrender first—trading thinking for automation before machines even surpass us.
- Real creativity lies in struggle and process, not just output; AI cannot replicate the experience of making.

## Open Question
If AI can solve problems faster than humans, how do we justify the 'inefficiency' of choosing to struggle—and will future generations accept that trade-off?

## India Angle
India's rapidly growing tech workforce and AI adoption must consciously preserve human agency—avoiding both uncritical acceptance of AI as solution and passive surrender to automation. This shapes how Indian companies build culture, how talent remains engaged, and how society navigates displacement.
