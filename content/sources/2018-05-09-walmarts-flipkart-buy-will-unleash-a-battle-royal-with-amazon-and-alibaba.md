## Core Argument
Foreign e-commerce giants will dominate India's market, but profitability requires navigating three distinct economic tiers with different blueprints.

## Key Points
- Indian e-commerce requires billions in sustained investment before profitability; market structure fundamentally differs from China or US.
- India's fragmented economy—three distinct tiers with different income, language, and consumption patterns—demands tier-specific strategies, not uniform approaches.
- Only 70 million active e-commerce customers today; another 100+ million must be activated at estimated $15-20B+ cost to unlock sustainable returns.

## Open Question
Does India have sufficient scale within profitable segments (India One: 50% of economy) to justify the massive capital burn needed to activate emerging tiers, or will e-commerce remain perpetually unprofitable?

## India Angle
Walmart's Flipkart acquisition signals foreign capital domination of Indian e-commerce; survival depends on solving India's unique challenges: fragmented consumer base, income pyramid warping, infrastructure friction, and need for differentiated strategies across three distinct economic tiers.
