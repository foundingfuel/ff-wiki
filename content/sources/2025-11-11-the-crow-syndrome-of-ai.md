## Core Argument
AI systems inherit human blindness to difference, encoding bias into outcomes at scale unless trained on diverse data.

## Key Points
- Algorithms trained on narrow datasets ignore contextual differences—like assuming all CFOs follow US fiscal calendars.
- Bias in training data becomes faster, wider discrimination: facial recognition arrests, resume screening, healthcare misdiagnosis.
- Fixing requires diverse datasets, transparency, continuous testing, and moral accountability from builders and regulators.

## Open Question
How do we balance the need for diverse training data with privacy concerns and the practical difficulty of gathering truly representative datasets across geographies and communities?

## India Angle
Indian enterprises adopting Western-trained AI models face the crow syndrome problem acutely—fiscal year-ends, naming conventions, cultural nuances are ignored. India's diverse population makes bias detection critical; regulatory frameworks must mandate local testing before deployment.
