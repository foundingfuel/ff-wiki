## Core Argument
Agility requires leaders to relinquish control, embrace dissent, and separate identity from ideas to unlock organizational performance.

## Key Points
- Command-and-control cultures suppress free thinking through peer pressure and social ostracism, preventing better ideas from surfacing.
- Agile organizations like Spotify and ING demonstrate superior outcomes by iterating, combining opposing views, and trusting teams.
- Leaders must feel secure enough to distribute power and enable psychological safety for teams to discover solutions collaboratively.

## Open Question
How can established hierarchical organizations in India—with deep cultural roots in command-and-control—genuinely transition to agile practices without reverting to old patterns under pressure?

## India Angle
Indian organizations and leaders need to adopt Agile principles to compete globally; command-and-control structures prevalent in Indian businesses stifle innovation and psychological safety required for agility.
