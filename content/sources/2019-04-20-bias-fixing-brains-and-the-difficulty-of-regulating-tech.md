## Core Argument
Technology amplifies human bias; regulation must evolve faster than innovation to prevent systemic harms.

## Key Points
- Regulatory sandboxes allow real-world testing before finalizing rules, reducing unintended consequences.
- AI bias reflects workforce homogeneity (18% women, 2.5% Black workers at major tech firms).
- Gene editing, brain implants, and 3D bioprinting raise ethical boundaries regulators struggle to define.

## Open Question
How can regulators establish ethical guardrails for exponentially advancing biotech and AI when deployment speed outpaces governance capacity and ethical consensus?

## India Angle
India's RBI regulatory sandbox approach for fintech offers a Build-Measure-Learn model that could address AI regulation challenges; however, India faces acute risks of AI bias amplifying existing gender disparities (65M women excluded from electoral rolls) and underrepresentation in AI development workforce.
