## Core Argument
India's institutional past offers lessons for navigating contemporary challenges in governance, education, and competitive strategy.

## Key Points
- India built world-class institutions (IIT, IIM, TIFR) through visionary leadership; their principles remain relevant today.
- State capacity is not about minimal government but about understanding what works, why, and how to democratize solutions.
- Global competition follows the Rule of Three: three generalist competitors dominate long-term; technology and globalization have reshaped this dynamic.

## Open Question
How can India balance the need for academic autonomy and institutional independence with state accountability and public interest, especially given the historical success of state-backed institutions?

## India Angle
India's institutional building capacity in higher education, state governance effectiveness, and competitive positioning in global markets—critical for understanding how India develops world-class institutions and navigates policy challenges.
