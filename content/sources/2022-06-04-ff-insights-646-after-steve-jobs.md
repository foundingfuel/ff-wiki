## Core Argument
Building lasting companies requires prioritizing product and purpose over short-term sales and exits.

## Key Points
- Jobs distinguished real entrepreneurship from startup exits; building enduring companies is harder but leaves legacy.
- Decline occurs when sales-driven leaders replace product-focused builders, as happened at IBM, Microsoft, and early Apple.
- MBA education risks oversimplifying complex human organizations through single-narrative case studies, limiting leadership capability.
- Indian parallel: Congress party's decline mirrors corporate decline when leadership abandons foundational principles for factional gains.

## Open Question
Can professionally managed companies (like Apple under Tim Cook) achieve trillion-dollar scale while preserving the founder's vision and soul, or is that trade-off inevitable?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs often chase exits over building lasting enterprises; Congress decline shows what happens when organizations lose foundational purpose—both lessons in building institutions that endure.
