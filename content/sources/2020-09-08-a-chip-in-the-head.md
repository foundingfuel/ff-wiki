## Core Argument
Brain-machine interfaces will create two classes of humans; staying relevant requires augmentation, not education alone.

## Key Points
- Knowledge half-life collapses: medical knowledge doubles every 73 days; engineering graduates obsolete in 3-5 years
- AI erodes traditional human advantages: algorithms write Shakespeare, AI-generated art sells for hundreds of thousands
- Chip adoption inevitable: early adopters gain competitive advantage like smartphone users; non-adoption risks permanent obsolescence

## Open Question
If brain-machine interfaces become mainstream, who decides access and ethical guardrails—and what happens to humans who reject or cannot afford augmentation?

## India Angle
Indian professionals face acute knowledge obsolescence risk (medical knowledge doubling every 73 days); brain-machine interfaces could amplify digital divide between haves and have-nots in India's unequal tech access landscape
