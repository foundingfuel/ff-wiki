## Core Argument
India's manufacturing ambitions risk entrenching exploitative labour practices without strong institutional safeguards.

## Key Points
- Wistron's exit and Tata's entry highlight industrial relations as critical but neglected in manufacturing competitiveness.
- State capacity erosion: weakened labour inspectorates, suspended laws, and weakened unions leave workers vulnerable to abuse.
- Automation will eventually eliminate low-skill jobs anyway, making exploitative conditions doubly damaging without upskilling pathways.

## Open Question
How can India attract manufacturing FDI while genuinely protecting worker rights when ease-of-business imperatives and labour law suspension seem structurally opposed to enforcement?

## India Angle
India's Make in India push risks replicating exploitative labour practices seen in China's manufacturing sector. Weakened labour oversight, gutted unions, and suspended labour laws in states like UP and Gujarat create conditions for worker exploitation while chasing FDI and GDP growth.
