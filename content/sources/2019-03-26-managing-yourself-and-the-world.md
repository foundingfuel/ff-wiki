## Core Argument
Technologies now directly target human minds; we must urgently redefine privacy, consent, and governance frameworks.

## Key Points
- Hallucinogens, VR, and brain-computer interfaces directly alter cognition—raising unprecedented ethical and societal questions.
- Privacy models are shifting from implicit to explicit consent; Account Aggregators in India exemplify this but reveal complexity in practice.
- Democracy and decision-making may outsource to AI; we need frameworks ensuring human values guide technology, not vice versa.

## Open Question
If technology can manage human minds better than humans manage themselves, who decides the values embedded in that technology, and how do we ensure those values represent humanity's diversity, not just its creators'?

## India Angle
India's digital ecosystem (social media, fintech with Account Aggregators, AI adoption) will face similar mind-altering tech disruptions and privacy challenges; policymakers must design consent frameworks and ethical guardrails proactively rather than reactively.
