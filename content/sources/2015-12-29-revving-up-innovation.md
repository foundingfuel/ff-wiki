## Core Argument
Indian CEOs must abandon TQM efficiency mindsets and embrace experimental cultures requiring controlled risk-taking and customer listening.

## Key Points
- TQM eliminated variance and risk; innovation requires organizational slack, experimentation, and tolerance for controlled failure.
- New tools like design thinking enable divergence and convergence; most Indian firms lack cultures supporting creative brainstorming and prototyping.
- Trust-based leaders who decentralize decision-making, align incentives for risk-taking, and listen to customers outperform hard-charging operational CEOs.

## Open Question
Can leaders shaped by two decades of TQM discipline genuinely unlearn efficiency-first thinking, or must boards replace them with a new generation to enable true innovation?

## India Angle
Indian IT and corporate leaders trained in TQM efficiency must fundamentally shift mindsets to drive innovation; examples like Bhaskar Bhat and Sandeep Bakshi show how decentralized, trust-based leadership works better for India Inc.'s next growth phase.
