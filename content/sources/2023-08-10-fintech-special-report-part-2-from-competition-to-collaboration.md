## Core Argument
Fintech startups shifting from disruption to collaboration as incumbents retain structural advantages in cost of capital and distribution.

## Key Points
- Fintech's unbundling thesis failed; banks grew larger while startups faced unsustainable customer acquisition costs.
- Incumbents possess irreplaceable advantages: cheaper capital access, existing customer base, regulatory favor, and distribution networks.
- Successful fintech model now centers on B2B partnerships with banks rather than direct market competition.

## Open Question
If fintech innovation only succeeds through serving incumbents, how does this reshape the original entrepreneurial promise of democratizing financial services?

## India Angle
Indian fintech startups' failed disruption strategy against incumbents reveals structural advantages (cost of capital, distribution, regulation) that favor established banks and NBFCs in India's financial services market.
