## Core Argument
Deliberate cheating destroys reputation differently than accidents; choice matters more than outcome for long-term success.

## Key Points
- VW's emissions cheating was deliberate, not accidental, making recovery fundamentally different from Toyota or J&J crises.
- The difficulty of meeting standards versus market opportunity created conditions for cheating as a perceived rational choice.
- Reputation and ethics are inseparable—short-term gains from cheating create long-term existential risk to businesses.

## Open Question
If regulatory standards are genuinely impossible to meet profitably, does that justify deliberate deception, or must companies accept market exit as the ethical choice?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs and CEOs face similar pressures to cut corners when navigating regulatory compliance versus profitability; the VW case offers a cautionary tale on how short-term gains destroy long-term value and trust in markets.
