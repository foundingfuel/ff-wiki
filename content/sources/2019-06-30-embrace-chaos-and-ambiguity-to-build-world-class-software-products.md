## Core Argument
Embracing ambiguity and chaos is essential for building products, but India's ecosystem—unlike the West—actively punishes this uncertainty.

## Key Points
- Product companies in India face structural disadvantages: ecosystem immaturity, organizational resistance to pivots, talent distrust of unstable ventures.
- Government institutions mirror the broader services-dominant mindset; concepts like product management don't exist in their institutional lexicon or processes.
- National Policy on Software Products is watershed moment to rebalance India's economy from services-centric to product-centric innovation.

## Open Question
If embracing chaos is necessary for great products, but Indian culture and institutions systematically punish ambiguity, how can policy alone shift deeply embedded preferences and behaviours?

## India Angle
India's software industry is dominated by services/outsourcing but lags in product-building; the National Policy on Software Products signals a systemic shift needed to transition India from a services nation to a product nation.
