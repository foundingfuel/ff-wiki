## Core Argument
Bullshit differs from lies: it ignores truth entirely, thriving when people speak beyond their knowledge.

## Key Points
- Bullshitting occurs when speaking obligations exceed actual knowledge—common in democratic public discourse.
- A liar respects truth by denying it; a bullshitter ignores facts entirely, caring only about utility.
- Centre-state tensions narratives mask collaborative governance realities, exemplifying how bullshit shapes India's policy discourse.

## Open Question
If bullshit is inevitable in democracies where citizens feel obligated to have opinions on everything, how can institutions and individuals distinguish signal from noise without expertise?

## India Angle
India's governance and public discourse are awash in bullshit—from Centre-state narratives to Covid policy claims. Understanding the distinction between lies and bullshit is critical for Indian citizens, policymakers, and institutions to navigate misinformation and build institutional trust.
