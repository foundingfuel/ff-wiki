## Core Argument
Digital-first companies collapse traditional globalisation timelines from decades to years through asset-light, internet-enabled models.

## Key Points
- Old model: decades to go global; new model: Uber went from San Francisco (2009) to 55 cities globally by 2014
- Asset-light strategies—licensing tech, online distribution, customer co-creation—enable rapid scaling without heavy local infrastructure
- Regulatory and cultural hurdles remain real threats despite speed; hubris poses equal danger to execution

## Open Question
If speed and technology solve globalisation barriers, why do regulatory and cultural challenges still trip up companies like Uber—and will Xiaomi face similar headwinds?

## India Angle
India represents a critical test market for digital-first global companies. Traditional MNC playbook (Unilever, P&G) took decades; new players like Uber and Xiaomi are expanding to India within years, reshaping how foreign businesses enter the Indian market and what local competition must contend with.
