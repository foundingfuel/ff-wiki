## Core Argument
China's sharing economy boom masks fragile business models sustained by venture capital hype rather than fundamentals.

## Key Points
- Sharing economy term encompasses vastly different models—from Bla Bla Car's peer-to-peer to Mobike's 'Renting 2.0' asset deployment.
- Mobike and Ofo raised $2B+ but operate on capital-intensive asset ownership model, not true asset utilization—unsustainable without continuous funding.
- Market expansion (shared umbrellas, basketballs) signals investor desperation to deploy capital rather than solving genuine underutilization problems.

## Open Question
How do capital-intensive 'sharing' models like bike-sharing survive when venture funding dries up if they don't achieve profitable unit economics?

## India Angle
India's sharing economy (Ola, OYO, Zomato) faces similar challenges of unit economics, regulatory ambiguity, and asset management; China's experience offers cautionary lessons on unsustainable growth models and the need for sustainable business fundamentals before venture capital euphoria.
