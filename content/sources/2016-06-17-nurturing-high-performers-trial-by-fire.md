## Core Argument
High-performer development requires clarity on roles and intent; ambiguity breeds crisis even among the elite.

## Key Points
- Hinault's ambiguous intentions despite public commitment created panic and undermined team cohesion and LeMond's confidence.
- High-pressure environments ('trial by fire') separate capability from character; testing under duress reveals true priorities and reliability.
- Mentorship demands clear boundaries: supporting a successor requires genuine commitment, not psychological games or hedging bets.

## Open Question
Does genuine high-performer development require intentional adversity and testing, or does ambiguity about support destroy the very culture needed for excellence?

## India Angle
Indian organizations often struggle with succession planning and managing mentor-protégé dynamics; this case illustrates how unclear role expectations and ambiguous leadership intentions can destabilize teams, a critical issue as Indian businesses scale and professionalize.
