## Core Argument
Fintech algorithms cannot crack India's credit problem without understanding data gaps and cultural financial behaviour.

## Key Points
- Fintechs assume complete digital data but miss offline transactions in informal economy, creating incomplete customer profiles.
- Alternative data sources remain experimental; banks are cautious about unverified intermediary data due to verification costs and quality concerns.
- Indian financial decision-making shaped by risk aversion, savings mentality, and trust in government—factors algorithms alone cannot address.

## Open Question
How can fintech build reliable credit models when the very populations they target (informal economy workers) operate partially outside digital ecosystems, and their borrowing behaviour is shaped by cultural factors, not just transaction data?

## India Angle
Indian fintech's data-centric credit assessment assumes complete digital transaction trails, but India's informal economy—street vendors, auto drivers, small shops—operates partially offline, creating blind spots that algorithms cannot fill. Cultural factors like risk aversion and trust in government also constrain fintech's ability to disrupt traditional banking.
