## Core Argument
Tata Group's silence on Mistry's dismissal undermines governance, raising questions about shareholder protection and institutional accountability.

## Key Points
- No credible evidence provided by Tata Sons board for Mistry's dismissal, violating due process norms.
- Alleged conflicts of interest in Nano and aviation projects suggest governance failures at highest levels.
- Mistry's public letter exposes systemic issues that internal processes failed to resolve or address transparently.

## Open Question
Does whistleblowing through public disclosure (transparency/sunshine) justify breaching institutional confidentiality, or does it permanently damage trust and institutional capability?

## India Angle
High-profile Tata Group governance crisis exposes gaps in Indian corporate accountability mechanisms and board oversight, setting precedent for how India's largest conglomerates handle internal conflicts and shareholder protection.
