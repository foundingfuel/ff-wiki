## Core Argument
Vision-driven entrepreneurs solve systemic problems by reimagining scale and distribution, not just incremental innovation.

## Key Points
- Ford's breakthrough wasn't engineering superiority but understanding mass-market needs and inventing assembly-line manufacturing to serve them affordably.
- Parle-G case shows how dominant incumbents leverage entrenched distribution networks as competitive moat, forcing new players to navigate regulatory and structural barriers.
- Musk's pattern: pursue problems requiring massive scale investment and complexity management, creating defensible advantages others won't attempt.

## Open Question
Can new entrants with capital and technology overcome legacy distribution advantages in regulated markets, or do structural barriers protect incumbents indefinitely?

## India Angle
India's automotive and retail sectors face similar challenges: how to democratize access (affordability, distribution) while competing against entrenched incumbents. Udaan-Parle case reflects tension between new models and legacy distribution networks—critical for India's B2B retail transformation.
