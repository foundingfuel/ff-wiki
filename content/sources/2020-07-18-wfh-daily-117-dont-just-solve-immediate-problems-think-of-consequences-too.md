## Core Argument
Second-order thinking prevents policy failures; leaders must anticipate unintended consequences before acting.

## Key Points
- Restricting foreign exchange outflows paradoxically reduced inflows; easing controls in 1991 increased reserves.
- Exaggerated activism (Aadhaar leaks, climate change) erodes public trust and undermines legitimate causes.
- Companies like DSM neutralized activist predators by planning ahead, not reacting defensively.

## Open Question
How do policymakers and activists balance urgency of action with avoiding exaggeration that undermines long-term credibility?

## India Angle
Indian policymakers historically suffer from single-stage thinking—exemplified by foreign exchange controls that backfired. This systemic inability to anticipate second-order consequences affects regulation (Aadhaar security), activism, and corporate strategy. Relevant for India's governance maturity and business leadership.
