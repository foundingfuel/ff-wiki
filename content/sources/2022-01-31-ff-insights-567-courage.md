## Core Argument
Courage is pretending to be brave until fear no longer defeats you; it's a learnable mindset, not innate fearlessness.

## Key Points
- Mandela's 'brave front' strategy worked because perceived confidence can shift power dynamics in asymmetrical situations.
- False polarization on social media occurs when moderates self-silence, allowing extremists to dominate and distort reality.
- Omicron's severity picture remains unclear; policymakers must act despite incomplete data, like leaders managing uncertainty.

## Open Question
If courage is performative—just 'pretending to be brave'—when does the pretense become authentic conviction, and what happens when it doesn't convince the other side?

## India Angle
Indian leaders and changemakers—from social entrepreneurs to policymakers navigating institutional resistance—must learn that courage in constrained environments means acting despite fear, not without it. This applies to India's complex regulatory landscape and hierarchical systems.
