## Core Argument
Systems with noisy data and weak theory breed false signals, corrupting understanding and delaying solutions.

## Key Points
- Without strong theoretical foundations, noisy data gets mistaken for meaningful patterns, polluting decision-making.
- Seismology and economics share a critical problem: inability to directly observe root causes, forcing reliance on flawed statistical methods.
- India's labour disruptions reveal gaps where theory and measurement fail—workers like Salil Tripathi pay the price for institutional blindness.

## Open Question
How can policymakers act decisively when foundational theory is weak and data is noisy—and what is the human cost of waiting for better understanding?

## India Angle
India's policymaking and economic analysis suffer from noisy data and underdeveloped theoretical frameworks—evident in labour market disruptions (like Salil Tripathi's story) where systemic failures aren't fully understood, and in sectors where statistical noise drowns out signal, leading to poor regulation and worker vulnerability.
