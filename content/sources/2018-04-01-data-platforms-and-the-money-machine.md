## Core Argument
Platform monopolies extracting user data as currency without consent or value-return represent a systemic problem deeper than business model choice.

## Key Points
- Two-sided platform model treats users as products sold to advertisers, not customers with rights.
- Switching costs and network effects prevent competition from policing data extraction excesses.
- Core unresolved question: who owns personal data and what ownership actually means legally and ethically.

## Open Question
If even switching to freemium models doesn't solve the data extraction problem, what structural or regulatory interventions can genuinely protect users without stifling platform innovation?

## India Angle
India lacks strong data protection frameworks and regulatory oversight; platform monopolies (Facebook, Google) wield disproportionate power over Indian users' data, votes, and consumer behaviour with minimal accountability or local governance capacity.
