## Core Argument
Social platforms may transition from proprietary silos to decentralized open standards, like AOL gave way to the web.

## Key Points
- Centralized platforms (Facebook, Twitter) are proprietary silos unlike open email and web infrastructure that work seamlessly across providers.
- Decentralized social networks face technical challenges around privacy, security, and performance but gain momentum after platform controversies.
- Historical precedent: AOL and CompuServe were swept aside by open web; today's giants may face similar disruption.

## Open Question
If decentralized networks are technically superior and user-preferred, why haven't open standards achieved mainstream adoption despite decades of proposals?

## India Angle
India's social media ecosystem could leapfrog to decentralized models if open standards emerge, avoiding lock-in by proprietary Western platforms and enabling indigenous innovation; relevant for data sovereignty and startup opportunities.
