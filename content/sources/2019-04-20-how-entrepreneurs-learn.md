## Core Argument
Large firms must shift from causal to effectual reasoning to foster true entrepreneurial agility and innovation.

## Key Points
- Functional silos and organizational memory are India's biggest blockers to entrepreneurial thinking in established firms.
- Effectual reasoning (exploratory, network-based) differs from causal reasoning (goal-driven, predictive) that business schools teach.
- Successful entrepreneurs learn through diverse stakeholder conversations and intellectual curiosity, not predetermined plans.

## Open Question
If large firms can't unlearn causal reasoning patterns embedded in decades of success, can exposure to entrepreneur stories alone drive meaningful behavioral change?

## India Angle
Indian large firms struggle with cross-functional silos and organizational memory that prevent agility; learning from startup founders requires unlearning ingrained hierarchical patterns endemic to Indian corporates.
