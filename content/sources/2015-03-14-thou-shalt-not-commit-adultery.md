## Core Argument
Modern marriage's impossible demand for three needs in one person creates systemic conflict and adultery.

## Key Points
- Marriage bundles sex, romantic love, and family-raising into one person, violating natural human needs distribution
- Victorian sexual fidelity ideal emerged with 18th-century prosperity but creates impossible standards for couples
- Adultery stems from structural mismatch between idealized marriage and human psychology, not moral failure

## Open Question
If adultery is psychologically inevitable given marriage's structural contradictions, what institutional alternatives exist without abandoning relationship commitment?

## India Angle
Indian marriages increasingly adopt Victorian ideals of sexual fidelity and romantic love in one person, creating modern pressures; Indian surveys show sex becoming obligation rather than joy, reflecting tension between traditional and contemporary values
