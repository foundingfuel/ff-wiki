## Core Argument
Front-line customer immersion teaches business fundamentals and builds lasting operational wisdom better than credentials alone.

## Key Points
- Indra Nooyi's thread sales role in 1970s Bombay forced mastery of product, customer empathy, and market realities.
- Direct customer interaction—hearing concerns, learning local context—instilled duty of care and long-term relationship thinking.
- Career development in 1970s India required front-line work first; office careers didn't start in boardrooms.
- Humbling door-to-door sales experience shaped lifetime business philosophy and listening skills.

## Open Question
How do modern Indian companies balance rapid scaling with the slower, deeper learning that comes from direct customer immersion, when organizational structures now often insulate leaders from ground realities?

## India Angle
India-specific: illustrates how Indian professionals in the 1970s built foundational business acumen through front-line customer exposure rather than office-based roles—a learning model with relevance for how Indian companies should develop talent today.
