## Core Argument
Aging leaders should transition to wisdom roles rather than resist generational change in organizations.

## Key Points
- Millennials are neurologically wired differently for digital; external adoption doesn't guarantee understanding.
- Successful organizations (Cognizant, Tatas) appointed younger CEOs (38-44) to lead, reducing generational friction.
- Wisdom—neurologically grounded in 50s brains—suits mentoring, counseling, and sense-making roles better than operational leadership.

## Open Question
Can organizational structures truly accommodate dual roles (younger operational leaders + older wisdom advisors), or does hierarchical habit make this integration impossible in practice?

## India Angle
Indian tech and services firms (Mindtree, Cognizant, Tata) are navigating generational divides as they scale. Leaders must rethink succession and role redesign to leverage both millennial digital natives and older executives' wisdom—critical for India's knowledge economy competitiveness.
