## Core Argument
Mega founders succeed through unwavering self-belief and storytelling, not pedigree; Middle India's scale demands problem-lived founders.

## Key Points
- Academic background doesn't determine startup success; network effects from elite colleges create visibility bias in ecosystem.
- Storytelling and reading the room outweigh data-driven pitches; founders need conviction to cut dissenting voices.
- Middle India's 300-400 million people need Tier-3 founders solving lived problems, not metro-based unicorn archetypes.

## Open Question
How can founders maintain the self-belief and conviction required for mega outcomes without becoming disconnected from ground reality or suppressing necessary dissent?

## India Angle
India's startup ecosystem is maturing, but success patterns remain concentrated among IIT/IIM graduates and urban founders. The massive Middle India opportunity (300-400 million people) requires a different founder archetype—those who've lived the problem—to unlock distributed entrepreneurship and economic security beyond metro unicorns.
