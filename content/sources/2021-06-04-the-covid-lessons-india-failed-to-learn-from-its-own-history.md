## Core Argument
India's pandemic response was hampered by collective amnesia about its own epidemic history and lack of scientific curiosity embedded in institutions.

## Key Points
- India forgot 40M deaths (1817-1920) and adopted unfounded 'hygiene immunity' hypothesis instead of evidence-based policy
- Europe's post-Enlightenment scientific culture created institutional memory and systematic learning; India lacks this epistemological foundation
- Public policy lags science due to demand-side failures—when disasters are seen as inevitable rather than manageable, state intervention isn't demanded

## Open Question
If scientific consensus takes decades to emerge even for transmission mechanisms (cholera took 30-40 years), how can India rapidly build the state capacity and scientific culture needed to respond to future pandemics without repeating historical cycles?

## India Angle
India failed to leverage its own pandemic history (1918 flu, cholera, plague) to inform COVID response, contrasting with Europe's systematic institutional memory. This reflects deeper gaps in scientific culture, public health infrastructure, and state capacity that have persisted since colonialism.
