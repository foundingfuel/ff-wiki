## Core Argument
Indian business leaders must reframe capitalism around shared value and social purpose, not just shareholder returns.

## Key Points
- Rising inequality and joblessness despite 25 years of liberalization create social instability and terror recruitment potential.
- Traditional capitalism's focus on quarterly results and short-term shareholder maximization conflicts with sustainable, inclusive growth needed in India.
- Porter's shared value framework offers alternative: businesses innovate to meet community needs while maintaining performance metrics through better tools and techniques.

## Open Question
How can Indian companies adopt long-term, society-focused strategies when quarterly earnings pressures and fiduciary duty doctrines remain institutionalized globally?

## India Angle
Indian business elite must move beyond GDP-obsession and address rising inequality, joblessness, and social fracture; inherited American capitalism model prioritizes quarterly returns over sustainable, inclusive growth addressing India's structural poverty and security challenges.
