## Core Argument
China prioritizes political ideology and technological self-reliance over growth stimulus, signaling systemic economic transformation.

## Key Points
- China stopped using fiscal stimulus despite slowdowns, prioritizing long-term modernization and self-reliance over short-term growth recovery.
- Years of debt-fueled stimulus created unsustainable leverage in real estate and shadow banking, forcing Xi's government to enforce hard deleveraging discipline.
- Xi's policy shift aligns political rejuvenation goals with economic strategy, accepting near-term pain to build immunity from external shocks and reduce vulnerability.

## Open Question
Can China sustain this politically-driven shift toward self-reliance without triggering deeper recession, or will ideological goals eventually force a return to stimulus despite stated intentions?

## India Angle
China's shift from stimulus-driven growth to self-reliance and technological modernization signals a fundamental reordering of global economic dynamics. For India, this means reduced Chinese stimulus may lower commodity prices (beneficial) but also intensified tech competition and potential decoupling effects that impact Indian tech sectors and supply chains. It underscores the importance of India developing indigenous technological capabilities rather than relying on global economic tides.
