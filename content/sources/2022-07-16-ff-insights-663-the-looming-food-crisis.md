## Core Argument
Institutional systems like food buffers reshape collective behavior, reducing hoarding and preventing crises.

## Key Points
- India's 1992 food distribution system and 2013 Food Security Act reduced household hoarding by building confidence in supply guarantees.
- Global food crises require coordinated buffer systems and surplus-sharing agreements, not market-alone solutions.
- Behavioral psychology—not just economics—determines whether systems succeed or fail in preventing catastrophe.

## Open Question
How can countries maintain political will for costly food buffer systems during non-crisis periods when hoarding psychology has already diminished?

## India Angle
India's National Food Security Act and public distribution system demonstrate how institutional design can break psychological hoarding cycles—a model relevant as India faces inflation and global food shocks amid its own agricultural vulnerabilities.
