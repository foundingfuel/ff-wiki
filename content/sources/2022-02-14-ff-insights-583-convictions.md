## Core Argument
Leaders with conviction and intellectual grounding navigate turbulence; reading and principle-driven reasoning matter more than rhetorical skill.

## Key Points
- Lincoln and Roosevelt prioritized reading and deep learning throughout life, shaping their leadership worldview.
- Conviction requires choosing to win arguments because they're right, not because you marshaled persuasive facts.
- Leaders need to understand human nature and the human soul, not just legal precedents or surface-level rules.

## Open Question
How do leaders maintain conviction and moral principles when operating within systems (law, politics, organizations) that reward rhetorical skill and compromise over principle?

## India Angle
Indian leaders and organizations often operate in turbulent environments (policy shifts, economic volatility); understanding how great leaders build conviction and make principled decisions despite pressure is directly applicable to how Indian entrepreneurs and executives navigate ambiguity and competing interests.
