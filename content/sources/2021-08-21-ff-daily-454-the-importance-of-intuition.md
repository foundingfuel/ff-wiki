## Core Argument
Experienced leaders should trust intuition built on deep expertise and accumulated judgment, not dismiss it as unscientific.

## Key Points
- Intuition becomes reliable only after years of deliberate experience and training—it's earned expertise, not gut feeling.
- Expert intuition operates faster than conscious analysis when stakes are high; Sully's Hudson River landing exemplifies this.
- Organizational leaders must create psychological safety for diverse decision-making styles, including intuitive thinking and introversion.

## Open Question
How do organizations differentiate between valuable intuition earned through experience versus cognitive biases masquerading as gut instinct?

## India Angle
Indian managers often over-rely on analytical frameworks inherited from Western management training; recognizing intuition as valid could improve decision-making in startups and large organizations navigating India's complex, ambiguous business environment.
