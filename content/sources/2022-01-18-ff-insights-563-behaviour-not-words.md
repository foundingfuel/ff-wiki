## Core Argument
Real change requires consistent behavior change, not words; intentions without actions remain unfulfilled promises.

## Key Points
- People confuse thoughts, wishes, and statements with actual behavioral change; words are not reliable communication.
- Sustained behavior modification is slow, incremental, and requires sustained effort—like a prison escape requiring months of planning.
- Actions define us more than declarations; discrepancy between what we say and do reflects misplaced focus on verbal commitments.

## Open Question
If behavioral change is slow and incremental, how do institutions and societies enforce accountability when people genuinely believe their stated intentions but fail to follow through?

## India Angle
Indian policymakers and leaders often rely on announcements and verbal commitments without measuring actual behavioral change—visible in pandemic response inconsistencies, regulatory compliance gaps, and development program outcomes. The gap between stated intent and implementation is a core governance challenge in India.
