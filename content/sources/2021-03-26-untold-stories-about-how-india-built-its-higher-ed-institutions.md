## Core Argument
India's world-class institutions were built on autonomy, integrity, and principled leadership; we risk losing this legacy.

## Key Points
- Visionary founders like Homi Bhabha, Satish Dhawan, PK Kelkar prioritized institutional mission over personal glory and enforced non-negotiable principles consistently.
- Autonomy in academic and administrative decisions, combined with financial dependence on government, created accountability without micromanagement.
- Decentralized governance structures—student senates, faculty committees with real authority—embedded ownership and diverse perspectives in decision-making.

## Open Question
How can modern Indian higher education institutions balance institutional autonomy with government funding dependency while protecting academic freedom under evolving political and regulatory pressures?

## India Angle
India's premier institutions (IITs, IIMs, TIFR, IISc) were built on principles of autonomy, integrity, and decentralized decision-making in the 1950s-80s. Current challenges at institutions like Ashoka University suggest these foundational principles are eroding, raising questions about how modern Indian higher education can reclaim this legacy.
