## Core Argument
Tax breaks don't drive meaningful R&D; competitive market intensity and science-industry integration do.

## Key Points
- R&D motivated by external incentives creates vanity projects disconnected from business needs.
- Competitive marketplace pressure, not subsidies, forces companies to innovate meaningfully.
- Indian pharma's success came from patent law reform and mutual respect between science and industry leaders, not tax policy.

## Open Question
If competitive market intensity is the real driver of R&D, how can government create such intensity without distorting markets or favoring incumbents over disruptors?

## India Angle
India's R&D policy relies on tax incentives, but the author argues this is ineffective. Instead, India should build competitive intensity in markets and strengthen links between science institutions and industry—as evidenced by the pharma sector's success post-patent law changes.
