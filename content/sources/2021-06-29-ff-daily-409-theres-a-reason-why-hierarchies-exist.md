## Core Argument
Hierarchies persist because they reduce cognitive load and mediate ability development unequally across social strata.

## Key Points
- Hierarchies enable strangers to interact efficiently without personal knowledge, using social cues and assumptions.
- Most abilities require nurturing; access to that nurturing depends on one's place in society's imagined hierarchy.
- Same abilities yield unequal success when different rules apply to different classes—legal and informal glass ceilings rig the game.

## Open Question
If hierarchies serve a functional purpose in enabling coordination among strangers, how can organizations dismantle unjust hierarchies without losing efficiency or creating paralysis in decision-making?

## India Angle
Indian hierarchies—caste, class, colonial legacies—still mediate opportunity and success; understanding how imagined hierarchies function is essential for building inclusive organizations and breaking systemic glass ceilings that persist post-independence.
