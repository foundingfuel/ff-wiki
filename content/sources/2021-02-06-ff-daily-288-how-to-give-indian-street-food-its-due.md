## Core Argument
Formalizing Indian street food through training, hygiene standards, and government support can unlock economic multiplier effects while protecting livelihoods.

## Key Points
- Street vendors are migrant workers sustaining millions; pandemic exposed vulnerability without formal support systems.
- Singapore's UNESCO recognition of hawkers as cultural heritage offers a model for India to legitimize its street food sector.
- Simple rules and systems (Toyota's kanban principle) can manage complex informal economies without heavy bureaucracy.

## Open Question
How can India balance formalizing street food—through training, hygiene, legality templates—without dismantling the very informality and flexibility that makes it economically resilient for millions of marginal workers?

## India Angle
Indian street food sector employs millions, particularly migrants, but lacks formal recognition and government support that Singapore's hawkers received via UNESCO heritage status. Formalizing and legitimizing this informal economy could unlock economic multiplier effects and livelihood protection.
