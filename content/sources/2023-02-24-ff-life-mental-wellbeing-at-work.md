## Core Argument
Mentally healthy workplaces require systemic culture shifts, not token interventions—balancing excellence with compassion and psychological safety.

## Key Points
- Work is central to mental wellbeing; organisations can either amplify stress or provide therapeutic space for vulnerable sharing.
- Psychological safety and trust enable people to manage emotions, take risks, and innovate without fear of penalisation.
- Talented high-performers often self-inflict stress through competitiveness; systemic support for emotional regulation is critical.

## Open Question
How do organisations maintain performance excellence and competitive drive while reducing the self-inflicted stress cultures that attract and reward 'alphas'—without compromising either?

## India Angle
Indian workplaces—particularly in competitive sectors like IT, consulting, and manufacturing—struggle with high-stress cultures and work-life integration. This discussion is crucial for Indian leaders rethinking toxic productivity norms and building psychologically safe, inclusive workplaces that still drive excellence.
