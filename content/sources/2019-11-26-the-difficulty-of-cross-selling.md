## Core Argument
Cross-selling magic that works in one domain often fails when competing in traditional markets elsewhere.

## Key Points
- Platforms assume competitive advantages in one domain transfer seamlessly to others, but market dynamics and competitors often prove otherwise.
- Publishers and businesses pursuing platform diversification face diminishing returns; loyalty and owned properties matter more than algorithmic reach.
- Truecaller's journey from spam-blocking to fintech shows the tension between core competency and adjacency—expansion doesn't guarantee profitability or user adoption.

## Open Question
If a platform's 'magic' only works in niche conditions, how do companies know when expansion into adjacent markets is viable versus wasteful?

## India Angle
Indian startups (Swiggy, Zomato, Truecaller) are attempting cross-selling strategies with mixed results; Uber's India operations exemplify the challenge of replicating US success in local markets with entrenched competitors.
