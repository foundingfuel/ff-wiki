## Core Argument
Systems transformation requires ethical toolkit combining systems thinking, ethical reasoning, and collaborative design—not just economic growth.

## Key Points
- Scientific specialization fragmented knowledge; solving systemic problems needs whole-systems thinking integrating physical and social sciences.
- Economics' emphasis on self-interest conflicts with Golden Rule ethics; excessive mathematization strips human values from social analysis.
- Young and experienced leaders are redefining success from material self-interest to justice, ecological sustainability, and intergenerational welfare.

## Open Question
How do we reconcile the immediate material needs of India's struggling millions with long-term ecological constraints and intergenerational justice when economic incentives fundamentally reward short-term extraction?

## India Angle
India faces dual crises: water scarcity (demand doubling while resources deplete) and development inequality. Current economic growth paradigm won't solve these systemic problems; India needs ethical frameworks that balance development with ecological sustainability and intergenerational equity.
