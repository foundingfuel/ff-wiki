## Core Argument
Mindful technology use requires intentional design of personal digital boundaries and systemic alternatives to attention-economy business models.

## Key Points
- Internet's advertising model created perverse incentive to maximize user engagement, embedding attention-capture into technology design itself.
- Digital hygiene practices—separating devices by function, limiting notifications, using phones as mirrors not windows—restore agency and calm.
- Digital public goods architecture (Aadhaar, UPI) redistributes power from monopolies to competitive markets, benefiting consumers and suppliers equally.

## Open Question
How do individuals practice mindfulness with technology while the underlying business models remain fundamentally designed to capture attention—is personal discipline enough without systemic change?

## India Angle
India's digital public infrastructure (Aadhaar, UPI) offers an alternative model to winner-takes-all platforms, distributing power and innovation benefits broadly rather than concentrating them—a distinctly Indian approach to technology governance.
