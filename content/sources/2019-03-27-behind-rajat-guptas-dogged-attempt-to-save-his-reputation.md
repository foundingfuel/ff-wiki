## Core Argument
Convicted insider trader Rajat Gupta seeks reputation recovery through selective narrative control rather than accountability.

## Key Points
- Gupta's defense strategy involves building detailed counter-narratives that his lawyers prevented him from presenting in court testimony.
- His fall generated four distinct reactions: amazement, derision, disillusionment from mentees, and frustration at his continued denial of wrongdoing.
- The interview piece grapples with whether to amplify his story-telling or interrogate its credibility and emotional authenticity.

## Open Question
Can a high-profile leader rehabilitate their reputation through storytelling and selective evidence presentation without genuine admission of wrongdoing, and should platforms grant them that opportunity?

## India Angle
Rajat Gupta's fall from grace has profound implications for Indian business leaders and institutions like McKinsey India, ISB, and corporate mentorship culture; his reputation struggle reflects broader questions about accountability and redemption in India's business ecosystem.
