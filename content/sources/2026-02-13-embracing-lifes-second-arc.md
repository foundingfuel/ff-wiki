## Core Argument
Life's second arc demands learning to let go and rewrite success scripts to move from achievement to meaning.

## Key Points
- Acquisition-focused first arc must give way to letting go; most societies don't teach this critical transition.
- Second arc arrives as quiet inner dissonance—external success masking internal incompleteness, often peaking in 45-50s.
- Modern elderhood combines wisdom with curiosity; identity shifts from self-building to service and generative contribution.

## Open Question
How can societies and organizations better prepare people for life's second arc when inherited success metrics no longer apply, especially given different gender anxieties (male irrelevance vs. female invisibility)?

## India Angle
Indian professionals face unique tension between inherited success scripts (education, career, status) and emerging questions of purpose; traditional concepts like vanaprastha offer cultural frameworks for second-arc transitions, but modern life has blurred these lifestage boundaries, leaving many without guidance.
