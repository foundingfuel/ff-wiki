## Core Argument
Yum's China success relied on localization and cultural understanding; food safety crises revealed vulnerabilities in supply chain trust.

## Key Points
- Yum localized aggressively by hiring local talent, understanding culture, and adapting menus rather than transplanting headquarters models
- Food safety scandals in 2012-2014 eroded consumer trust despite standardization that once differentiated Yum from local competitors
- Aspirational branding and novelty drove early explosive growth as Western fast food represented modernization and status for Chinese consumers

## Open Question
If standardization and localization were both key to Yum's success, why did standardized supply chains become a liability when food safety scandals emerged?

## India Angle
Indian MNCs expanding internationally face similar localization challenges as Yum did in China; food safety and supply chain trust remain critical for QSR growth in India; understanding guanxi-like relationship ecosystems is relevant for Indian businesses entering new markets
