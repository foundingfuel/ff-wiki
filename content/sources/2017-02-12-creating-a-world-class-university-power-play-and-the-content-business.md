## Core Argument
World-class Indian universities require autonomy, merit-based hiring, and freedom; traditional content thrives through creative adaptation.

## Key Points
- IISc's success stems from autonomy, meritocracy, and hiring best talent globally—not government funding alone.
- Private universities can attract foreign faculty if they solve working conditions and India's brand perception barriers.
- Content's value hasn't diminished; disruption offers expansion opportunities through contextual adjacencies, not universal prescriptions.

## Open Question
Can India simultaneously decentralize university funding and maintain quality standards without creating a two-tier system where only well-funded private institutions thrive?

## India Angle
India's ambitious plan to build 20 world-class universities requires understanding autonomy, funding models, and institutional culture—IISc proves it's possible but demands patience, deep pockets, and freedom from state micromanagement.
