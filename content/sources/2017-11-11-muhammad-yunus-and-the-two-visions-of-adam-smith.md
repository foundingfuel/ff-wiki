## Core Argument
Capitalism needs fundamental restructuring toward social business, not just philanthropy, to eliminate poverty and inequality.

## Key Points
- Yunus argues social business—profit secondary to social impact—can fix capitalism's wealth concentration problem.
- Three megaforces needed: youth empowerment, technology democratization, and good governance with human rights protection.
- Legal and financial frameworks designed by incumbent businesses must expand to accommodate social enterprises.

## Open Question
If capitalism's tendency to concentrate wealth is a natural law (as the Duke engineer argues), can legal and institutional reforms truly redirect it toward social good, or is systemic restructuring fundamentally insufficient?

## India Angle
India's inequality and poverty reduction depend on whether capitalism can be reformed through social business models and better governance—Yunus's framework directly applies to India's development challenges and institutional gaps.
