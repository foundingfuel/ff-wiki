## Core Argument
India's entrepreneurship surge masks deep geographic inequality in business creation across districts.

## Key Points
- 132,000 new companies created in 21 months (6,200/month), but concentrated in few cities.
- Geographic divide reveals entrepreneurship is not reaching most of India's districts equitably.
- Media hype obscures reality that startup ecosystem remains clustered, limiting national impact.

## Open Question
If entrepreneurship is genuinely accelerating nationally, why is it so concentrated geographically—and what would it take to distribute it?

## India Angle
India's entrepreneurship boom is heavily concentrated in a few metros (likely Delhi, Bangalore, Mumbai), leaving most districts far behind—a critical gap for inclusive growth and regional economic development.
