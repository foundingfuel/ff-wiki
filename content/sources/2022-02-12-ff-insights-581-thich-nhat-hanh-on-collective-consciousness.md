## Core Argument
Collective consciousness shapes individual behaviour; awareness of what we consume—information, volition, relationships—determines societal health.

## Key Points
- Four nutriments feed us daily: edible food, sense impressions, volition, and collective consciousness; each can be nourishing or toxic.
- Successful social movements like RTI and MGNREGA required building broad coalitions across media, academia, economists to shift collective consciousness.
- Awareness of consumption patterns—media, goals, group influence—is the key protection against unhealthy individual and collective nourishment.

## Open Question
If collective consciousness shapes individual behaviour so deeply, how do individuals resist or transform unhealthy group consciousness without leaving the system?

## India Angle
Aruna Roy's case study shows how Indian social movements succeed by building collective consciousness across diverse constituencies—media, academia, civil society—essential for policy change in India's complex democratic ecosystem.
