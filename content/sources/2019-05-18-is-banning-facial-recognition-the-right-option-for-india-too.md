## Core Argument
India must chart its own facial recognition policy, balancing genuine state capacity needs against surveillance risks, rather than importing binary Western solutions.

## Key Points
- Technology has real benefits in India's under-resourced policing context but carries major misuse and bias risks.
- Binary ban-or-embrace debate obscures middle-ground options like moratoriums, regulated deployment, and transparency frameworks.
- India's poor track record on tech regulation (Aadhaar delays, data protection confusion) demands homegrown solutions, not Western policy mimicry.

## Open Question
How can India's genuinely under-resourced police force address critical cases (missing children, unidentified bodies) without enabling the surveillance state risks that bans aim to prevent?

## India Angle
India faces unique pressures: severe police-population ratio, 75,000+ unidentified bodies, missing children cases. Unlike the US, India needs tailored regulation accounting for state capacity gaps and Aadhaar's existing biometric infrastructure, not blanket bans or uncritical Western policy imports.
