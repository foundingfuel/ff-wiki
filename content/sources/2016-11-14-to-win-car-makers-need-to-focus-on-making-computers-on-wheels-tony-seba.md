## Core Argument
Automakers winning in EVs must become software companies building 'computers on wheels,' not just electric vehicles.

## Key Points
- Legacy automakers underestimate speed of disruption; by 2025, all cars will be electric—incremental EV plans guarantee failure.
- The real disruption is software-driven: autonomous vehicles and car-sharing eliminate manufacturer-consumer relationships entirely.
- Incumbent business models (selling cars, not services) create cognitive lock; making EVs is easy—letting go of cash cows is hard.

## Open Question
If car manufacturers lose direct consumer relationships to Uber/Lyft and must compete on software, can traditional automakers execute this reinvention faster than tech companies entering mobility—and what does this mean for India's automotive ecosystem?

## India Angle
India's software expertise positions it uniquely to lead in automotive software/AI for EVs and autonomous vehicles—but Indian automakers must recognize this is a software-first disruption, not just manufacturing. OEMs risk repeating incumbents' mistakes by treating EVs as incremental rather than transformative.
