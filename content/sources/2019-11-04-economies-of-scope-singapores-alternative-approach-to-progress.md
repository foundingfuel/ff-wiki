## Core Argument
Economies of scope—not scale—drive progress through decentralized coordination closer to ground-level problem-solving.

## Key Points
- Singapore achieved rapid development through 'economies of scope': decentralized coordination and local problem-solving rather than centralized scale.
- Liveable cities require citizen participation in local governance of schools, health, and services via community centres, not just smart technology.
- Systems thinking education across disciplines develops integrated problem-solving capability essential for navigating complex interdependencies.

## Open Question
How can India transition from deeply entrenched centralized bureaucratic systems to genuinely decentralized, participatory local governance without diluting accountability or equity?

## India Angle
India must shift from centralized, scale-based approaches to local systems solutions for complex problems like urban development, health, and skill formation. Singapore's model offers a blueprint for decentralized governance and community participation that could address India's governance challenges and inclusive growth needs.
