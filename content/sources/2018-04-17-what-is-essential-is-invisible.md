## Core Argument
Complex change requires listening to invisible stakeholders; binary discourse obscures essential truths underlying transformation.

## Key Points
- Public debates collapse into false binaries ('for us or against us') during periods of major institutional change.
- Understanding stakeholder perspectives—protagonist, architect, beneficiary, recipient, afflicted—reveals hidden complexity invisible to surface discourse.
- Truth has a half-life; ideas and institutions must constantly re-examine assumptions as data and information environments evolve.

## Open Question
How do leaders navigate major institutional transitions when the public discourse is polarized and prevents genuine multi-perspective engagement?

## India Angle
India's public discourse—especially around polarizing issues like Aadhaar—reflects a false binary that ignores nuance and multiple stakeholder perspectives. Indian policymakers and leaders must move beyond binary thinking to navigate complex institutional change effectively.
