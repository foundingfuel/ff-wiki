## Core Argument
Governments worldwide grapple with regulating Big Tech monopolies using outdated frameworks and unclear legal processes.

## Key Points
- Twitter's intermediary status in India remains unclear legally; no formal revocation process exists under IT Act.
- Lina Khan's FTC appointment signals US shift: questioning whether lower prices alone justify monopoly tolerance.
- Amazon Prime's 'free shipping' model masks underlying costs shifted to sellers, exemplifying modern monopoly tactics.

## Open Question
How can governments regulate digital monopolies effectively when legal frameworks are ambiguous, enforcement mechanisms unclear, and traditional antitrust thinking is proving inadequate for platform economies?

## India Angle
India's government-Twitter conflict mirrors global Big Tech regulation struggles; Indian policymakers lack clear legal frameworks for intermediary status revocation, creating regulatory ambiguity that affects platform operations and free speech.
