## Core Argument
Gen-Z's digital-native fluency and preference for independence will reshape post-Covid workplace culture and remote collaboration norms.

## Key Points
- Gen-Z sees no distinction between online and offline relationships, giving them advantage in virtual work environments.
- Independence and flexibility matter more than isolation; Gen-Z thrives with autonomy in scheduling and task execution.
- Millennials copy physical-world interactions onto Zoom; Gen-Z creates new digital collaboration models using gaming and tools like Slack.

## Open Question
Does giving Gen-Z preference for independence and virtual-first work erode organizational cohesion and informal knowledge transfer that happened in physical offices?

## India Angle
Indian companies transitioning to hybrid/remote work must understand Gen-Z expectations for flexibility and digital-native collaboration, especially as they enter the workforce in large numbers across metros and tier-2 cities.
