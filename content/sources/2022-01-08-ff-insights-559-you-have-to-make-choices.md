## Core Argument
Meaningful lives require deliberate sacrifice of conveniences, not just elimination of tedium.

## Key Points
- Convenience culture creates illusion that we can have everything by removing only tedious tasks, but this is false.
- Radical choices like Sylvia Keesmaat's farm move demonstrate that meaning requires giving things up, not optimizing time.
- Resisting convenience-first thinking requires fortitude because society increasingly questions those who choose inconvenient paths.

## Open Question
How can individuals and organizations operating within convenience-dependent systems (digital infrastructure, market expectations) authentically choose meaningful inconvenience without becoming isolated or dysfunctional?

## India Angle
Indian professionals face acute time scarcity due to rapid urbanization and always-on work culture; the tension between convenience and meaning is particularly acute for knowledge workers navigating career transitions and family obligations in a high-pressure economy.
