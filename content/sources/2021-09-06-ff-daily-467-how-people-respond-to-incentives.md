## Core Argument
People rationally optimize behavior in response to incentives; poorly designed policies create unintended consequences.

## Key Points
- Incentive structures drive rational self-interested behavior, not stupidity or tradition
- Short-term policy impacts are small, but behavioral responses compound over time into systemic changes
- Policymakers must anticipate second-order effects before implementing interventions to avoid counterproductive outcomes

## Open Question
How can policymakers design interventions that account for all possible behavioral adaptations without becoming paralyzed by complexity?

## India Angle
India's policymakers must understand how citizens respond to incentive structures—from tax design to public health campaigns to regulatory frameworks. The rat-tail bounty and kerosene adulteration examples directly parallel India's policy implementation challenges across healthcare, sanitation, and taxation.
