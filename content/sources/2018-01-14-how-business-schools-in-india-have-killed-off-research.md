## Core Argument
Indian business schools must bridge theory-practice gap by recruiting industry practitioners as faculty to restore research relevance.

## Key Points
- Business schools should blend academic theory with industry practice; curriculum should reflect real organizational challenges, not academic agendas.
- India's emphasis on PhD-holding faculty over experienced practitioners creates knowledge disconnect; global schools value clinical faculty with dual expertise.
- Historical Indian business school professors (Bhattacharya, Pareek) were respected consultants shaping industry thought; current model has abandoned this model.

## Open Question
How can business schools maintain academic rigor and research standards while prioritizing industry-relevant practitioners who may lack traditional doctoral credentials?

## India Angle
Indian business schools have drifted from their core purpose of serving industry through relevant research and thought leadership. Unlike global peers, they prioritize academic credentials over practitioner expertise, creating a disconnect between curriculum and real business challenges facing Indian organizations.
