## Core Argument
India must reconnect with Buddhism as lived practice, not historical artifact, to reclaim civilisational influence.

## Key Points
- Buddhism thrives in East Asia as daily practice while thinning in India despite originating here.
- The Piprahwa relics exhibition represents temporary gathering, not possession or triumphalism—emphasizing sharing over reclamation.
- Dispersal across institutions and borders has fragmented Buddhist heritage, requiring intentional reunion and reinterpretation for modern India.

## Open Question
How can India authentically reconnect with Buddhism as lived spiritual practice when it has largely been absent from everyday Indian life for centuries, and what does 'reclamation' mean without repeating colonial or nationalist frameworks?

## India Angle
India's estrangement from Buddhism as lived practice while other Asian cultures preserve it actively; reclaiming inherited civilisational knowledge through exhibitions and reconnection with diaspora teachings.
