## Core Argument
Hedonic adaptation makes wealth, power, and fame ineffective happiness drivers; pursuing intrinsic goals yields better outcomes.

## Key Points
- Lottery winners return to baseline happiness within two years due to psychological adaptation to new circumstances.
- Materialistic people experience higher rates of mental disorders; long-term happiness depends on intrinsic, not extrinsic goals.
- Expenses and needs expand with income, creating treadmill effect where higher earnings fail to improve well-being.

## Open Question
If happiness doesn't come from material success, what alternative framework should guide career and life decisions for ambitious professionals?

## India Angle
Indian professionals and entrepreneurs often chase wealth and status as markers of success; this challenges the cultural narrative that financial achievement guarantees life satisfaction, relevant for career decisions and organizational purpose-building.
