## Core Argument
Economic growth magnifies geopolitical exposure; hedging works only when shocks are temporary, not prolonged instability.

## Key Points
- India's strategy of de-hyphenation (balanced relationships with Israel, Gulf states, Iran) assumes compartmentalisation that war makes impossible.
- West Asia supplies 19% of imports, absorbs 44% of jewellery exports, hosts 27M workers; conflict threatens entire value chains simultaneously.
- Hedging architecture depends on temporary shocks; prolonged conflict risks unpredictable interaction between economic, energy, and diplomatic layers.

## Open Question
How can India maintain genuine strategic autonomy when the region enabling that autonomy—West Asia—is structurally prone to systemic instability that collapses compartmentalised relationships?

## India Angle
India's strategic autonomy strategy faces acute stress from US-Israel-Iran conflict: energy security (90% import-dependent, 50% via Hormuz), $178.56B Gulf trade, 27M diaspora workers, and Chabahar connectivity ambitions all vulnerable to prolonged West Asia instability.
