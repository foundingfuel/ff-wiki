## Core Argument
Internet's anonymity and algorithmic commodification of human creativity have perverted its original ideals of empowerment.

## Key Points
- Platforms replaced genuine human need with manufactured demand, hiding true sources of creativity behind aggregation.
- Anonymity and standardized design shifted internet from proud self-expression to fragmented, monetizable content.
- Thinking deeply before acting prevents costly consequences; mental models like inversion help better decision-making.

## Open Question
If the internet's structural incentives inevitably corrupt good intentions, can individual or policy interventions realistically reverse this perversion?

## India Angle
India's emerging digital economy and regulatory framework must learn from how unchecked tech platforms erode trust and autonomy; relevant to India's data protection and social media governance debates
