## Core Argument
Thought patterns shape actions and circumstances; mindful mental cultivation precedes behavioural change and wellbeing.

## Key Points
- Thoughts physically manifest in appearance and behaviour; negative thoughts attract unfavourable circumstances
- Digital addiction in India is real but nuanced—rural-urban divide, gender gaps, and emotional distress are key factors
- NIMHANS addiction clinic reports rising cases; work/academic pressure and proximity to devices create dependent behaviour

## Open Question
If thought shapes circumstance, how do we break cycles for those trapped by structural constraints like poverty or lack of digital access?

## India Angle
Indian phone usage patterns show rural-urban and gender divides; NIMHANS clinic sees rising digital addiction cases, mostly urban; rural beneficiaries struggle with basic digital access while some urban users skip meals for gaming.
