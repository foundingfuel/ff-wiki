## Core Argument
Impostor syndrome, when reframed, fuels preparation and growth rather than paralyzes performance.

## Key Points
- Defensive pessimism—anticipating worst outcomes—paradoxically drives better preparation and peak performance.
- Self-doubt is especially common among women and marginalized groups; it can unlock creative problem-solving.
- Leadership vulnerability and candor, modeled early, creates psychological safety for diverse teams to thrive.

## Open Question
How do we distinguish between impostor syndrome that motivates versus that which paralyzes—and is the line individual or contextual?

## India Angle
Indian professionals, especially women and minorities navigating corporate hierarchies, can apply Grant's frameworks to reframe self-doubt as a productivity tool rather than a liability—relevant for India's growing management talent pool.
