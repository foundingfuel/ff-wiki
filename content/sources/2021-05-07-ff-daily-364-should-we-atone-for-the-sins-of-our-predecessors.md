## Core Argument
Nations must decide if collective moral responsibility transcends individual culpability and generational boundaries.

## Key Points
- Moral individualism argues individuals cannot atone for predecessors' sins absent explicit personal commitment.
- Public apologies honor victims, recognize persistent injustice effects, and attempt institutional atonement for historical wrongs.
- Question of whether governments should be morally neutral remains unresolved and foundational to justice frameworks.

## Open Question
How can a society balance individual moral agency with collective institutional responsibility for historical injustices without either absolving systems or burdening innocents?

## India Angle
India's context of historical injustices, caste-based discrimination, and colonial legacies makes this question of institutional/collective apology and moral responsibility acutely relevant for governance, nation-building, and social cohesion.
