## Core Argument
AI natives redefine technological fluency through experimentation; non-adopters risk obsolescence without mindset shifts.

## Key Points
- AI natives like HudZah demonstrate intuitive, experimental engagement with AI tools as integrated partners, not just utilities.
- Adoption follows Diffusion of Innovations curve; most are laggards/majority waiting for forced change rather than early adopters.
- The real risk is not AI itself but growing gap between AI immigrants and natives, creating workforce inequality without deliberate intervention.

## Open Question
If AI adoption is behaviorally driven and requires experimental mindset, how do organizations force or encourage this shift in risk-averse cultures without coercion or widening inequality?

## India Angle
Indian professionals and organizations risk being left behind in a rapidly AI-native world; requires urgency in upskilling, cultural shift from resistance to experimentation, and inclusion of non-innovators in AI adoption to prevent a two-tier workforce divide.
