## Core Argument
Shift from consent-based to rights-based data protection model, with adaptive regulation assessing harms rather than predicting them.

## Key Points
- Consent model favors data controllers; rights model shifts burden of privacy risk evaluation to data collectors.
- India's unprecedented path of data-richness without prior economic development requires novel legal frameworks.
- Regulation must assess imbalances created by technology and respond in small, regular intervals rather than predict harms in advance.

## Open Question
How can regulators craft meaningful data protection frameworks when technology evolves faster than legal systems can assess harms, and no precedent exists for protecting a data-rich but economically poor nation?

## India Angle
India faces unique challenge of becoming data-rich while economically developing—without established privacy norms—requiring adaptive legal frameworks that protect citizens before harms crystallize, not after.
