## Core Argument
Privacy protection in India extends beyond Aadhaar to government surveillance, tech companies, and constitutional rights across diverse domains.

## Key Points
- Right to privacy relevant to homosexuality laws, medical records, police action, and tech company data practices—not just Aadhaar.
- India operates five government surveillance programmes with minimal public scrutiny: Central Monitoring System, NATGRID, Netra, and others.
- Lack of transparency about surveillance systems makes it impossible to assess true privacy invasion or enforce accountability.

## Open Question
How can India balance legitimate security needs (post-2008 Mumbai attacks) with democratic accountability and privacy protection when surveillance programmes operate with minimal public oversight?

## India Angle
India's privacy framework is fragmented across Aadhaar, surveillance programmes, and constitutional interpretation—requiring comprehensive regulation beyond identity systems. Government surveillance programmes like NATGRID and CMS lack public accountability, a critical governance gap for India's democratic institutions.
