## Core Argument
Indian e-commerce enters a new phase requiring frictionless commerce, customer-centric models, and novel logistics for untapped markets.

## Key Points
- Snapdeal rejected Flipkart merger to pursue independent profitability path, signaling founder autonomy over investor pressure.
- SoftBank's $2.5B Flipkart bet reflects investor confidence in winner-take-most consolidation within India's e-commerce.
- Next growth lies in India II/III markets requiring innovations in logistics, commerce friction, and localized business models.

## Open Question
Can Flipkart's scale and capital advantage drive profitability in underserved markets faster than independent competitors like Snapdeal can innovate their way there?

## India Angle
Indian e-commerce's competitive dynamics and the challenge of profitability in India II/III markets; how Indian companies like Flipkart and Snapdeal must innovate differently than Western counterparts to address unique logistical and customer adoption barriers.
