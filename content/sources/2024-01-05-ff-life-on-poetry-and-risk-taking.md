## Core Argument
Ghazal poetry mirrors entrepreneurial psychology, offering wisdom for navigating startup's emotional and strategic challenges.

## Key Points
- Startups mirror unrequited love: asymmetric odds, perseverance through pain, oscillating hope and despair.
- Poetry reframes founder experiences—from optimism bias to envy to pivoting—through Ghalib, Faiz, and classical Urdu verse.
- Risk-taking in startups mirrors the ghazal lover's vulnerability; both demand acceptance of loss as part of the journey.

## Open Question
If poetry helps entrepreneurs endure pain and accept failure, does it also risk romanticizing poor decisions and delaying necessary pivots?

## India Angle
Uses Urdu/Persian ghazal poetry—a distinctly subcontinental literary tradition—to frame entrepreneurial experiences, showing how Indian founders can draw on indigenous cultural wisdom rather than Western management frameworks.
