## Core Argument
Transparency and information access dramatically reduce public fund leakages; Aadhaar extends this principle to India's subsidy system.

## Key Points
- Uganda increased school fund delivery from 13% to 90% through publishing expenditure data in newspapers and radio.
- Information asymmetry enabled embezzlement; transparency shifted bargaining power to beneficiaries and deterred officials.
- Aadhaar aims to replicate Uganda's approach at scale across India's complex subsidy ecosystem with greater systemic risk.

## Open Question
Can transparency mechanisms that work at small scale (Uganda's schools) be effectively implemented at national scale (Aadhaar) without creating new exclusion risks or governance challenges?

## India Angle
India's Aadhaar system applies Uganda's transparency lesson to tackle systemic leakages in subsidies and public spending, but at a vastly larger scale with contested implications for citizen rights and inclusion.
