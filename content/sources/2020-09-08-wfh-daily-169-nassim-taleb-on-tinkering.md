## Core Argument
Success comes from aggressive tinkering and trial-and-error, not top-down planning, especially in uncertain times.

## Key Points
- Tinkering and aggressive trial-and-error beat prediction; luck compounds through repeated exposure to opportunities.
- Working with family succeeds when core values align; mentorship spans role transitions and career changes.
- Finding mentors remotely requires specificity, genuine reconnection, and building relationships before asking for help.

## Open Question
How do you balance the need for consistent execution and long-term planning with a philosophy of constant tinkering and pivoting, especially in family businesses where stability matters?

## India Angle
Indian entrepreneurs and family-business leaders can adopt Taleb's tinkering philosophy to navigate high uncertainty; the Lumiere example shows how Indian family enterprises can blend values alignment with flexible execution for sustained growth.
