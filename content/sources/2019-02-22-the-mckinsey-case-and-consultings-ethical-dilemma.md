## Core Argument
Consulting firms abandoned trusted advisor missions for wealth-maximizing ventures, creating ethical compromises.

## Key Points
- McKinsey and peers shifted from objective advisory to profit-maximizing investment vehicles for partners.
- 1990s capitalism culture ('greed is good') incentivized consultants to pursue personal wealth over client interests.
- Firms created complex webs of ventures that blurred lines between professional duty and financial self-interest.

## Open Question
How can professional service firms retain talent and compete financially while maintaining the ethical integrity that justified their premium positioning in the first place?

## India Angle
Indian consulting firms and business leaders face similar pressures to balance wealth creation with ethical advisory roles; as India's consulting sector grows, these conflicts of interest become increasingly relevant to Indian enterprises and their governance standards.
