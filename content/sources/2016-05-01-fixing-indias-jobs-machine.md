## Core Argument
India must redesign jobs policy around five converging drivers: technology, enterprise models, social security, skills, and regulation.

## Key Points
- Technology and automation are making workers redundant faster than new opportunities emerge, including disruptive models like Uber.
- New enterprise forms (Uber, Airbnb) blur employment relationships, raising unfunded social security obligations for workers without formal contracts.
- Skills development must shift from catching up to Industry 2.0 while simultaneously preparing for Industry 4.0, requiring businesses to invest despite short-term contracts.

## Open Question
How can India balance regulatory freedom for innovation and job creation with demands for worker protections and skill investment when businesses explicitly avoid long-term employment relationships?

## India Angle
India faces a crisis of 280 million job seekers entering the market by 2050 amid fragmented policy powers, outdated agriculture-tech coexistence, and competing demands for worker protections versus entrepreneurial freedom—unique to India's scale and complexity.
