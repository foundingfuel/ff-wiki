## Core Argument
India's political and social institutions struggle to reconcile merit, equality, and solidarity amid inequality and partisan division.

## Key Points
- West Bengal election reflects BJP's consolidation of anti-incumbency anger, risking edge through defector influx while TMC relies on local roots and state machinery.
- Pandemic highlighted moral paradox: 'we are all in this together' rang hollow amid unprecedented inequality and partisan rancour, requiring political-moral renewal beyond expertise.
- Caste discrimination in Silicon Valley exposes how Indian social hierarchies transcend geography, challenging meritocratic narratives in tech and governance.

## Open Question
How can political systems genuinely mobilize collective action and solidarity when institutions lack moral credibility and inequality undermines claims of shared sacrifice?

## India Angle
West Bengal elections reveal how political parties weaponize local grievances and identity politics in India's electoral landscape; Cisco caste discrimination case exposes how Indian social hierarchies persist in global tech hubs, challenging India's meritocratic self-image.
