## Core Argument
Happiness deferred to planned breaks leaves ordinary days hollow; contentment emerges from attention, not geography.

## Key Points
- Anticipated memories create phantom happiness by borrowing joy from future, leaving present underexperienced
- Speed compresses days so thoroughly that pleasant moments vanish before they register consciously
- Small buffers of unhurried time reshape how days feel without requiring travel or elaborate rituals

## Open Question
If vacations genuinely interrupt routine and compress time feels genuinely different there, can the same psychological shift be sustained amid ordinary work structures without external change?

## India Angle
Reflects broader Indian cultural tension between aspirational consumerism (vacation culture, bucket-list living) and traditional wisdom emphasizing present-moment awareness and contentment—increasingly relevant as Indian middle class pursues leisure-based happiness
