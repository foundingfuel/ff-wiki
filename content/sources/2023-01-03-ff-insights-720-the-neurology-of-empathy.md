## Core Argument
Empathy and fairness evolve with age; organizations must similarly mature beyond distrust-based control.

## Key Points
- Children develop empathy progressively from self-focused to abstract justice; adults often regress to cynicism.
- Moonlighting debate reveals outdated employer control models conflicting with modern autonomy expectations.
- India's data governance gap exposes need for proactive regulation before technology enables mass identification.

## Open Question
If empathy naturally declines with age and socialization, how can organizations foster cultures of trust and fairness when both leaders and workers are shaped by systems that punish vulnerability?

## India Angle
India's regulatory approach to data protection and employee rights is evolving; moonlighting policies and data monetization by state enterprises like IRCTC reflect tension between old-guard corporate control and modern flexibility norms.
