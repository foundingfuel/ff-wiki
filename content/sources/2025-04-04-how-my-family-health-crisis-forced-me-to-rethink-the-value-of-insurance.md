## Core Argument
Personal health crisis exposed systemic insurance gaps; proactive risk assessment prevents financial devastation for Indian families.

## Key Points
- Middle-class India risks poverty from single major hospitalization; 70% of hospital networks are private and costly.
- 82% of urban Indian households lack health insurance; 23% finance hospitalizations through debt.
- Assessing family health risks and adequate coverage requires deliberate planning, not waiting for crisis.
- Psychological blockers—denial, underestimation of risk—prevent Indians from securing necessary insurance cover early.

## Open Question
How can we shift Indian middle-class behavior from reactive crisis response to proactive health and financial planning when cultural and psychological biases work against insurance adoption?

## India Angle
India's 70% private healthcare system, 82% uninsured urban households, and catastrophic health costs make family insurance critical for middle-class financial stability and poverty prevention.
