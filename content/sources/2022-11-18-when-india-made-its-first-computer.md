## Core Argument
India's 1960s computer ambitions were hamstrung by leadership loss and missed manufacturing partnerships.

## Key Points
- CDC proposed a 50:50 core-memory manufacturing venture in India in 1965; proposal was shelved and never revived.
- TIFR's CDC 3600 became India's national computing facility, spurring research and training across 100+ institutions nationwide.
- Homi Bhabha's 1966 death removed a crucial technocrat advocate who could persuade government to make bold tech bets.

## Open Question
Why did institutional inertia and bureaucratic processes prevent India from seizing the core-memory opportunity when technical capability and foreign partnership were both available?

## India Angle
India missed a critical opportunity in the 1960s to establish itself as a global hardware manufacturing hub when CDC proposed a core-memory factory partnership. This shapes India's current position: dominant in software services but dependent on imports for hardware, a strategic vulnerability that persists today.
