## Core Argument
Cultural frameworks shape fairness intuitions neurologically, affecting how societies construct moral and legal systems.

## Key Points
- Collectivist cultures (Chinese harmony focus) and individualist cultures (Greek agency) process fairness and social obligation differently at neurological levels.
- Social organization influences cognitive processes—collective-minded people see holistic systems; individual-minded people focus on discrete objects and proportions.
- Fairness intuitions are innate but culturally mediated; they cascade from neural biology to behavior to institutional law.
- Hybrid work tensions reveal that senior management (individualist, control-oriented) misaligns with employee values (collective well-being, autonomy).

## Open Question
If fairness intuitions are neurologically hardwired yet culturally malleable, how do multicultural organizations (or India's hybrid-work transition) reconcile conflicting moral frameworks without imposing one culture's definition of fairness?

## India Angle
Indian organizations often operate with hierarchical, collective values similar to traditional Chinese frameworks; understanding how fairness intuitions vary across cultures is critical as Indian companies navigate hybrid work and institutional reform.
