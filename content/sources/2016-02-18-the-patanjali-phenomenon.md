## Core Argument
Patanjali's FMCG dominance stems from cultural authenticity, cost efficiency, and grassroots distribution—not just Ramdev's charisma.

## Key Points
- Patanjali achieved 20-25% cost advantage through direct retail outlets and low overheads, enabling 15-30% cheaper pricing.
- Consumer trust in Ayurveda and 'natural products' increasingly matters more than mass advertising spend among Indian buyers.
- Success emerges from three vectors: cultural identity revival, authenticity/value signal, and wellness/natural product trend.

## Open Question
Can Patanjali sustain its growth trajectory while expanding to 400+ SKUs across disparate categories, or will portfolio stretch dilute its core equity in natural/Ayurvedic positioning?

## India Angle
Patanjali's disruption of India's FMCG sector reveals how Indian consumers increasingly seek products rooted in cultural identity, Ayurveda, and authenticity—challenging the Western-centric playbook of multinational FMCG giants that have long dominated Indian markets.
