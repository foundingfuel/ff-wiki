## Core Argument
Excessive criminalization of economic law, not deregulation, now poses India's greatest threat to freedom.

## Key Points
- Criminal penalties in economic law exceed 1970s licence-permit raj despite supposed liberalization.
- Low state capacity + harsh criminal sanctions = wrongful raids, corruption, abuse of power.
- Civil/monetary penalties suffice for non-compliance; criminal jailing is disproportionate state coercion.

## Open Question
If monetary penalties are adequate to deter economic violations, why have democracies globally shifted toward criminalization despite knowing this risks abuse?

## India Angle
India faces a critical juncture: the state has shifted from licence-permit raj intrusion to criminalization of economic offences, expanding enforcement discretion at a time of low state capacity. This risks arbitrary persecution of businesses and individuals, particularly during regulatory transitions like energy markets.
