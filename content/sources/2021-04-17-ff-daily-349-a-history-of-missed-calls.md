## Core Argument
Indian entrepreneurs turn constraints into competitive advantages through ingenious consumer hacks like missed calls.

## Key Points
- Missed calls emerged as a consumer hack when Indian cellular costs made calling prohibitively expensive in the 2000s.
- ZipDial commercialized missed calls to create 'Google Analytics for the offline world,' helping brands reach 95% cash-based consumers.
- Cognitive biases in strategy rooms cause teams to overestimate their innovations while missing market realities and emerging threats.

## Open Question
Why do leaders and organizations systematically default to adding solutions rather than subtracting complexity, despite evidence that subtraction often works better?

## India Angle
ZipDial exemplifies how Indian entrepreneurs leverage local constraints (expensive cellular calls, cash-based economy, offline commerce) as innovation springboards—a distinctly Indian business model that wouldn't exist elsewhere, now a playbook for constraint-driven innovation in emerging markets.
