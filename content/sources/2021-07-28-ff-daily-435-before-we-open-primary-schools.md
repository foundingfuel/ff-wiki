## Core Argument
School reopening in India requires prioritizing nutrition, vaccination, and play before academic learning resumes.

## Key Points
- Midday meal scheme must provide cooked meals, not cash transfers, to address pandemic-induced nutritional deficits and stunted growth risks.
- Teachers need priority vaccination before reopening; playgrounds should be used for play-and-interaction over academics in first three months.
- Rural students face invisible learning losses because parents lack capacity to complete workbooks, widening inequality during reopening.

## Open Question
How can India balance the urgent need to restore schooling with the reality that many rural students have lost ground invisibly, and their families lack capacity to bridge learning gaps during reopening?

## India Angle
India's school reopening must address dual crises: learning loss in rural areas and nutritional deficits from missed midday meals. Policy must account for India's specific vulnerabilities—teacher vaccination gaps, stunted growth risks, and parental capacity disparities between urban and rural communities.
