## Core Argument
Social enterprises must innovate organizational models to achieve impact with fewer resources, not scale conventionally.

## Key Points
- Social enterprises in India lost CSR funding during COVID; millions depend on them for services.
- Conventional scaling traps social entrepreneurs into fundraising cycles, diverting energy from core mission.
- For-profit and non-profit enterprises have fundamentally incompatible purposes and values; applying business models to social work fails.

## Open Question
How can social enterprises achieve 'impact at scale' without adopting the organizational structures and funding models that inevitably compromise their values and mission?

## India Angle
India's 4 million social enterprises face existential threats post-COVID; youth demographic dividend at risk without sustainable models that don't depend on scarce CSR funding or conventional scaling approaches suited to for-profit enterprises.
