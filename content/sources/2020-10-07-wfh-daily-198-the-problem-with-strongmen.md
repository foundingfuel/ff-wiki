## Core Argument
Perceived strength in leaders often masks ineffectiveness; diversity hires face structural biases regardless of capability.

## Key Points
- Strong leadership perception doesn't correlate with effective governance or organizational outcomes.
- Glass cliff: minorities/women appointed to leadership during crises, then blamed for circumstances beyond control.
- Food price expertise now critical to RBI's monetary policy—expertise diversity matters for institutional effectiveness.

## Open Question
How can organizations genuinely embed diversity in leadership when structural biases—not just bias in hiring—undermine diverse leaders' success?

## India Angle
India's leadership culture often valorizes strongmen; understanding the limits of strong leadership and the glass cliff phenomenon is critical as Indian companies diversify leadership and RBI appoints women economists to key roles.
