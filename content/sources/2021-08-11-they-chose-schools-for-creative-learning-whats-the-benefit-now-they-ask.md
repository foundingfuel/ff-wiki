## Core Argument
Alternative schools' pandemic pivot reveals mismatch between progressive pedagogy promises and working parents' capacity to deliver them.

## Key Points
- Parents chose creative-learning schools expecting hands-off experience; pandemic forced them into teaching facilitator roles they're unprepared for.
- Working mothers juggle office Zoom calls, household duties, and enforced classroom participation—a burden that falls disproportionately on women.
- Schools charging full fees while delivering suboptimal online learning; the gap between pedagogical philosophy and pandemic reality is stark.

## Open Question
If alternative education models cannot function without significant parental involvement during crises, are they truly scalable or equitable for working families in India?

## India Angle
India's 250 million children faced 500+ days of school closure; inequality deepens as only 1-in-4 have digital access; working mothers in urban India squeezed between career demands and unexpected pedagogical responsibilities.
