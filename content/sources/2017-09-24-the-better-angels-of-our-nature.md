## Core Argument
Modern discourse replaces genuine questions with disguised opinions, preventing humble exploration of complex societal transitions.

## Key Points
- Current debates masquerade opinions as questions; 'experts' echo anchors rather than genuinely inquire or disagree constructively.
- True questions require humility to say 'I don't know' and mutual exploration; most discourse demands pre-formed answers backed by facts.
- Moral evolution (vegetarianism, animal welfare, synthetic alternatives) happens through gradual shifts in values, not ideological purity wars.

## Open Question
If synthetic meat is biochemically identical to animal tissue but lacks consciousness, how do we resolve the ethical question—and does our inability to answer it reflect ignorance or the impossibility of the question itself?

## India Angle
India's public discourse—from Aadhaar to dietary choices to development policy—is increasingly polarised into binary positions. This reflects a deeper crisis in how Indians engage with complexity, trade-offs, and uncertainty in nation-building conversations.
