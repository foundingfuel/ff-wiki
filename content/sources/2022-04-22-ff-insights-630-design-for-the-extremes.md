## Core Argument
Design for extremes, not averages—innovation emerges from outlier needs, not mainstream markets.

## Key Points
- Average users are rare; designing for them creates products for non-existent personas and stifles innovation.
- Extreme or 'weird' consumers drive innovation more than conventional users; market research focused on averages kills ideas.
- Social media's impact varies—harmful for vulnerable subsets while neutral for others; requires extreme-case thinking in product design.

## Open Question
How do organizations balance designing for profitable mainstream markets with the innovation upside of serving extreme users, especially when extreme segments may be smaller or less profitable?

## India Angle
Indian businesses and leaders should challenge the 'aam aadmi' focus and innovate for extreme users; this mirrors Gandhi's approach and is relevant for Indian market design, social media impact on urban Indian teens, and mentorship culture.
