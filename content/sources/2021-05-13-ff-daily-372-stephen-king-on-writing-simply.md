## Core Argument
Simplicity and directness in communication—and knowledge—serve truth better than complexity.

## Key Points
- Use the first word that comes to mind; hesitation produces inferior, indirect expression of meaning.
- Patents designed to incentivize innovation have been weaponized to block competition and protect corporate profits over lives.
- Life-saving knowledge should not be hoarded; during pandemics, IP must yield to collective human survival.

## Open Question
If innovation requires IP protection to incentivize investment, how do societies guarantee equitable access to life-saving knowledge without destroying the incentive structure itself?

## India Angle
India faces dual challenges: both as a vaccine manufacturer needing IP flexibility and as a developing nation dependent on accessible knowledge. Patent reform directly impacts Indian pharma's ability to produce life-saving drugs and India's public health policy during crises.
