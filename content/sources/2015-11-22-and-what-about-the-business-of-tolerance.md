## Core Argument
Businesses claim to value innovation but punish dissent; true tolerance requires safe spaces for challenging leadership positions.

## Key Points
- Employees lack safe channels to challenge unreasonable targets, unfair performance systems, or leader-backed failing strategies.
- Intolerance to minority concerns drove unionization 150 years ago; non-unionized modern firms replicate same power imbalance.
- Innovation rhetoric collapses when CEOs sponsor failing products—only new leadership breaks past models, not internal debate.

## Open Question
If innovation requires divergent thinking but organizations punish contrarian views, how can businesses achieve genuine strategic disruption without leadership turnover?

## India Angle
Indian businesses—from IT to banking to startups—lack psychological safety for dissent. Trade unions historically emerged because Indian companies didn't tolerate minority voices; modern firms without unions replicate this intolerance through performance management and hierarchy, undermining innovation claims.
