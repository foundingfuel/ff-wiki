## Core Argument
Ethics is imbibed through experience, relationships, and emotional connection, not classroom teaching alone.

## Key Points
- Ethics develops through osmotic learning via storytelling, mentorship, and early professional exposure during 'professional childhood'.
- Business schools face existential dilemma: they are businesses themselves, reluctant to teach ethics unless corporations value ethical graduates.
- Programs like Abhyudaya and CEO ethics interviews embed values through lived experience and emotional connection, not instruction.

## Open Question
If ethics is fundamentally imbibed through family and social norms rather than teaching, how can institutions systematically scale ethical development, especially for those without privileged backgrounds?

## India Angle
Indian business schools and family-rooted traditions offer unique models for embedding ethics through experience and relationships rather than classroom instruction; Abhyudaya and mentoring programs at SPJIMR exemplify Indian institutional approaches to ethical development.
