## Core Argument
Hybrids, not pure EVs, will dominate India's automotive transition due to infrastructure constraints and cost realities.

## Key Points
- Hybrids eliminate range anxiety, charging infrastructure dependency, and offer lower ownership costs than EVs while maintaining environmental benefits.
- Maruti Suzuki's partnership with Toyota leverages frugal engineering to develop low-cost hybrids targeting 35 kmpl fuel efficiency, disrupting the EV-only strategy.
- Government must recalibrate GST incentives from 5% (EVs) toward 12-15% (hybrids) to reflect market demand and infrastructure realities.

## Open Question
If hybrids become the dominant path, will India miss the long-term emissions reduction benefits of pure electrification, or is a 30-40 year hybrid phase actually optimal given infrastructure maturity timelines?

## India Angle
India's EV transition is stalling due to charging infrastructure gaps and cost barriers; hybrids offer a pragmatic intermediate solution aligned with Indian consumer preferences and frugal engineering strengths. Policy incentives must shift to reflect market reality, not ideological EV-first assumptions.
