## Core Argument
India's poor face permanent damage from lockdown; immediate support needed for livelihoods, nutrition, education access.

## Key Points
- Livelihoods decimated for gig workers, migrants, domestic staff; government transfers insufficient to sustain families.
- Child malnutrition causes permanent damage; community kitchens wound down, homeless scattered without food access.
- Digital divide deepens inequality: 67% of Mumbai municipal school students lack devices for online learning.

## Open Question
How can India simultaneously address immediate survival needs (food, livelihoods) while preventing long-term human capital loss (education, nutrition) when government capacity and donor resources are both stretched?

## India Angle
India's pandemic response exposed systemic gaps: inadequate government transfers (₹1,000-2,000), broken safety nets for gig workers, urban-rural migration crises, digital divide in education (only 33% of Mumbai municipal school students have device access), and public health system collapse affecting non-COVID vulnerable populations.
