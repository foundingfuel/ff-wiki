## Core Argument
Lost academic year narrative risks becoming self-fulfilling prophecy; adolescent brain plasticity suggests resilience over permanent damage.

## Key Points
- Early adolescence is a critical period of heightened brain plasticity and adaptability, not just vulnerability.
- Remote learning contradicts adolescents' needs for social contact, but interruption alone doesn't cause permanent developmental damage.
- Framing the pandemic year as 'loss' can create self-fulfilling prophecy; reframing enables focus on adaptability and resilience.

## Open Question
How do policymakers balance acknowledging real learning gaps and social deprivation with avoiding catastrophizing narratives that undermine student agency and resilience?

## India Angle
India's school closures during COVID-19 affected millions of students; understanding trauma narratives vs. neuroplasticity evidence is critical for policy on reopening, mental health support, and learning recovery strategies.
