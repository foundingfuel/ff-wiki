## Core Argument
Self-deception about personal capabilities is widespread and self-defeating, yet deeply rooted in human cognition.

## Key Points
- Humans systematically misremember negative information about health, skills, and risk to maintain positive self-image.
- Inaccurate mental maps of reality—despite seeming self-defeating—persist across domains from health to leadership.
- Awareness of self-deception is first step toward honest self-assessment and better decision-making.

## Open Question
If self-deception is so harmful to individual outcomes, why has evolution selected for it rather than eliminating it?

## India Angle
Indian professionals in high-performance roles often face cultural pressure to project competence; self-deception about capabilities undermines decision-making in startups and large organizations navigating rapid change.
