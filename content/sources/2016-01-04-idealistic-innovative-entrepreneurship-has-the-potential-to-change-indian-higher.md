## Core Argument
Entrepreneurial leadership can transform rural education by solving access, quality, and social barriers simultaneously through systems thinking.

## Key Points
- Rural India has massive untapped talent; local 'degree shops' reveal systemic failure of traditional higher ed models
- Founder Dubey solved education as ecosystem: buses, logistics, confidence-building, employment pathways, not just academics
- Social/cultural barriers (forced marriages, gender norms) are harder to overcome than academic ones; require persistent relationship-building

## Open Question
How sustainable is SSISM's model if it depends heavily on founder's personal relationships, family involvement, and goodwill from village elders rather than scalable systems or policy change?

## India Angle
Indian higher education in tier-2/tier-3 towns requires entrepreneurial reimagining to overcome structural barriers: limited college access, social/cultural resistance to girls' education, and low employment outcomes. SSISM exemplifies how founder-led innovation can address India's rural education gap.
