## Core Argument
India's data protection law clarifies platform ownership rights, but fails to resolve creator agency over built audiences and fan relationships.

## Key Points
- DPDP Act establishes platforms as Data Fiduciaries controlling user data; creators have no automatic ownership rights without explicit contractual terms.
- Indian creators lack bargaining power—small comedy community depends on platforms for reach, leaving them vulnerable to delisting and data loss.
- Legal ambiguity persists: law protects data collection practices but doesn't address whether creators can reclaim audience relationships built on platforms.

## Open Question
If platforms own all creator-audience data by default, how can India's fledgling creator economy build sustainable, independent businesses without being held hostage by platform policies?

## India Angle
India's new Digital Personal Data Protection Act (2023) and 2025 rules create the legal framework for data ownership disputes. The Kunal Kamra case exemplifies how Indian creators on platforms like BookMyShow face unclear contractual rights over audience data, a critical issue as India's creator economy grows but lacks maturity in audience-building infrastructure.
