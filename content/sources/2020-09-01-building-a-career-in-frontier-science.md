## Core Argument
Building careers in frontier science requires bridging academia-industry divides, cross-disciplinary thinking, and hands-on learning experiences.

## Key Points
- Quantum computing is transitioning from lab to industry; India has announced quantum budget and AI policy support.
- Traditional either-or choice between academia and industry is dissolving; researchers increasingly run startups simultaneously.
- Talent gap exists not in coding ability but in problem-structuring, lateral thinking, and cross-domain knowledge transfer.

## Open Question
India invests in frontier tech infrastructure and policy, yet struggles to develop talent capable of cross-disciplinary thinking and problem-structuring—how can education systems evolve faster than industry needs?

## India Angle
India's emerging quantum computing policy, AI initiatives, and talent development needs in frontier science; challenges in bridging academic research with industry application and addressing talent deficits in deep tech sectors.
