## Core Argument
Urban development fails when it prioritizes technical solutions over human behavior and community vitality.

## Key Points
- Jane Jacobs critiqued modernist urban planning that eliminated vibrant street life for engineered order and vistas.
- E Sreedharan's 'Metro Man' reputation masks how complex projects require political judgment, not just engineering excellence.
- Gerd Gigerenzer argues simple heuristics often outperform complex models in real-world decision-making contexts.

## Open Question
How do we balance the need for order and efficiency in cities with preserving the spontaneous human interactions that create vitality and community?

## India Angle
Indian urban development and public infrastructure projects (Delhi Metro, smart cities) often prioritize engineering efficiency over human-centered design; policymakers and leaders like Sreedharan exemplify this technocratic approach, relevant as India scales urbanization.
