## Core Argument
Annual performance reviews are ineffective, biased systems that damage innovation, teamwork, and ethics—major companies now abandoning them.

## Key Points
- Annual reviews originated in 1950s US military culture; persist despite 60+ years of evidence they don't work.
- Rank-and-yank systems breed sabotage, fear, and undermine collaboration—Microsoft's stack ranking crippled innovation.
- Systemic bias pervades reviews; 62% of grade variance stems from rater perception, not actual performance.

## Open Question
If annual reviews are so flawed, why have organizations clung to them for decades, and what replaces them effectively without creating new problems?

## India Angle
Indian firms, especially those in IT and consulting, have adopted Western HR practices wholesale; moving away from annual reviews requires cultural shift from hierarchical, control-based management to trust-based systems—a significant challenge given India's organizational structures.
