## Core Argument
Facilitation—making progress easier through dialogue—is the forgotten leadership skill necessary for meaning-making in information-rich, hierarchy-questioning organisations.

## Key Points
- Modern meetings generate motion without shared understanding; facilitation creates clarity by building group ownership of outcomes.
- Presence and curiosity matter more than expertise; leaders who trust groups to find their own way unlock collective wisdom.
- Structured dialogue converts diverging views into understanding, preventing conversations from hardening into acrimony or being abandoned offline.

## Open Question
If facilitation requires genuine neutrality and trust in group wisdom, how do leaders reconcile this with accountability for outcomes and the pressure to drive decisive action in time-constrained environments?

## India Angle
Indian organisations—from startups to large corporates—often default to hierarchical decision-making and instruction-based leadership. As information democratises and younger workforces demand participation, facilitation becomes critical for navigating India's shift from command-and-control to collaborative leadership, especially in high-stakes meetings where buy-in determines execution.
