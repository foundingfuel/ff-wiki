## Core Argument
Electoral narratives are constructed post-hoc; fertilizer subsidies reveal policy trapped by voter incentives over long-term sustainability.

## Key Points
- Election strategies appear brilliant in retrospect due to hindsight bias; outcomes remain fundamentally unpredictable despite campaign sophistication.
- India imports 55% DAP, 100% MoP; simultaneous export cuts from China, Russia create supply crisis amid Russia-Ukraine conflict and energy crises.
- Agricultural subsidies persist despite documented soil degradation because politicians prioritize short-term voter appeal over systemic reform.

## Open Question
How can governments reform agricultural subsidy systems when electoral incentives reward short-term voter appeasement over long-term sustainability and resilience-building?

## India Angle
India's fertilizer crisis exposes systemic vulnerabilities: heavy import dependence (55% DAP, 100% MoP), geopolitical exposure to China/Russia disruptions, and political inability to reform despite documented soil damage from subsidies.
