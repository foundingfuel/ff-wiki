# Founding Fuel Wiki

*A decade of Founding Fuel's reporting, synthesised into a knowledge base for Indian practitioners.*

---

## How to use this wiki

Use it as a map to the archive, not a substitute for it.

**By theme** — Each of the ~15 theme pages has three parts. It opens with a scene-setting paragraph naming what is broken or misunderstood about that topic in India. Then come the principles — the sharpest insights the evidence supports, each with an explanation, an India-specific reality, a named FF article as proof, and one concrete implication for Monday morning. Finally, there is FF's View — a long essay covering the core conviction, what India changes about the theme, where global best practices fail, how FF diverges from mainstream media, the evolution of thinking across a decade, the key voices, what FF is skeptical of, and what remains unresolved. A theme page is not a summary. It is an argument.

**By author** — Each of the 33 author profiles covers: who the person is and what they bring that others don't, their dominant thesis, their India lens, the themes they engage most deeply, their mental models, how their thinking has evolved, their distinctive voice, what they are skeptical of, and their best work. Reading an author profile is the fastest way to understand a contributor's full intellectual contribution across years of writing.

**As a query layer** *(coming soon)* — The wiki's deeper value will come when you can ask questions of it and get a perspective back, grounded in the corpus. For now, read the theme pages as the closest proxy — each one is written as an answer to: what does FF believe about this, and why?

## A note from Anmol

Hello fellow reader,

I started this as an experiment — a fun project that grew into something I think is genuinely useful.

**What made me build it?**

**First**, because I had no real way to read Founding Fuel as a whole. I would have had to read something like 2,000 articles. That is possible in theory, but not in practice. What this wiki does is compress that problem. It lets you read 30–40 pages — around 20 theme pages and 33 author profiles — and you begin to understand what the whole body of work covers, how its thinking has evolved over 10–11 years, and where the deeper patterns lie.

Beyond saving time, it serves as institutional memory. It helps readers synthesise the archive more deeply than any individual article allows. And in a small way, it celebrates the work put in by so many contributors and the company — so carefully built over time.

**Second**, I wanted answers to questions I keep pondering. If Founding Fuel were to distil something equivalent to Sun Tzu's *Art of War*, what would it look like? What would FF say about technology and disruption that mainstream media won't? What beliefs do most of us hold that FF quietly dismantles? What does it believe about AI, business building, leadership, or India — as a body of work, not just as individual articles? What has it covered deeply? What has it missed? Where has the needle moved?

Answering these questions required moving from article-level reading to library-level synthesis. Earlier, AI could help me with one article at a time. This was an attempt to do something at a different scale — to treat the archive not as a collection of pieces, but as a single body of thought.

**Third**, I was looking for connections at a conceptual level — not just a lexical one. I was not satisfied with NotebookLM, vector databases, or RAG architectures, because they operate at the word and token level. That architecture relies on word similarity — and so it misses deeper connections. The wiki architecture is better placed to surface those. It can spot what a music maestro and an entrepreneur have in common in how they work on their craft. That is the kind of connection I wanted to see.

**Fourth**, I wanted a system I could ask questions of — one that gets better with each question. My punt, which will be tested soon, is that an LLM that reads Founding Fuel's synthesised concepts rather than a raw vectored database will produce better answers. A wiki is a strong foundation for a powerful query system. *(The query feature is being built.)*

**Fifth**, I wanted to explore use cases that could genuinely improve Founding Fuel itself. Could I design a workflow that pits Stratechery's worldview against FF's — and in doing so, reveal our blind spots? Could it help Ram and Charles with the newsletter? The system has now read 800+ newsletters. It knows the voice, the type of stories we gravitate toward, how we connect ideas to everyday life and to something topical. Could it help with research? Suggest better reading material? Point out what we keep missing? Building the wiki is a step closer to all of this.

**Finally**, I just felt like having fun. Playing around. Seeing what emerges.

*— Anmol Shrivastava*
